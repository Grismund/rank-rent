## Understanding Full Bathroom Remodel

A full remodel strips the existing bathroom to the studs. The old tub, shower, toilet, vanity, tile, flooring, and light fixtures are all removed. Plumbing and electrical are updated or replaced as needed. The room is then rebuilt with a waterproofing membrane, backer board, new tile, new fixtures, fresh electrical, and paint.

The typical scope includes: demolition, plumbing rough-in, electrical rough-in, waterproofing, backer board, tile (floor and walls), new tub or shower, vanity, toilet, faucets, lighting, ventilation fan, and trim. In New Braunfels, any project that relocates plumbing or modifies electrical circuits requires a permit from the City of New Braunfels Building Safety department under the adopted 2021 IPC.

## What to Expect During Full Bathroom Remodel

### Planning and Design

The contractor visits the bathroom, assesses the existing plumbing and electrical, checks for water damage behind walls and under flooring, and discusses your goals. Decisions cover layout, tile material and pattern, fixture brands, vanity style, lighting, and whether to keep the tub or convert to a walk-in shower. The contractor then produces a scope of work, timeline, and price.

If the project requires a permit — and most full remodels do — the contractor submits the application to the City of New Braunfels before demolition begins. Plumbing, electrical, mechanical, and fuel gas permits are separate schedules in New Braunfels and are not bundled into a single building permit.

### Demolition and Rough-In

Demo takes one to two days for a standard bathroom. The crew strips all fixtures, tile, drywall, and flooring down to the framing. They inspect for water damage, mold, and subfloor integrity. At 20 GPG hardness, slow leaks behind tile accumulate mineral deposits that mask damage until walls are opened — finding moisture issues during demo is routine here.

Plumbing rough-in follows: new supply lines (PEX is standard in this area), drain relocations, and valve placements. Electrical rough-in updates wiring, adds GFCI outlets, and installs the ventilation fan. A rough inspection is required before closing the walls.

### Finishing and Inspection

The waterproofing membrane goes on the shower pan area and walls. Backer board, then tile. Fixtures, vanity, toilet, mirrors, and trim follow. The final inspection covers plumbing connections, electrical, ventilation, and waterproofing.

A full remodel typically takes three to six weeks from demo to completion, depending on scope, material lead times, and inspection scheduling with the city.

## Full Bathroom Remodel Materials and Styles

The finish level determines both the appearance and the final cost:

- **Tile:** Ceramic runs $1 to $5 per square foot for material. Porcelain is $3 to $10. Natural stone starts at $5 and goes higher. In a hard-water area like New Braunfels, low-porosity porcelain is the practical choice — it resists mineral absorption and cleans more easily than porous stone.
- **Vanity:** Stock vanities start around $200. Semi-custom runs $500 to $1,500. Double vanities are the standard request for master baths in the newer subdivisions.
- **Shower:** Fiberglass inserts are the least expensive. Tiled walk-in showers with glass enclosures are the most popular upgrade. Large-format porcelain tile reduces grout lines, which matters in hard water.
- **Tub:** Standard alcove tubs start around $200. Freestanding soaking tubs range from $500 to $3,000+. Walk-in tubs for accessibility run $2,000 to $5,000 for the fixture.
- **Fixtures:** Budget sets start around $100 total. Mid-range runs $300 to $800. Specify fixtures with ceramic disc cartridges and easy-clean aerators that tolerate mineral deposits.

## Full Bathroom Remodel Pricing in New Braunfels

A full bathroom remodel in the New Braunfels area typically falls between **$10,000 and $15,000** for a standard master bathroom. The final number depends on bathroom size, the extent of plumbing and electrical changes, tile and fixture selections, and whether the layout changes.

Factors that push costs higher: relocating the toilet or shower drain on a slab foundation, water damage repair behind walls, upgrading to a freestanding tub or frameless glass shower enclosure, and premium tile. New Braunfels sits on the Balcones Fault where Hill Country limestone meets expansive clay, so slab conditions vary by neighborhood — drain relocation costs depend on what the contractor encounters.
