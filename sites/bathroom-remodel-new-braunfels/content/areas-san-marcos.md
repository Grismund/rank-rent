## Local Bathroom Remodel in San Marcos

San Marcos is the Hays County seat, 15 miles northeast of New Braunfels along the I-35 corridor. The city's population has reached an estimated 91,082 in 2026, driven by Texas State University's 47,513 enrolled students and the rapid growth of master-planned communities like Trace, La Cima, Kissing Tree, and Blanco Vista.

That growth creates two distinct bathroom remodel markets. The 69.2 percent of San Marcos housing that is renter-occupied generates demand for landlord-driven quick-turn remodels: new vanity, fixtures, tile, and paint to attract tenants or meet habitability standards. The 30.8 percent that is owner-occupied — concentrated in master-planned communities — drives higher-scope projects including master bath renovations and accessibility upgrades.

San Marcos water comes from a blend of groundwater sources including the Edwards Aquifer. Water hardness measures approximately 269 PPM (15.7 grains per gallon) — very hard. That mineral content leaves heavy scale on shower fixtures, faucet aerators, glass doors, and tile grout. Total dissolved solids measure 377 PPM. Any bathroom remodel in San Marcos should account for fixtures and finishes that withstand this water from day one.

## Why San Marcos Homeowners Are Remodeling Bathrooms

### Home Ages and Common Bathroom Issues

San Marcos has a relatively young housing stock in the newer subdivisions, but the established neighborhoods tell a different story:

- **Downtown neighborhoods (Dunbar, Blanco Gardens, Westover, Southwest Hills):** Homes from the 1950s through 1970s with original small bathrooms, single-sink vanities, and outdated plumbing. These are candidates for full remodels that modernize layout and replace galvanized or cast-iron piping.
- **Kissing Tree (55+ community):** Concentrated demand for accessibility-focused bathroom remodels — curbless showers, grab bars, comfort-height toilets, and non-slip flooring for aging-in-place residents.
- **Trace, La Cima, Cottonwood Creek, River Bridge Ranch:** Newer construction with modern PEX plumbing. Remodels here tend to be aesthetic upgrades — builder-grade finishes replaced with tile, glass, and upgraded fixtures.

### Local Housing Market Trends

Approximately 1,424 construction projects are active in San Marcos, including 589 new builds and 835 renovations or remodels. Median home value sits at approximately $310,000 to $320,000. That combination of active renovation culture and moderate home values makes bathroom remodeling a practical investment, particularly for the owner-occupied segment that competes for resale appeal.

The large student rental market also drives a specific remodel pattern: property owners updating bathrooms to reduce maintenance calls and tenant turnover. Quick-turn remodels with durable, low-maintenance materials are the standard scope for rental properties.

## San Marcos Bathroom Remodel Permits

San Marcos follows the 2021 International Residential Code with local amendments adopted via Ordinance 2023-09. A building permit is required for bathroom remodels that involve plumbing relocation, new or relocated electrical outlets, or structural modifications. Cosmetic updates — replacing tile, swapping fixtures in the same location, painting — do not require a permit.

Permit fees in San Marcos are $0.42 per square foot for interior remodels plus a flat $15 technology fee. A standalone residential plumbing permit runs $63 plus the $15 tech fee. Permits are obtained through Planning and Development Services at 630 E. Hopkins Street or online via the MGO Connect portal.

Homeowners who own and occupy their homestead may pull their own permit and perform the work themselves. For rental properties — a large share of San Marcos housing — a registered plumber is required if the work is hired out.

Plan for two to four weeks for permit approval on most residential projects.

## What San Marcos Homeowners Are Choosing

The most requested bathroom upgrades in San Marcos reflect the local market:

- **Walk-in showers** replacing rarely-used tubs, especially in Kissing Tree and other communities with older residents
- **Large-format porcelain tile** on shower walls and floors to minimize grout lines that collect mineral scale in the 269 PPM water
- **Water softener integration** during remodels — plumbing for a softener loop is best added while walls are open, particularly when installing tankless water heaters that are sensitive to hard water scaling
- **Durable, easy-clean fixtures** in rental properties where maintenance visits are costly
