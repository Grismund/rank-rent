## Bathroom Remodel Questions Answered

### How long does a bathroom remodel take?

A cosmetic refresh — new vanity, toilet, fixtures, and paint with no layout changes — takes about one week. A standard remodel with new tile, an updated shower, and upgraded fixtures runs two to three weeks. A full gut-and-rebuild with plumbing and electrical changes takes three to six weeks, including permit inspections. Material lead times, especially for custom glass enclosures or specialty tile, can extend the timeline. The [timeline guide](/guides/how-long-does-a-bathroom-remodel-take/) breaks down each scope in detail.

### Do I need a permit for a bathroom remodel in New Braunfels?

In New Braunfels, any project that relocates plumbing, adds new drain lines, or modifies electrical circuits requires a permit from the City of New Braunfels Building Safety department. The city follows the 2021 International Plumbing Code. Note that plumbing, electrical, mechanical, and fuel gas permits are separate schedules — they are not bundled into a single building permit. Cosmetic work — replacing a vanity, swapping a toilet in the same location, retiling, or painting — typically does not require a permit. The contractor confirms with the building department before work begins.

### What about permits in Canyon Lake or unincorporated Comal County?

Canyon Lake is unincorporated Comal County. The county does not require building permits for interior residential work. However, Texas state law still requires that all plumbing work be performed by a TSBPE-licensed plumber and that electrical work meets National Electrical Code standards. If the property is on a septic system, adding a bathroom fixture may require a septic evaluation through the Comal County OSSF program.

### Can I remodel a bathroom on a budget?

Yes. A surface refresh in the $3,000 to $6,000 range covers a new vanity, faucet, toilet, light fixtures, mirror, floor tile, and paint — the items that define the room's appearance. The key to staying on budget is keeping the layout intact: when supply and drain lines stay put, labor costs drop and permits are usually not required. Stock materials from home improvement stores keep fixture costs down. The [small bathroom remodel](/small-bathroom-remodel/) page covers this scope in detail.

### Does hard water affect my bathroom remodel choices?

Significantly. New Braunfels water tests at 20 grains per gallon — among the hardest municipal supplies in Texas. That mineral content deposits scale on faucets, showerheads, glass enclosures, and tile grout. Contractors in this area typically recommend low-porosity porcelain tile, fixtures with ceramic disc cartridges and easy-clean aerators, and coated glass that resists water spots. Installing a water softener or softener loop during the remodel — while walls are open — is a common add-on that protects the investment long term.

### Should I replace or refinish my bathtub?

Refinishing coats the existing tub with a new surface layer. It costs $300 to $600, takes a day, and lasts 5 to 10 years under normal use. Replacement costs $1,500 to $5,000 and gives you a completely new fixture with a full lifespan. Refinishing makes sense when the tub is structurally sound and you need a cosmetic fix on a tight budget. Replace when the tub is cracked, badly chipped, or when you want to change the type. In New Braunfels' hard water, mineral deposits degrade refinished surfaces faster, which shortens refinishing lifespan compared to soft-water areas.

### What is the most expensive part of a bathroom remodel?

Labor, consistently. It accounts for 40 to 50 percent of a typical bathroom remodel budget, covering tile setting, plumbing, electrical, and carpentry. After labor, tile and countertops are the next largest category at 15 to 25 percent. The single most expensive decision is usually the shower: a custom-tiled walk-in with frameless glass costs significantly more than a prefabricated insert. The [cost guide](/cost/) breaks down where the budget goes by component.

### Can a bathroom be remodeled in stages?

Yes, and it is a practical approach for homeowners on a fixed budget. A common sequence: Stage 1, replace the vanity, toilet, and faucet. Stage 2, retile the floor. Stage 3, remodel the shower. Each stage can be done independently, months or years apart, as long as you are not moving plumbing between stages. The key limitation is that any work requiring plumbing relocation needs to be done as one project because you do not want to open and close walls multiple times.
