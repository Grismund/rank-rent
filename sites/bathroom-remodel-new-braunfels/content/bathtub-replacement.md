## Understanding Bathtub Replacement

Bathtub replacement removes the existing tub and installs a new one. The scope depends on whether the new tub fits the same footprint or requires plumbing and framing modifications. A same-size, same-location swap is the simplest version: the old tub comes out, the drain and supply connections are adapted, and the new tub goes in. Switching to a different tub type — freestanding where an alcove used to be, or a walk-in model — involves more plumbing work, potential framing changes, and a permit.

The surrounding tile, drywall, and flooring may need partial replacement depending on their condition after the old tub is removed.

## What to Expect During Bathtub Replacement

### Planning and Design

The contractor inspects the existing tub, checks the drain and supply plumbing, and measures the space. If you are keeping the same tub type and location, the project is straightforward. If you are changing the tub type — from alcove to freestanding, for example — the contractor evaluates whether the floor can support the new weight (a filled cast-iron freestanding tub can weigh over 500 pounds) and whether drain and supply lines need to move.

### Demolition and Rough-In

Removing the old tub typically takes a few hours. The crew disconnects plumbing, removes surrounding trim or tile, and lifts the tub out. With the tub removed, the contractor inspects the subfloor and wall framing for water damage — a common finding in homes with hard water and years of caulk deterioration.

If plumbing changes are needed, rough-in work follows. A same-location replacement often reuses the existing drain position. A change in tub type may require a new drain location, which means cutting through the slab on slab-on-grade foundations.

### Finishing and Inspection

The new tub is set, leveled, and connected. Surrounding tile, drywall, and trim are repaired or replaced. Caulk joints are sealed. If plumbing was relocated, the City of New Braunfels requires an inspection. A straightforward same-location swap that does not alter plumbing typically does not require a permit.

Most bathtub replacements take two to five days, depending on the scope of surrounding repair work.

## Bathtub Replacement Materials and Styles

- **Standard alcove tub:** Fits a three-wall enclosure. Acrylic and fiberglass models start around $200. Cast-iron enameled tubs run $400 to $1,000 and last significantly longer.
- **Freestanding tub:** A standalone fixture that requires open floor space and exposed plumbing. Acrylic freestanding models start around $500. Cast-iron clawfoot or slipper tubs range from $800 to $3,000+.
- **Walk-in tub:** Designed for accessibility with a door, built-in seat, and low entry threshold. Models run $2,000 to $5,000 for the fixture alone. These are increasingly popular in the Canyon Lake and Gruene areas where many residents are aging in place.
- **Soaking tub:** Deeper than standard, designed for full immersion. Japanese-style soaking tubs and deep freestanding models are common upgrades in master bath remodels.

For all tub types in this area, specify fixtures with finishes that resist hard water spotting. Brushed nickel and matte black hold up better against mineral deposits than polished chrome.
