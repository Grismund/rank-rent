## Understanding Tub-to-Shower Conversion

A tub-to-shower conversion removes the bathtub entirely and installs a walk-in shower in the same footprint. The project involves several distinct trades: plumbing modification, waterproofing, tile or panel installation, and glass enclosure fitting. Because the drain relocates from the tub position to the shower position, the contractor cuts into the slab on slab-on-grade foundations and reroutes the pipe.

The conversion is not just an aesthetic change. It transforms the daily functionality of the bathroom. A standard 60-inch alcove tub becomes a 60-inch walk-in shower with more usable space, better accessibility, and a modern appearance.

## What to Expect During Tub-to-Shower Conversion

### Planning and Design

The contractor inspects the tub alcove, checks the existing drain and supply positions, and measures for the new shower layout. Key decisions include the threshold type (curbed or curbless), tile selection, glass enclosure style, showerhead configuration, and whether to add a built-in bench or niche.

If the home has only one bathroom — common in some older New Braunfels neighborhoods and Canyon Lake cabins — the contractor plans the work sequence to minimize time without a functioning shower.

### Demolition and Rough-In

The tub comes out, along with the surrounding tile, backer board, and drywall as needed. The contractor inspects the framing and subfloor for moisture damage. At 20 GPG water hardness, mineral-crusted leaks behind old tub surrounds are a frequent discovery.

Plumbing rough-in moves the drain from the tub position to the shower position and adjusts supply lines and valve placement. This work requires a plumbing permit in New Braunfels. A rough inspection must pass before the walls are closed.

### Finishing and Inspection

Waterproofing membrane goes on the shower pan and walls. Backer board, then tile installation. The glass enclosure, showerhead, valve trim, and accessories are installed last. A shower pan flood test verifies waterproofing integrity. The final inspection covers all plumbing connections.

Most tub-to-shower conversions take one to three weeks, depending on tile complexity and whether custom glass is ordered.

## Tub-to-Shower Conversion Materials and Styles

- **Threshold:** A standard curb is the simplest option. A curbless (zero-threshold) design provides full accessibility but requires precise floor slope engineering — the entire bathroom floor may need to be re-sloped toward the drain. Curbless adds labor cost but eliminates the step-over barrier entirely.
- **Tile:** Porcelain tile with minimal grout lines is the practical choice in this hard-water area. Large-format wall tiles and mosaic floor tiles for slip resistance are a common combination.
- **Glass enclosure:** Frameless glass panels or doors are the most popular finish. Semi-frameless and framed options reduce cost. Curbless designs often use a single stationary glass panel instead of a door.
- **Showerhead:** Fixed, handheld, rain head, or a combination. A handheld on a slide bar adds flexibility and is an accessibility asset.
- **Bench:** A built-in tile bench adds a seating option for accessibility and daily convenience.
