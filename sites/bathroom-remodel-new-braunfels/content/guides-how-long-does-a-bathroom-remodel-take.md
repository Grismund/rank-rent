**How long does a bathroom remodel take?** One to six weeks under normal conditions, depending on scope. A cosmetic refresh wraps up in about a week. A standard remodel runs two to three weeks. A full gut-and-rebuild with plumbing and electrical changes takes three to six weeks. The wide range exists because "bathroom remodel" covers everything from a vanity swap to a complete room reconstruction.

This guide breaks down the timeline by project scope, explains what extends or shortens it, and covers how New Braunfels-specific factors like permit scheduling, hard water damage, and Hill Country soil conditions affect the schedule.

*Last updated: November 2026. Based on typical New Braunfels-area contractor timelines. Your specific project may vary.*

## Quick Summary

The timeline for a bathroom remodel is driven by three things: how much demolition is involved, whether plumbing or electrical changes require permits and inspections, and how long the finishing work (tile, fixtures, glass) takes to install.

Here is the quick breakdown:

| Scope | Typical timeline | Permit needed? |
|-------|-----------------|----------------|
| Surface refresh | 1 week | Usually no |
| Standard remodel | 2--3 weeks | Often yes |
| Full gut-and-rebuild | 3--6 weeks | Yes |
| Tub-to-shower conversion | 1--3 weeks | Yes |
| Bathtub replacement (same location) | 2--4 days | Usually no |
| Shower retile | 3--5 days | Usually no |

These timelines cover active construction. They do not include the design and planning phase before work begins, which can take one to four weeks depending on how quickly decisions are made and materials are ordered.

## Key Differences

**Surface refresh (1 week).** New vanity, toilet, faucet, mirror, light fixtures, and paint. No demolition beyond removing old fixtures. No plumbing relocation. No tile work on walls (floor tile may be included). This is the fastest bathroom project because there is no waiting for inspections, no waterproofing cure time, and no tile setting on vertical surfaces. A skilled crew can complete it in three to five working days.

**Standard remodel (2 to 3 weeks).** New tile on the floor and in the shower, new vanity with countertop, updated fixtures, possibly a new shower valve. This scope involves demolition (removing old tile and backer board), waterproofing, tile setting, and grouting — each requiring drying or curing time before the next step.

The timeline breaks down roughly like this:
- Demo and inspection for water damage: 1--2 days
- Plumbing and electrical rough-in: 1--2 days
- Rough inspection (if permit required): scheduling adds 1--2 days
- Waterproofing membrane application and cure: 1 day
- Shower pan flood test: 1 day
- Backer board and tile setting: 3--5 days
- Grout cure: 1 day
- Fixture installation, glass, trim: 2--3 days
- Final inspection: 1 day

**Full gut-and-rebuild (3 to 6 weeks).** Everything comes out to the studs. The room is rebuilt with new framing repairs (if water damage is found), new plumbing rough-in, new electrical, waterproofing, tile, fixtures, and paint. The extended timeline comes from two factors: more demolition and repair work at the front end, and mandatory inspections at multiple stages.

A full remodel in New Braunfels requires at minimum: a rough plumbing inspection, a rough electrical inspection, a shower pan flood test, and a final inspection. The City of New Braunfels Building Safety department schedules these, and the contractor cannot proceed until each one passes. Note that plumbing, electrical, mechanical, and fuel gas permits are separate schedules — each may require its own inspection timeline.

**Tub-to-shower conversion (1 to 3 weeks).** The plumbing modification (rerouting the drain from tub to shower position) requires a permit and inspections in New Braunfels, which adds scheduling time. The tile work and glass installation take longer than a simple fixture swap. A basic conversion with stock tile and a standard glass door finishes in about one week of active work plus inspection days. A custom-tiled conversion with frameless glass and a bench takes two to three weeks.

## How This Affects Your Decision

**What extends the timeline:**

- **Material lead times.** Custom glass enclosures can take two to four weeks from order to delivery. Specialty tile may need to be ordered. The contractor can begin demolition and rough-in while waiting for materials, but finishing stalls if the glass or tile has not arrived.
- **Permit inspection scheduling.** In New Braunfels, inspection wait times depend on the building department's volume. During busy construction periods — and the I-35 corridor is growing fast — scheduling delays can add days at each inspection point.
- **Hidden water damage.** Finding rot behind the shower walls or under the subfloor adds demolition and repair time. At 20 grains per gallon water hardness, mineral-crusted slow leaks behind tile often mask damage that is worse than expected. A contractor cannot give a firm timeline until demo reveals the actual condition behind the walls.
- **Slab complications.** New Braunfels sits on the Balcones Fault where Hill Country limestone meets expansive clay. Some slabs have experienced differential movement that affects drain routing. If the slab is cracked or the drain path encounters rock, the plumbing work takes longer than on a uniform slab.

**What shortens the timeline:**

- **Keeping the existing layout.** No plumbing relocation means no permit, no rough inspection, and significantly less labor. This is the single biggest time saver.
- **Choosing stock materials.** Stock tile, stock vanities, and standard glass enclosures are available immediately. No waiting for custom orders.
- **Canyon Lake projects.** Because unincorporated Comal County does not require local building permits for interior work, Canyon Lake remodels skip the municipal inspection scheduling steps. A licensed plumber is still required by Texas state law, but the lack of local inspection scheduling can shorten the overall timeline by several days.

**How to plan around it:** Most contractors recommend scheduling bathroom remodels during drier months (October through March in this area) when humidity is lower and drying times for waterproofing, thin-set, and grout are shorter. If you have only one bathroom, the contractor can typically keep the toilet functional throughout the project, but the shower will be out of service during the tile and waterproofing phase — usually four to seven days for a standard remodel.
