## Local Bathroom Remodel in Canyon Lake

Canyon Lake is an unincorporated census-designated place in Comal County, centered on the 8,230-acre reservoir created when the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers completed Canyon Dam in 1964. The community sits at approximately 965 feet elevation on the Edwards Plateau in the Texas Hill Country, about 15 miles northwest of New Braunfels.

The CDP has grown from 21,262 residents in 2010 to an estimated 33,883 today — a 46.4 percent increase over the decade. Canyon Lake is not a single municipality but a cluster of unincorporated communities including Sattler, Startzville, Canyon City, Cranes Mill, and Hancock, spread across more than 80 subdivisions. The 85 percent homeownership rate and median household income of $97,301 indicate a population with both equity and income to fund bathroom remodeling projects.

Canyon Lake Water Service Company provides water from the reservoir and the Trinity Aquifer. Water hardness averages 157 PPM (approximately 9.2 grains per gallon) — classified as hard. The limestone geology of the Edwards and Trinity aquifers dissolves calcium and magnesium that cause mineral scale on shower heads, faucets, glass doors, and tile.

## Why Canyon Lake Homeowners Are Remodeling Bathrooms

### Home Ages and Common Bathroom Issues

Canyon Lake's housing stock reflects the community's evolution from lake-recreation destination to year-round residential area:

- **Original lakeside cabins (1964–1980s, approximately 7.3 percent of stock):** Built after the dam's completion, these homes commonly have galvanized steel supply pipes now 40 to 60 years old and cast-iron drain pipes that corrode, flake, and restrict flow. A bathroom remodel in these properties typically requires full repiping.
- **1970s–1980s homes (approximately 15 percent of stock):** Small, compartmentalized bathrooms with standard 5-foot alcove tubs, single-sink vanities, and no exhaust ventilation fans. These are prime candidates for modernization with open layouts and walk-in showers.
- **Post-2000 construction (51 percent of stock, including Mystic Shores and Vintage Oaks):** Modern PEX plumbing and PVC drains in good condition. Remodels here are primarily cosmetic upgrades from builder-grade finishes.

The 19.9 percent housing vacancy rate reflects a large stock of vacation homes and weekend lake houses — properties where durable, low-maintenance bathroom finishes matter because the home sits empty for extended periods.

### Local Housing Market Trends

Canyon Lake's median age of 48.2 years — 35 percent higher than the Texas median — means many homeowners are aging in place. Demand for accessibility features is higher here than in most suburbs: curbless showers, grab bars, comfort-height toilets, and non-slip flooring are common remodel requests.

The lake-adjacent microclimate adds moisture pressure. The 8,230-acre reservoir increases ambient humidity for properties on lower coves, putting additional stress on bathroom surfaces, caulk, and grout compared to surrounding Hill Country communities or homes on the bluffs.

## Canyon Lake Bathroom Remodel Permits

Canyon Lake's permit situation is unique in the New Braunfels service area: there is no municipal building department. Comal County does not require traditional building permits for interior residential work in unincorporated areas. Only septic (OSSF), floodplain, driveway/right-of-way, and Fire Marshal permits apply at the county level.

However, Texas state law still governs the trades. All plumbing work beyond ordinary minor repairs to accessible fixtures must be performed by a plumber licensed through the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners. Electrical work that adds or relocates circuits must comply with the National Electrical Code as enforced by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation.

One critical consideration: Canyon Lake properties on septic systems must ensure bathroom remodels that add fixtures do not exceed the system's designed capacity. Adding a bathroom or toilet may require a septic system evaluation or upgrade through the Comal County OSSF program.

Property owners in deed-restricted subdivisions like Mystic Shores, Oak Shores, and Vintage Oaks should check POA or HOA covenants — some require Architectural Control Committee approval before structural modifications.
