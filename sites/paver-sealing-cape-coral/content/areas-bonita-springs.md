## What Drives Paver Sealing Demand in Bonita Springs

### Climate and Weather Impact

Bonita Springs has a subtropical climate with an average annual temperature of 75 degrees and 55 inches of rainfall, the majority falling between June and September. The distinct dry season from November through April provides the ideal sealing window with just 1 to 2 inches of monthly rainfall.

What sets Bonita Springs apart from inland communities is salt exposure. Many properties sit within a few miles of the Gulf of Mexico and Estero Bay. Oceanfront and canal-side properties in Lee County see paver deterioration 40 to 60 percent faster than inland homes, and Bonita Springs properties are squarely in that category. The combination of UV, salt air, tropical rain, and year-round humidity creates conditions that degrade unsealed pavers faster than virtually any other region in the continental United States.

Waterfront Bonita Springs properties need paver sealing every 2 to 3 years with breathable, salt-resistant sealers. Properties further inland may stretch to 3 years between applications.

### Common Paver Issues in Bonita Springs

Lee County soil is 87.4 percent sand over marine limestone, and Bonita Springs properties near the Imperial River and Estero Bay have particularly high water tables that push moisture upward through the base and into paver joints. That moisture carries dissolved minerals to the surface, producing efflorescence — white powdery deposits that mar the appearance and indicate active moisture cycling.

Sandy soils compress unevenly under load. Proper paver base preparation in Lee County requires 4 to 6 inches of compacted crushed shell or gravel, with more depth needed for driveways and properties near waterways with elevated water tables. The most common paver failure traces to base preparation shortcuts exposed by the region's intense rainy seasons.

Salt crystallization from Gulf air fractures paver stone from the inside out as one of the key deterioration mechanisms in coastal Bonita Springs. Regular sealing creates a barrier that prevents salt from penetrating porous paver surfaces.

## Common Paver Surfaces in Bonita Springs

**Travertine pavers** are the most popular choice for Bonita Springs pool decks — cool underfoot in direct sun and naturally slip-resistant. The city's upscale communities feature extensive travertine hardscaping.

**Concrete pavers** dominate the driveway market, available in colors, textures, and interlocking patterns that complement Florida architecture.

**Premium materials** are more common in Bonita Springs than in other Lee County communities. Marble, limestone, and designer-series concrete pavers with textured finishes are standard in neighborhoods like Bonita Bay and Pelican Landing. Each material has specific sealer requirements that a qualified contractor will match to the stone.

**Clay brick** provides a timeless look for driveways and walkways. Brick pavers can last 50 to 100 years with proper maintenance but absorb more heat than travertine in direct sun.

**The 45-degree herringbone pattern** is the top choice for driveways due to its structural interlock under heavy vehicle traffic.

## Local Considerations

**Permits.** Re-sanding or sealing existing pavers does not require a building permit in Bonita Springs. Paver driveways, retaining walls over 4 feet, and projects affecting drainage or the public right-of-way do require a city permit. Work in the city right-of-way requires a separate permit.

**HOA requirements.** Bonita Springs' gated communities — Bonita Bay, Pelican Landing, The Colony, and others — require architectural review board approval for hardscape changes including paver materials, colors, and patterns. The downtown streetscape improvement project is adding brick pavers to roads, burying wires, and adding roundabouts, further establishing pavers as the standard surface material in the city.

**Environmental regulations.** Bonita Springs follows NPDES stormwater regulations. Lee County enforces fertilizer and chemical use blackout periods from June 1 through September 30 near waterways. Properties near the Imperial River or Estero Bay may face additional compliance requirements for sealant runoff. Water-based, low-VOC sealers are preferred for environmental reasons and are increasingly the default choice among local contractors.
