## Understanding Paver Cleaning and Sealing

Combined cleaning and sealing is the standard maintenance service for paver surfaces in Southwest Florida. The process starts with a full pressure wash to strip away dirt, mold, algae, efflorescence, and old sealer residue. Once the surface is clean and dry, the crew re-sands joints with polymeric sand and applies a fresh coat of sealer.

The two steps are inseparable by design. Sealer will not bond to a dirty surface, and a cleaned surface left unsealed is immediately exposed to the same salt air, UV radiation, and moisture that caused the buildup in the first place. Doing both in one visit ensures the pavers go from their worst state to their most protected state without a gap.

## Benefits of Paver Cleaning and Sealing

**Cost efficiency.** One mobilization, one crew, one project. The contractor is already on site with equipment and materials. Booking cleaning and sealing together typically saves 15 to 25 percent compared to two separate visits.

**Consistent results.** The person who cleans the surface is the same person who seals it. They see the paver condition firsthand and adjust the sealer application accordingly — heavier coats on porous areas, extra attention to joints that lost sand, appropriate product selection for the material and exposure.

**Full protection cycle.** Cape Coral's combination of 53 inches of annual rain, 72 percent humidity, salt air from 400-plus miles of canals, and UV index readings that hit "Extreme" from April through October means pavers are under constant environmental stress. **Extends paver lifespan.** Regular cleaning and sealing prevents the cumulative damage that leads to spalling, cracking, and color loss. The alternative is ignoring the pavers until they need [repair](/paver-repair/) or replacement — which costs significantly more.

## How Paver Cleaning and Sealing Is Done

### Surface Preparation

The crew pressure-washes the entire paver surface using commercial-grade equipment. Concrete pavers get 2,500 to 3,000 PSI; travertine and natural stone get a gentler 1,200 to 1,800 PSI to avoid surface damage. Mold, algae, efflorescence, embedded dirt, and failing old sealer are all removed.

Joints are cleaned out and inspected. Weeds and ant colonies are cleared. Any settled, cracked, or loose pavers are flagged — minor issues can be addressed on the spot, while more significant damage may require a separate [repair](/paver-repair/) visit before sealing.

### Application

After the surface dries completely — typically overnight — the crew re-sands joints with polymeric sand and activates it with a light mist of water. Then the sealer goes on by roller or low-pressure sprayer in two thin, even coats.

You choose the finish before the work begins:

- **Natural/matte** — preserves the original look, $1 to $2 per square foot
- **Wet-look** — enhances color depth, the most popular choice in Cape Coral, $1.50 to $3 per square foot
- **High-gloss** — reflective shine, premium pricing at $2 to $4 per square foot

The [wet-look vs. natural guide](/guides/wet-look-vs-natural-paver-sealer/) compares the two most common options in detail.

### Curing and Aftercare

Keep foot traffic off for 24 to 48 hours and vehicles off for 48 to 72 hours. Full cure takes about a week. The dry season from November through May offers the best conditions for curing — lower humidity, fewer rain interruptions, and moderate surface temperatures. Summer work is possible with careful scheduling around afternoon storms.

Plan on repeating the combined service every 2 to 3 years. Waterfront properties and pool decks exposed to heavy salt and chlorine may lean toward the shorter end. The [resealing frequency guide](/guides/how-often-should-pavers-be-sealed/) covers timing recommendations.
