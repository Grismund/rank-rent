Homeowners asking **how often seal pavers Florida** contractors recommend hear the same answer: every 2 to 5 years. That range is wide enough to be nearly useless without context. In Cape Coral and Southwest Florida, the conditions that determine your specific schedule are salt exposure, sealer type, UV intensity, and how much rain your pavers absorb. This guide narrows the range for each situation.

*Last updated: November 2026. Based on manufacturer specifications and Southwest Florida climate data.*

## Overview

Paver sealer is not permanent. It is a sacrificial layer — it absorbs the UV, salt, moisture, and foot traffic that would otherwise hit the paver directly. As it degrades, the protection thins until the paver surface is exposed again. The question is not whether you will reseal, but when.

Three variables control the timeline:

**Sealer type.** Different chemistries break down at different rates. Natural/matte penetrating sealers last longest because they sit inside the paver rather than on top of it. Film-forming sealers like wet-look and high-gloss sit on the surface and take direct punishment from UV and traffic. They look better but wear faster.

**Salt exposure.** Cape Coral has over 400 miles of canals and Gulf access on the western side. Properties within a few blocks of water absorb salt-laden air daily. Coastal salt air can cut a sealer's effective lifespan roughly in half compared to inland applications. Pine Island properties face the most aggressive salt conditions in the service area.

**UV and weather intensity.** Cape Coral's UV index averages 7.6 to 7.7 annually and reaches "Extreme" (12) from April through October. That UV load, combined with 53 inches of annual rain and 72 percent average humidity, accelerates sealer degradation faster than in drier or more northern climates. Florida sealers work harder and wear out sooner than the same products applied in Virginia or Ohio.

## How They Compare

Here is how the three common sealer categories hold up under Cape Coral conditions:

**Natural/matte sealers** — 3 to 5 years between applications. These penetrating sealers absorb into the paver and protect from within. They do not change the appearance and do not wear visibly. You will not see them failing — the paver just gradually becomes more vulnerable to staining and moisture penetration. A water test (pour water on the surface; if it soaks in rather than beading, the sealer has worn off) tells you when it is time.

Best for: homeowners who want invisible protection, travertine pool decks that need a breathable sealer, low-traffic walkways. Least expensive option per application and the longest interval between treatments.

**Wet-look sealers** — 2 to 4 years. The most popular choice in Cape Coral. These film-forming sealers enhance the paver color and add a subtle sheen that makes the surface look finished. The visual enhancement is the draw, and it is also how you know when to reseal — when the color starts looking flat and the sheen has dulled, the sealer is nearing the end of its useful life.

Waterfront properties in Cape Coral should plan on the 2-year end of this range. Inland properties with less salt exposure can often go 3 to 4 years. Driveways wear faster than patios because of vehicle traffic.

Best for: driveways and patios where color enhancement is desired. The standard choice for most Cape Coral residential work.

**High-gloss sealers** — 2 to 3 years. The premium finish with the shortest lifespan. High-gloss creates a reflective surface that shows every imperfection — scuff marks, tire tracks, and wear patterns are more visible than on matte or wet-look surfaces. The shiny finish degrades faster under UV and traffic, and partial wear creates uneven patches that look worse than a fully worn matte sealer.

Best for: pool decks and showcase patios that see foot traffic only. Not recommended for driveways in Cape Coral unless the homeowner is committed to the shorter maintenance cycle.

## Practical Takeaways

**Start with wet-look for most surfaces.** It offers the best balance of appearance, protection, and maintenance interval for Cape Coral conditions. The [cost guide](/cost/) has pricing for all three options.

**Plan for 2-year cycles on waterfront properties.** If your property faces a canal, the Caloosahatchee, or any tidal waterway, budget for sealing every 2 years regardless of which sealer you choose. The salt never stops.

**Use the water test between applications.** Pour a small amount of water on the paver. If it beads, the sealer is still working. If it soaks in within seconds, it is time to call for a [cleaning and sealing](/paver-cleaning-and-sealing/) visit.

**Schedule during the dry season.** November through May offers the best conditions for sealer application in Cape Coral — lower humidity, fewer rain interruptions, and moderate surface temperatures. Sealer applied during dry season cures more completely and lasts longer.

**Budget for the full cycle, not just the first application.** A wet-look sealer on a standard driveway costs $500 to $900 per application. Over 10 years at a 3-year cycle, that is three or four applications. Factor that into your planning when choosing a sealer type.

**Do not wait until the pavers look bad.** Sealer should be reapplied before it fails completely. Once the paver surface is exposed, salt, moisture, and UV begin damaging the material itself — damage that sealer cannot reverse, only prevent.
