## Understanding Paver Repair

Paver repair covers everything between routine maintenance and full replacement: releveling sunken pavers, replacing cracked or spalled units, refilling eroded joint sand, resetting shifted sections, and rebuilding base material where it has washed out. The goal is a structurally sound, level surface ready to accept sealer.

Cape Coral's geology is the reason repair work stays in demand. The city is built on the Fort Thompson Formation — sandy soils that drain fast but settle unevenly under load. Heavy summer storms carry sand away from beneath pavers, causing rocking corners, surface waves, and gaps that widen with each rain cycle. The high water table drives moisture upward through the base, compounding the erosion. Without periodic repair, small problems become large ones.

## Benefits of Paver Repair

**Prevents trip hazards.** Uneven pavers catch toes, trip wheels, and create liability issues around pool decks and walkways. Releveling eliminates the problem before someone gets hurt.

**Stops progressive damage.** A single sunken paver transfers load unevenly to its neighbors. Left alone, it spreads — one shifted paver becomes a shifted section. Early repair keeps the fix small and affordable.

**Prepares for sealing.** Sealer follows the surface it is applied to. If pavers are uneven, cracked, or missing joint sand, the sealer will not perform properly. Contractors typically assess repair needs as part of any [cleaning and sealing](/paver-cleaning-and-sealing/) project.

**Restores drainage.** Settled pavers create low spots where water pools instead of draining. In a city where over 60 percent of properties sit within a FEMA flood zone, proper drainage across hardscape surfaces matters. Cape Coral's impervious surface calculation requirements reinforce the importance of maintaining correct grade on driveway and patio surfaces.

**Preserves investment.** Well-maintained pavers can last 50 to 100 years. Deferred repair leads to base failure that requires tearing out and rebuilding entire sections — far more expensive than fixing individual pavers as issues appear.

## How Paver Repair Is Done

### Assessment and Planning

The contractor walks the entire surface, identifying settled areas, cracked pavers, eroded joints, and sections where the base has failed. They probe the base material in problem areas and check for drainage issues. The assessment determines whether the fix is spot repair — a few pavers lifted and reset — or a larger section that needs base reconstruction.

### Leveling and Replacement

For spot repairs, the contractor lifts the affected pavers, adds or compacts base material (typically crushed limestone in Lee County), and resets the pavers at the correct grade. Cracked or spalled units are replaced with matching pavers when available.

For larger areas where the base has eroded significantly — common after major storms or in older installations built over inadequately compacted sand — the crew may need to remove an entire section, rebuild the base to 4 to 6 inches of compacted crushed shell, relay the sand bedding layer, and reinstall the pavers.

Cape Coral's sandy substrate requires careful compaction during base reconstruction. The most common paver failure in Lee County traces back to improper sub-base preparation for sandy soil combined with the intensity of the June-through-September rainy season.

### Finishing and Sealing

Once all pavers are level and secure, the crew refills joints with polymeric sand and compacts the entire surface with a plate compactor. At this point the repaired section is ready for [sealer application](/paver-sealing/) — either immediately if the repair was planned alongside a sealing project, or on a future visit.

Matching sealer finish across old and newly repaired sections is straightforward for contractors who work with the same products consistently. The result should be a uniform surface with no visible boundary between repaired and untouched areas.
