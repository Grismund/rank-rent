## Understanding Paver Sealing

Paver sealing applies a liquid coating over cleaned paver surfaces and into the joints between them. As the sealer cures, it forms a protective barrier that bonds to the paver material — concrete, travertine, brick, or natural stone. The result is a surface that resists moisture penetration, blocks UV damage, and locks joint sand in place.

There are three main finish types, each with a different look and price point:

- **Natural/matte** sealer preserves the original paver appearance without adding sheen. It penetrates the surface and protects without changing color. Runs about $1 to $2 per square foot.
- **Wet-look** sealer enhances the paver color as if the surface is damp, adding depth and richness. The most popular choice in Cape Coral. Typically $1.50 to $3 per square foot.
- **High-gloss** sealer creates a shiny, reflective finish that makes colors pop. Premium pricing at $2 to $4 per square foot. Best suited for pool decks and showpiece patios rather than high-traffic driveways.

The [wet-look vs. natural sealer guide](/guides/wet-look-vs-natural-paver-sealer/) compares the two most common options in detail.

## Benefits of Paver Sealing

Cape Coral sits on the Fort Thompson Formation — sandy soils over permeable marine limestone — with a water table that never drops far and salt air drifting in off the Gulf. Those conditions make sealing a defensive measure, not just a cosmetic upgrade.

**Color protection.** UV index readings of 10 to 12 from March through October break down paver pigments and surface binders. Sealed pavers retain their original color years longer than unsealed ones.

**Joint sand stabilization.** Cape Coral averages 53 inches of rain annually, most of it falling in heavy summer downpours. Unsealed joints lose sand rapidly, allowing pavers to shift and weeds to take root. A joint-stabilizing sealer locks everything in place.

**Salt and efflorescence defense.** Salt particles carried by coastal breezes seep into porous pavers, causing corrosion, spalling, and white powdery efflorescence deposits. Sealer blocks salt penetration at the surface.

**Weed and moss suppression.** Sealed joints leave no foothold for organic growth. This matters especially on pool decks and shaded walkways where biological growth thrives in Cape Coral's humidity.

**Stain resistance.** Oil, rust, leaf tannins, and fertilizer runoff sit on top of a sealed surface instead of soaking in. Cleanup is a garden hose and a mild soap.

## How Paver Sealing Is Done

### Surface Preparation

The crew pressure-washes the entire paver surface to remove dirt, mold, algae, efflorescence, and old sealer residue. Joints are cleaned out and inspected. Any damaged, settled, or missing pavers are flagged for [repair](/paver-repair/) before sealing proceeds. This is the step that determines how well the sealer bonds — skipping it or rushing it leads to adhesion failure.

### Application

Once the surface is clean and dry, the crew re-sands joints with polymeric sand — a material that hardens when activated with water and locks pavers together. Then the sealer is applied by roller or low-pressure sprayer in thin, even coats. Most jobs require two coats for full coverage and protection.

The dry season from November through May is the optimal window for sealer application in Cape Coral. Humidity drops below 70 percent, rain-free stretches of 12-plus hours are common, and surface temperatures stay in the 50 to 90 degree range where sealers cure properly. Summer applications are possible but require careful weather monitoring.

### Curing and Aftercare

Foot traffic is typically safe after 24 to 48 hours. Vehicles should stay off for 48 to 72 hours, depending on the product and weather conditions. Full cure takes about a week. Your contractor will give you specific timing.

For ongoing maintenance, sweep regularly, rinse with a garden hose as needed, and clean up oil or chemical spills promptly. Sealed pavers in Cape Coral should be resealed every 2 to 3 years, with waterfront properties toward the shorter end of that range.
