## What Drives Paver Sealing Demand in Fort Myers

### Climate and Weather Impact

Fort Myers shares Cape Coral's subtropical climate but adds its own wrinkles. The city receives approximately 54 inches of rain annually, with heavy deluges concentrated between June and September. Those summer storms do not just wet the pavers — they displace joint sand, undercut poorly drained paver beds, and push standing water against surfaces for hours.

UV index readings of 10 to 12 from April through October break down the pigments and surface binders in paver materials. Salt air drifting in off the Caloosahatchee and the Gulf accelerates that damage on any property within a few miles of the water. Breathable, salt-resistant sealants must be reapplied every 2 to 3 years to maintain protection.

The optimal window for paver sealing in Lee County is the dry season from November through May, when humidity drops below 70 percent, surface temperatures stay between 50 and 90 degrees, and 12-plus-hour rain-free windows allow full 24- to 72-hour curing.

### Common Paver Issues in Fort Myers

Lee County's dominant soils — Myakka and Immokalee fine sands — are poorly drained with a seasonally high water table 12 to 24 inches below grade. A porous limestone substrate beneath allows moisture to wick upward through the sand base and into paver joints, accelerating efflorescence and sealer failure.

The karst topography beneath Fort Myers includes porous limestone with dissolved voids that can cause localized settling under paver installations over time. Combined with summer storm intensity, this means Fort Myers driveways and patios are prone to settling, shifting, and joint sand loss if the base was not properly prepared and the surface is not regularly maintained.

## Common Paver Surfaces in Fort Myers

**Travertine** is the most popular paver material in the Fort Myers market. It stays cooler than concrete in direct sun, drains well, and does not become slippery when wet — making it the dominant choice for pool decks and patios. Travertine requires a penetrating or breathable sealer that allows moisture to escape the stone without trapping it inside.

**Concrete pavers** are the most widely installed driveway material, available in herringbone, running bond, and basketweave patterns. They handle vehicle traffic well and accept all sealer types.

**Brick pavers** remain popular for traditional and Mediterranean-style homes. Laid in herringbone or running bond patterns, they seal well with both wet-look and natural finishes.

**Shellstone, porcelain, and marble** are also found in Fort Myers, particularly in upscale neighborhoods. Each material has specific sealer requirements that a qualified contractor will match to the stone.

## Local Considerations

**Permits.** Re-sanding and sealing existing pavers on residential property does not require a building permit in Fort Myers or unincorporated Lee County — the work is classified as maintenance. New paver installation affecting drainage or the right-of-way does require a permit. A Lee County Local Business Tax Receipt and Certificate of Use are required for any paver sealing company operating in the area.

**Stormwater regulations.** Lee County's NPDES permit prohibits illicit discharge into the storm sewer system. Sealant rinse water and chemical runoff must not enter storm drains. Fort Myers' environmental advisory board has recognized permeable interlocking pavers as pervious surfaces for stormwater calculations, and homeowners who install permeable pavers may qualify for a stormwater utility credit.

**Environmental standards.** Water-based, low-VOC sealers are increasingly used by Fort Myers contractors due to reduced environmental impact. Coal-tar based sealants produce PAH levels in stormwater runoff 65 times higher than unsealed surfaces and are being phased out across the region.
