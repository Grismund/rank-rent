The short answer on **paver sealing cost Cape Coral** homeowners can expect: most residential jobs fall between $400 and $1,200, depending on the surface area, paver condition, and sealer finish. The rest of this page explains what moves your number within that range.

These figures reflect what Cape Coral-area contractors are quoting in the current market. Your specific pavers, finish choice, and the contractor you select will shift it.

*Last updated: November 2026. Costs vary by contractor and site conditions.*

## Average Paver Sealing Prices

### Small Patio or Walkway

Roughly 100 to 200 square feet. Budget **$200 to $500**. The low end is a natural/matte sealer on pavers in good condition. The high end covers a wet-look finish on pavers that need heavy cleaning, joint sand replacement, and minor repair work before sealing.

Small jobs carry a higher per-square-foot cost because setup does not scale down proportionally. Mobilizing the pressure washer, mixing polymeric sand, and preparing sealer takes nearly the same time regardless of area size.

### Driveway Sealing

The most common request in Cape Coral. A standard driveway covers 400 to 600 square feet, typically running **$500 to $900**. That range covers cleaning, joint sand replenishment, and two coats of sealer.

Most homeowners choose a wet-look finish for driveways — it enhances the paver color and provides a finished appearance without the maintenance demands of a high-gloss surface. Driveways also take vehicle traffic, so the sealer needs to withstand tire wear and occasional fluid drips. A wet-look or natural sealer handles both; high-gloss is better suited for lower-traffic areas.

### Full Property (Driveway + Patio + Pool Deck)

When you seal everything at once — common in Cape Coral homes where paver hardscaping extends across the driveway, patio, walkways, and pool deck — the total area is typically 800 to 1,200 square feet. Expect **$800 to $1,400**.

Bundling the full property into a single project saves money. The contractor is already on site with equipment and materials, so you pay for one mobilization instead of two or three separate visits. The per-square-foot cost drops as area increases.

## Factors That Influence Paver Sealing Cost

Five variables, roughly in order of impact:

**Surface condition.** Pavers that are heavily stained, covered in algae, or have significant efflorescence need more cleaning time before sealer can go down. Pavers with eroded joints need polymeric sand replacement. Settled or cracked pavers need [repair](/paver-repair/) first. The condition of the surface is the single biggest reason two properties with the same square footage receive different quotes.

**Square footage.** More area means more material and labor, but less per square foot. The pricing tiers above reflect this.

**Sealer finish.** Natural/matte runs $1 to $2 per square foot. Wet-look is $1.50 to $3. High-gloss is $2 to $4. The product cost is the primary difference — application labor is similar across all three.

**Paver material.** Concrete pavers accept all sealer types readily. Travertine requires a breathable, penetrating sealer that costs more and must be applied carefully to avoid trapping moisture inside the stone. Natural stone, marble, and shellstone each have their own product requirements.

**Access and layout.** A simple rectangular driveway is faster to seal than a curved pool deck with borders, steps, and landscaping edges. Complex layouts require more masking, cutting in, and careful roller work.

## Wet-Look vs. Natural vs. Penetrating Sealers

Three categories, each at a different price point and with a different visual result:

**Natural/matte sealers** ($1 to $2 per square foot) preserve the paver's original appearance. They penetrate the surface and protect from within without changing the color or adding sheen. Best for homeowners who want protection without a visual change. Re-seal every 3 to 5 years.

**Wet-look sealers** ($1.50 to $3 per square foot) enhance the paver color as though the surface is damp, adding depth and richness. The most popular choice in Cape Coral — it makes faded pavers look new again. Re-seal every 2 to 4 years.

**High-gloss sealers** ($2 to $4 per square foot) add a shiny, reflective finish. They make colors pop but require more maintenance — they show scuff marks, tire tracks, and wear patterns more readily than matte or wet-look finishes. Best suited for pool decks and showpiece patios rather than driveways. Re-seal every 2 to 3 years.
