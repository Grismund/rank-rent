Choosing between **wet look vs natural paver sealer**? If one contractor recommends wet-look while another suggests natural, this guide explains the difference so you can make an informed decision. These are the two most common sealer categories applied to Cape Coral pavers, and the right choice depends on the surface, the look you want, and how the pavers are exposed to Southwest Florida's climate.

*Last updated: November 2026. Based on manufacturer specifications and local contractor practices.*

## Overview

**Natural (matte) sealers** are penetrating sealers. They soak into the paver and protect from within. The surface looks the same after application as it did before — no added color, no sheen. You would not know the sealer was there by looking at it. The protection is invisible: moisture resistance, salt blocking, and UV defense all happen below the surface.

**Wet-look sealers** are film-forming sealers. They sit on top of the paver and create a visible coating that enhances the color as though the surface is damp. Faded pavers look new again. The finish has a subtle sheen — not quite glossy, but noticeably richer than the uncoated material. It is the most popular finish in Cape Coral because the visual result is immediate and dramatic.

Both categories protect against the same threats: UV degradation, salt penetration, moisture cycling, efflorescence, and joint sand erosion. Where they differ is appearance, durability profile, maintenance interval, price, and suitability for specific paver types.

## How They Compare

**Appearance.** Natural sealer is invisible. Wet-look enhances color and adds sheen. If your pavers have faded from years of Cape Coral sun, wet-look restores the original vibrancy. If the pavers look fine and you just want protection, natural does the job without changing anything.

**Durability.** Natural penetrating sealers typically last 3 to 5 years between applications because they are protected inside the paver rather than exposed on the surface. Wet-look film-forming sealers last 2 to 4 years — the film takes direct punishment from UV, rain, salt, and foot traffic. On waterfront Cape Coral properties where salt exposure is constant, wet-look may need refreshing every 2 years.

**Price.** Natural sealers run $1 to $2 per square foot installed. Wet-look runs $1.50 to $3 per square foot. Over a 10-year period, the total cost difference narrows because natural requires fewer applications. On a 500-square-foot driveway, natural costs roughly $500 to $1,000 per application versus $750 to $1,500 for wet-look — but natural goes 3 to 5 years between applications while wet-look goes 2 to 4.

**Application.** Natural sealers are more forgiving during application. Because they soak in, overlapping roller strokes do not create visible lines. Wet-look requires more precision — uneven application shows as dark streaks or hazy patches on the surface. Professional application matters more with wet-look than with natural.

**Moisture management.** This matters significantly in Cape Coral. The city sits on the Fort Thompson Formation — sandy soils over permeable limestone — with a water table that stays high year-round. Moisture wicks upward through the base and into pavers continuously. Natural penetrating sealers allow that moisture to escape through the surface while still blocking salt and water from entering above. Wet-look film-forming sealers partially block moisture from escaping, which can cause whitening or hazing if moisture is trapped beneath the film.

For travertine pool decks — where moisture from the pool, rain, and ground all converge — natural penetrating sealers are almost always the better choice. For concrete driveway pavers where the base is well-drained and ground moisture is managed, wet-look performs without issues.

**UV resistance.** Both sealer types block UV from reaching the paver. The difference is that UV also attacks the wet-look film itself, gradually dulling the sheen and color enhancement. Natural sealers, being below the surface, are not directly exposed to UV and degrade more slowly.

## Practical Takeaways

**Choose wet-look for concrete driveways and patios.** The color enhancement makes the biggest visual impact on concrete pavers, and the surface handles the film-forming chemistry well. It is the standard Cape Coral choice for good reason.

**Choose natural for travertine pool decks.** Travertine needs a breathable sealer that lets moisture escape the stone. A film-forming wet-look sealer on travertine can trap moisture, causing whitening and potentially damaging the stone. Most Cape Coral contractors default to natural penetrating sealers for any travertine surface.

**Consider natural for waterfront properties.** If your property faces a canal or the Gulf and salt exposure is extreme, a natural penetrating sealer may be more practical. It lasts longer between applications and does not develop the visible wear patterns that wet-look shows under heavy salt conditions.

**Mix finishes across the property.** There is no rule requiring the same sealer on every surface. Many Cape Coral homeowners use wet-look on the driveway for curb appeal and natural on the pool deck for breathability. The contractor handles both in the same visit.

**Do not choose high-gloss for driveways.** High-gloss is a third option covered in the [cost guide](/cost/) that creates a reflective, shiny finish. It looks striking but wears the fastest and shows every scuff mark. It is best reserved for low-traffic pool decks and showpiece areas.

**Always start with clean pavers.** Regardless of which sealer you choose, the surface must be pressure-washed and old sealer must be stripped before application. Sealer over dirt or failing old product will not bond. The [combined cleaning and sealing](/paver-cleaning-and-sealing/) service handles everything in one visit.
