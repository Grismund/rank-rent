Understanding **Florida window tint laws** is essential before getting any vehicle tinted in Cape Coral. Florida sets specific VLT (visible light transmission) limits by window position and vehicle type, and getting them wrong means a traffic citation and the expense of re-tinting to comply. This guide covers the exact percentages, where they apply on the vehicle, enforcement details, medical exemptions, and what the law means for your film choices in Lee County.

This information applies to all vehicles registered or driven in Florida, regardless of where the tint was applied.

*Last updated: November 2026. Based on Florida Statute §316.2953 and current enforcement guidance.*

## What You Need to Know

Florida law sets different VLT minimums depending on the window position and vehicle class:

**Sedans and coupes:**

- Front side windows must allow at least **28 percent VLT** with no more than 25 percent reflectance.
- Rear side windows must allow at least **15 percent VLT**.
- Rear windshield must allow at least **15 percent VLT**.
- Windshield permits only a non-reflective tint strip above the manufacturer's AS-1 line.

**SUVs, vans, and trucks:**

- Front side windows follow the same rule: at least **28 percent VLT** with no more than 25 percent reflectance.
- Rear side windows and rear windshield may go as dark as **6 percent VLT** — significantly more permissive than sedans.

**Additional rules:**

- Colored tint that changes the normal color of the window is not allowed.
- A sticker identifying legal tinting is required on the inside of the driver's side door jamb after installation.
- No red, amber, or blue tint is permitted on front windows.

**Medical exemptions:** Florida Statute §316.2954 allows drivers with qualifying medical conditions to apply for a medical exemption for darker front window tint. A physician's prescription is required, and the exemption documentation must be carried in the vehicle. Qualifying conditions include photosensitivity, lupus, and other conditions where reduced light exposure is medically necessary.

## Side-by-Side Comparison

Here is where Florida's law stands relative to common misconceptions and neighboring state rules:

**Vehicle-type distinction matters.** Unlike Arizona and many other states, Florida treats sedans differently from SUVs and trucks on the rear glass. Sedans are limited to 15 percent VLT on rear side windows and rear windshield, while SUVs, vans, and trucks can go as dark as 6 percent. This is one of the more permissive rear-window allowances in the country for larger vehicles.

**Front windows are uniform.** All vehicle types follow the same 28 percent VLT rule on the front driver and passenger windows. There is no distinction between a compact car and a full-size truck at the front glass.

**No annual inspection.** Florida does not have a mandatory vehicle safety inspection program. There is no routine tint check after installation. Enforcement happens during traffic stops, where law enforcement may test VLT with a handheld tint meter if the windows appear out of compliance.

**Reflectance limit.** The 25 percent reflectance cap on front windows eliminates highly mirrored and metallic tint products. Modern carbon and ceramic films are not reflective in this sense — they reject heat through chemical composition, not mirror-like reflection. This restriction rarely creates a practical issue with current film products.

**Residential and commercial glass is unregulated.** Florida tint law applies only to vehicle glass. Homeowners and business owners may apply any film they choose to building windows, subject only to HOA rules and applicable energy codes for commercial buildings.

## What This Means for Your Project

For Cape Coral vehicle owners, Florida's law creates a practical framework with meaningful choices:

**Front windows: invest in film chemistry, not darkness.** At 28 percent VLT, the front side windows admit a moderate amount of light. The shade is visible but not dark. What determines heat performance at this level is the film composition — a ceramic film at 28 percent VLT rejects substantially more infrared heat than a dyed film at the same shade. Since you cannot legally go darker on the front, choosing ceramic where it counts delivers the strongest heat protection within the legal limit.

**Rear windows: match darkness to your priorities.** Sedan owners can go to 15 percent VLT across the rear glass, which provides strong privacy and significant heat blocking. SUV and truck owners can go to 6 percent — nearly full limo tint — for maximum privacy and heat rejection on the glass that covers the most area.

**SUV and truck owners have more flexibility.** With nearly 75 percent of new vehicle sales in Florida being SUVs and trucks, the more permissive rear limits benefit the majority of vehicles on Cape Coral's roads. Darker rear glass on a vehicle that spends hours in a Cape Coral driveway or parking lot under direct sun makes a measurable difference in cabin temperature.

**Enforcement is not aggressive, but it is real.** Officers typically notice obviously non-compliant front windows — dark, reflective, or clearly below 28 percent VLT. A properly installed film at or near the legal limit does not draw attention. If you are stopped, the officer may use a tint meter. Films installed at the legal percentage by a professional rarely fail a meter test.

**The installer handles compliance.** Any qualified Cape Coral tint shop knows Florida's limits by window position and vehicle type. The installer will recommend shades that fall within legal bounds and affix the required door-jamb sticker after installation.
