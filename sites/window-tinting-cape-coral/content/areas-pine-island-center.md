### Sun and Heat in Pine Island Center

Pine Island shares the Cape Coral-Fort Myers metro area's subtropical climate: 264 to 271 sunny days per year, a UV index averaging 7.6 to 7.7 annually, and peak UV readings of 12 (Extreme) during April, May, and August. The island's open water surroundings amplify effective UV exposure through reflection off Matlacha Pass and Pine Island Sound. A vehicle or home on Pine Island absorbs UV from both direct sunlight and reflected radiation bouncing off the surrounding water.

The subtropical climate delivers an average annual temperature of 75 degrees, summer highs averaging 91 degrees, and constant breezes from the surrounding water. Those breezes carry salt particles that settle on vehicle surfaces and attack clearcoat, making [paint protection film](/paint-protection-film/) an especially relevant companion service for island residents.

### Energy Savings and Comfort

Pine Island homes range from older Florida-style houses to newer construction, many oriented to capture water views across Pine Island Sound or Charlotte Harbor. Those view windows absorb significant solar heat gain. Residential film rated for solar rejection can block 35 to 70 percent of incoming energy, reducing the cooling load on air conditioning systems that run nearly year-round in this climate.

The island's small population and limited commercial development mean most window tinting demand comes from residential and automotive applications rather than commercial projects. Homeowners who invested in waterfront property want to protect interiors — furniture, flooring, artwork — from the same UV that drew them to the island in the first place.

## Legal Tint Limits in Florida

No permit is required for vehicle window tinting in Florida. Tint shops must comply with Florida Statute §316.2953:

- **Front side windows:** At least 28 percent VLT, no more than 25 percent reflectance
- **Rear side windows (sedans):** At least 15 percent VLT
- **Rear side windows (SUVs/vans/trucks):** As dark as 6 percent VLT
- **Rear windshield (sedans):** At least 15 percent VLT
- **Rear windshield (SUVs/vans/trucks):** As dark as 6 percent VLT
- **Windshield:** Non-reflective tint strip above the AS-1 line only

Medical exemptions for darker front tint are available under Florida Statute §316.2954. Full details are in the [Florida tint laws guide](/guides/state-window-tint-laws-explained/).

## Local Considerations

Pine Island Center is unincorporated and falls under Lee County's permitting and regulatory system. While no permit applies to vehicle tinting, the island has limited HOA governance compared to mainland communities — but individual subdivisions may still have deed restrictions on home modifications, including visible residential window film.

The island's vehicle fleet skews heavily toward trucks and SUVs for boat towing and rural driving. Ceramic film is the most recommended option for these larger vehicles, particularly for trucks that spend hours at boat ramps and waterfront parking areas under full sun and reflected UV.
