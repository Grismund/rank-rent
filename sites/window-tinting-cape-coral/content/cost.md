Here is the bottom line on **window tinting cost Cape Coral** vehicle owners and homeowners can expect: a professional automotive tint job generally runs between $150 and $500 depending on the vehicle and film selected. Residential film is priced per square foot. Commercial projects scale with the building. The sections below explain what moves your number within those ranges.

These figures reflect what Cape Coral-area installers are quoting in the current market. Your specific vehicle, windows, film choice, and the installer you select will shift the final number.

## Typical Window Tinting Costs

### Sedan or Coupe

The most straightforward automotive tint job. Smaller glass area, fewer windows, and faster installation. Budget **$150 to $350** for a full set of side and rear windows. The low end represents dyed film; the high end represents ceramic. Front side windows at Florida's 28 percent VLT limit and darker film on the rear glass is the typical configuration for sedans.

Base labor charges keep the floor from dropping much below $150 even on compact vehicles, since setup time is similar across vehicle sizes.

### SUV or Truck

More glass area means more film and more time. SUVs and crossovers — which account for roughly 75 percent of new vehicle sales in Florida — carry wide rear windows, cargo area glass, and sometimes panoramic roofs. Full-size trucks with crew cabs add four full-size rear doors.

Budget **$200 to $500** for a standard SUV or truck. Full ceramic on a large SUV with rear cargo glass and a panoramic roof can reach the upper end of that range. Costs vary by installer.

### Home Windows

Residential window film is priced per square foot, typically **$5 to $15 installed**. A standard Cape Coral home with 10 to 15 windows might cost **$1,000 to $3,000** for a full installation. The number, size, and accessibility of windows drive the total. Second-story glass that requires ladder work costs more per window than ground-level sliders.

For commercial projects, see the [commercial window tinting page](/commercial-window-tinting/). Pricing runs $6 to $20 per square foot depending on film type and project complexity.

## What Affects Window Tinting Pricing?

Five factors, roughly in order of impact:

**Film type.** This is the largest variable on most jobs. Dyed film is the least expensive. Ceramic is the most. The gap between the cheapest and most expensive film on the same vehicle can exceed $200. The [film comparison section below](#comparing-tint-film-costs) details the differences.

**Vehicle size and glass area.** More glass means more material and more labor hours. A compact coupe has less than half the tintable glass area of a full-size SUV with a panoramic roof.

**Number of windows.** Some vehicle owners tint only the rear windows. Others do the full set including a windshield visor strip. Each additional window adds incremental cost.

**Existing tint removal.** If the vehicle already has film that needs to come off — faded, bubbling, or the wrong shade — stripping the old material adds time and cost before new film is applied. Removal on a full vehicle can add $100 to $200 depending on how stubbornly the old adhesive holds.

**Installer and location.** Pricing varies between shops. A tint shop with high overhead in a retail plaza may quote differently than a mobile installer who comes to your property. Neither approach is inherently better — what matters is the quality of the work and the warranty behind it.

## Comparing Tint Film Costs

The film you select determines both the price and the performance of the tint. Here is how the three main categories compare for automotive applications:

**Basic dyed film: $50 to $150 per vehicle.** The entry-level option. Dye absorbs visible light and provides privacy. Heat rejection is limited at 15 to 30 percent. The film fades over time. Suitable for tight budgets or vehicles that spend most of their time in a garage.

**Mid-range carbon film: $150 to $250 per vehicle.** Carbon particles block more infrared heat at 35 to 50 percent rejection without fading to purple. Consistent finish over the life of the film. The practical choice for daily drivers who want real heat reduction at a moderate price.

**Premium ceramic film: $200 to $400 per vehicle.** Nano-ceramic particles reject 50 to 70 percent of infrared heat while transmitting more visible light than darker films at comparable performance levels. No signal interference, no fading, no color shift. Full ceramic on a larger vehicle (SUV, crew-cab truck) can reach $300 to $600.
