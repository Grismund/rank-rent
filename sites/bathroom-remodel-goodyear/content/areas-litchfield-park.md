## What Drives Bathroom Remodel Demand in Litchfield Park

### Home Ages and Common Bathroom Issues

Litchfield Park has two distinct layers of housing stock that drive remodel demand for different reasons.

The older layer centers on Old Litchfield Park, where single-family ranch-style homes date back to the 1950s. These homes have been through multiple decades of use, and their bathrooms often need full gut renovations. Original cast-iron drain lines may be corroded. Galvanized supply pipes restrict water flow after years of mineral buildup. Floor structures beneath tubs and showers may show moisture damage from decades before modern waterproofing standards were common.

The newer layer includes subdivisions like The Village at Litchfield Park, Wigwam Creek, and Russell Ranch, where the median home construction year is 2003. These homes — now 20-plus years old — are entering their first remodel cycle with the same builder-grade finishes that are aging across the West Valley.

Litchfield Park's water hardness compounds both issues. Liberty Utilities groundwater wells deliver water averaging 13.6 GPG, with some readings as high as 21.1 GPG. That hard water has been depositing mineral scale on fixtures and inside pipes for the full life of every home in the city.

## Permit Requirements in Litchfield Park

The Litchfield Park Building Department requires a building permit for bathroom remodels that involve plumbing, electrical, or structural changes. Applications are submitted to Building Safety at 214 W Wigwam Blvd. Residential remodel permits typically take five to ten business days to process.

An important distinction for Litchfield Park: the city currently enforces the 2018 International Codes, including the 2018 International Plumbing Code and the 2017 National Electrical Code. This is one code cycle behind Goodyear, Avondale, Buckeye, and Tolleson, all of which have adopted the 2024 editions. Your contractor should verify the active code before submitting plans.

For properties in unincorporated areas near Litchfield Park, permits are handled by the Maricopa County Building Department rather than the city. Arizona ROC licensing requirements apply the same regardless — any project over $1,000 in labor and materials requires a licensed contractor.
