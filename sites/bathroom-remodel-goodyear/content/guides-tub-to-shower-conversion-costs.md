Thinking about replacing your bathtub with a walk-in shower? Understanding the tub to shower conversion cost Goodyear homeowners pay helps you decide whether this project fits your budget and your home. This guide breaks down the major cost components, compares conversion pricing against keeping and updating the existing tub, and covers Arizona-specific factors that influence the bottom line.

## What You Need to Know

A tub-to-shower conversion in the Goodyear area typically costs between $4,000 and $9,000. That range covers removing the existing tub, repositioning the drain, running new supply plumbing for the shower valve, waterproofing the enclosure, tiling the walls and floor, and installing a glass door or panel.

The conversion costs more than a simple tub replacement ($2,500 to $7,000) because it involves structural changes to the plumbing. A tub drain and a shower drain sit in different positions. Moving the drain requires cutting into the slab or subfloor, connecting a new P-trap, and routing the new drain line to the existing waste stack. This is the single biggest driver of conversion cost compared to a like-for-like tub swap.

Arizona building code adds another layer. In Goodyear, Avondale, Buckeye, and Tolleson, any plumbing modification triggers a permit under the 2024 International Plumbing Code. Litchfield Park enforces the 2018 edition but has the same permit trigger. Permit fees range from $100 to $500 depending on the municipality. The contractor pulls the permit, schedules rough and final inspections, and factors that timeline into the project.

Arizona also requires ROC licensing for any contractor performing work where labor and materials exceed $1,000. This is non-negotiable under state law and applies to every tub-to-shower conversion.

### Key Cost Components

Here is how the typical conversion budget breaks down:

- **Labor** — 45% to 55% of the total. Covers demolition, plumbing rough-in, waterproofing, tile installation, glass installation, and cleanup.
- **Tile and waterproofing** — 20% to 25%. Shower wall tile, floor tile, waterproofing membrane, and grout. Porcelain tile is the most cost-effective hard-water-resistant option.
- **Glass enclosure** — 10% to 15%. A frameless glass panel or hinged door. Semi-frameless options cost less. Shower curtains are the cheapest option but offer a less finished result.
- **Plumbing** — 10% to 15%. Drain relocation, new mixing valve, supply-line adjustments, and showerhead installation.
- **Permits and overhead** — 5% to 8%. Building permit fees, dumpster rental, material disposal, and protection of adjacent areas during construction.

### What Affects the Final Number

Several factors push your conversion cost toward the lower or upper end of the range:

- **Slab vs. raised foundation** — Relocating a drain in a slab-on-grade home (common in Goodyear) requires cutting concrete, which costs more than accessing plumbing through a crawlspace.
- **Curbless vs. curbed entry** — A curbless (zero-threshold) shower requires precise floor slope work and sometimes structural modifications. It adds $500 to $1,500 over a standard curbed shower.
- **Tile grade** — Basic ceramic tile starts around $2 per square foot. Mid-range porcelain runs $5 to $12. Natural stone or decorative mosaics can exceed $20.
- **Glass type** — A single fixed panel costs less than a full frameless hinged door. Factory-applied hydrophobic coatings add $100 to $300 but reduce hard-water maintenance.

## Side-by-Side Comparison

Here is how tub-to-shower conversion stacks up against two alternatives for the same space:

**Tub-to-Shower Conversion: $4,000 to $9,000**
- Removes the bathtub entirely
- Installs a walk-in shower with tile and glass
- Requires drain relocation and plumbing permit
- Opens up floor space
- Easier daily maintenance in hard-water areas

**Tub Replacement (same footprint): $2,500 to $7,000**
- Swaps the old tub for a new unit
- Keeps the existing drain location
- May not require a permit if no plumbing relocation
- Preserves bathtub functionality for resale

**Tub-Shower Combo Remodel: $3,500 to $8,000**
- Updates tile, fixtures, and hardware around the existing tub
- New showerhead and valve
- Preserves both tub and shower function
- Less disruptive than a full conversion

The right choice depends on how your household uses the space. If nobody takes baths and you want to reclaim floor area, the conversion is worth the premium. If resale value matters and you have only one bathroom with a tub, keeping the tub may be the smarter financial move.

## What This Means for Your Project

Before committing to a conversion, consider these Goodyear-specific factors:

**Hard water protection is essential.** At 13 to 22 GPG across the West Valley, untreated water will coat your brand-new shower tile and glass with mineral scale within months. Discuss water softener options with your contractor or at minimum select fixtures and glass with hard-water-resistant finishes.

**Slab homes dominate the area.** Most Goodyear-area homes are built on concrete slabs. Drain relocation means cutting into concrete, which adds cost but is routine for experienced plumbing contractors. Ask for the drain-relocation cost as a separate line item in your estimate so you can compare bids accurately.

**Keep at least one tub if possible.** Removing the only bathtub in a home can reduce its appeal to buyers with young children. If your home has two or more bathrooms with tubs, converting one to a shower has minimal resale impact.

**Permits protect your investment.** Skipping the permit to save $100 to $500 means the work is not inspected and may not comply with current code. Unpermitted plumbing modifications can create liability issues if a leak causes damage later, and they complicate the sale of your home.
