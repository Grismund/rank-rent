## Understanding Full Bathroom Remodel

A full remodel means every component gets replaced. The existing tile, fixtures, vanity, toilet, lighting, and often the subfloor all come out. The contractor inspects framing and plumbing lines once the walls are open, addresses any water damage or code deficiencies, and rebuilds the space with new materials throughout.

In the Goodyear area, homes built between 2002 and 2011 are prime candidates for a full remodel. Two decades of hard water at 13 to 22 GPG has taken a toll behind the walls. Supply lines may show mineral scale buildup that restricts water flow. Drain connections can develop slow leaks at joints that corrode over time. Opening the walls during a full remodel lets a contractor catch and fix these hidden issues before they cause serious damage.

This level of work requires permits in every West Valley municipality. Arizona also requires the contractor to hold an active ROC license for any project where labor and materials exceed $1,000.

## What to Expect During Full Bathroom Remodel

### Planning and Design

Your contractor visits the home, takes detailed measurements, and reviews the existing plumbing and electrical layout. You choose materials together — tile, fixtures, countertop, lighting, hardware. The contractor prepares a scope of work, pulls permits through the local building department, and sets a construction schedule. Expect this phase to take one to two weeks.

### Demolition and Rough-In

Demolition removes everything down to the studs. The contractor inspects framing for water damage and replaces any compromised lumber. New plumbing supply lines and drain connections go in, along with updated electrical circuits and GFCI-protected outlets as required by the 2024 International Codes. A rough-in inspection by the local building department happens before walls get closed up.

### Finishing and Inspection

Waterproofing membrane goes on the shower walls and floor pan. Tile installation follows, then the vanity, countertop, toilet, and fixtures. The contractor installs lighting, mirrors, and hardware last. A final inspection from the building department confirms everything meets code before you use the space.

The full process typically takes three to six weeks from demolition to final walkthrough. Arizona's inspection scheduling can add a few days between phases, so your contractor factors that into the timeline.

## Full Bathroom Remodel Materials and Styles

The West Valley's hard water makes material selection especially important. Porcelain and ceramic tile handle mineral deposits far better than natural stone, which needs regular sealing to resist scale absorption. Frameless glass shower enclosures look sharp but require consistent cleaning in hard-water areas. Many Goodyear homeowners opt for treated glass coatings that shed mineral buildup.

Popular material choices for full remodels in the area include:

- **Porcelain tile** in large-format sizes for fewer grout lines and easier maintenance
- **Quartz countertops** that resist staining and do not require sealing
- **Water-efficient fixtures** rated for Arizona's dry climate and conservation requirements
- **Matte or brushed finishes** on faucets and hardware that show water spots less than polished chrome

Desert-inspired neutral palettes — warm grays, sand tones, and soft whites — remain the most popular aesthetic across the Goodyear market.
