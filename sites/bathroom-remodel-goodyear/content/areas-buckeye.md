## What Drives Bathroom Remodel Demand in Buckeye

### Home Ages and Common Bathroom Issues

Buckeye's housing stock is younger than its West Valley neighbors. The median construction year is 2011, and less than 2% of homes were built before 1960. That means most Buckeye bathrooms are 10 to 20 years old rather than 20-plus — newer, but still reaching the age where builder-grade finishes start showing wear.

What accelerates the timeline is water quality. Buckeye water hardness averages 16 to 25 GPG depending on source and season, with some areas spiking above 25 GPG during summer months when water sources shift. At those levels, mineral scale accumulates rapidly. Water heater anode rods exhaust in two to three years instead of the typical six to eight. Tankless heat exchangers lose efficiency without regular descaling. Faucet cartridges and shower valves corrode internally, reducing flow and causing drips.

In Buckeye, a home that is only 12 or 15 years old can have bathroom fixtures that look and perform like they are much older because of the aggressive water conditions.

### Local Housing Market Trends

Buckeye grew 37.1% since the 2020 census, adding 33,947 residents and making it one of the fastest-growing cities in the nation. Master-planned communities like Verrado, Tartesso, Sundance, and Sun City Festival account for much of this growth. Tartesso alone has 49,000 housing units planned at buildout.

That growth wave means thousands of homes from the mid-2000s to early 2010s are all reaching remodel age at roughly the same time. The first generation of Verrado homes, built starting around 2004, are now 20-plus years old. Even the "newer" homes in Tartesso built in 2015 are approaching 12 years — right when hard-water damage starts becoming visible on fixtures and glass.

Buckeye's 86.1% owner-occupancy rate also supports strong remodel spending. Homeowners invest in upgrades; renters typically do not. The high ownership concentration means more households making discretionary renovation decisions.

## Permit Requirements in Buckeye

Buckeye issues its own building permits through the city's Development Services department — work is not reviewed by Maricopa County. Bathroom remodels involving structural, plumbing, or electrical changes require a permit. Cosmetic updates such as paint and flooring replacement may be exempt.

Buckeye adopted the 2024 International Codes — including the 2024 IPC and 2023 NEC — effective January 1, 2025. All permit applications filed after that date must comply with the 2024 code edition. The city conducts its own rough-in and final inspections for plumbing and electrical work.

Arizona ROC licensing applies: any project exceeding $1,000 in labor and materials requires a licensed contractor. Plumbers must hold a C-37R or C-77 classification.
