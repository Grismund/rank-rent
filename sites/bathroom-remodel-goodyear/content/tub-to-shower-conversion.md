## Understanding Tub-to-Shower Conversion

Many Goodyear-area homes built between 2002 and 2011 came with a tub-shower combination in the master bathroom and a second tub in the hall bath. Homeowners who rarely use one of those tubs are increasingly converting it to a dedicated walk-in shower. The result is a more functional space that reflects how the household actually uses the bathroom.

A conversion involves more than pulling out a tub and dropping in a shower base. The drain location usually needs to move because tub drains and shower drains sit in different positions. Supply plumbing may need adjustment for a new valve location. The wall surfaces, waterproofing, and floor pan all get rebuilt to suit the shower configuration.

Because the work involves plumbing modifications, a permit is typically required. In Goodyear, Avondale, Buckeye, and Tolleson, that means compliance with the 2024 International Plumbing Code. Litchfield Park currently enforces the 2018 edition. Your contractor handles the permit application and coordinates inspections.

## What to Expect During Tub-to-Shower Conversion

### Planning and Design

Your contractor evaluates the existing tub alcove, measures the space, and discusses your preferences for shower size, entry style, tile, and fixtures. If you want a curbless (zero-threshold) shower for accessibility, the contractor checks whether the floor structure can accommodate the necessary slope to the drain. The design phase also covers fixture selection and permit paperwork.

### Demolition and Rough-In

The tub comes out, and the contractor removes the surrounding tile and old waterproofing. The drain gets repositioned to the center of the new shower footprint or to whatever location the design calls for. Supply lines move if the shower valve sits in a different spot than the old tub valve. A rough plumbing inspection happens before the walls close up.

### Finishing and Inspection

A waterproofing membrane covers the shower walls and the new floor pan. Tile goes up on the walls and floor, followed by grout, a glass enclosure or curtain rod, and the showerhead and controls. The building department conducts a final inspection of the plumbing and electrical connections. Most tub-to-shower conversions take one to two weeks.

## Tub-to-Shower Conversion Materials and Styles

Conversion projects in the Goodyear area commonly feature:

- **Porcelain floor tile with a textured finish** — Slip resistance matters in a shower without a tub rim to contain water. Matte or textured porcelain provides grip while resisting hard-water mineral absorption.
- **Linear drains** — A single channel drain along one wall replaces the traditional center drain. The floor slopes in one direction instead of four, simplifying the tile layout and creating a cleaner look.
- **Frameless glass panels** — A fixed glass panel or hinged door replaces the old shower curtain or tub surround. Factory-applied coatings help shed hard-water deposits.
- **Hand-held showerheads on a slide bar** — Practical for households with varying heights and useful for accessibility. Many models include a fixed head plus a detachable wand.
