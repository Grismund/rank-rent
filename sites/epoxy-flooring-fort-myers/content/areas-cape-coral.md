## Why Cape Coral Homeowners Choose Epoxy Flooring

### Climate and Concrete Conditions

Cape Coral's geology creates the most moisture-intensive environment in the Fort Myers service area. The city is built on the Fort Thompson Formation — permeable marine limestone overlaid with sandy soils — and sits atop a surficial aquifer that recovers to near land surface during the wet season. More than 60 percent of Cape Coral falls within a FEMA Special Flood Hazard Area. Florida slabs routinely measure 5 to 15 pounds of moisture emission per 1,000 square feet per 24 hours, far above the 3-pound threshold where epoxy begins to fail.

Humidity averages 72 percent annually, peaking near 79 percent from July through September. At those levels and temperatures, epoxy pot life shortens to roughly 15 minutes on a hot slab, and installers schedule applications for early morning before the garage heats up.

Salt air compounds the issue. Cape Coral is surrounded by water — the Caloosahatchee to the south, the canal network throughout, and Gulf access to the west. Salt-laden breezes settle on concrete continuously, accelerating corrosion on bare slabs and making a sealed surface practical in addition to cosmetic.

### Popular Applications in Cape Coral

City code requires every single-family home to include a garage with at least 14 by 20 feet of unobstructed space — carports are prohibited on detached properties. What goes into that garage explains the demand for durable floors.

Boats are the defining feature. Lee County has roughly 50,000 registered recreational vessels, and Cape Coral's 400-plus miles of navigable canals make it one of the most boat-dense communities in the state. Code requires boats and trailers stored in the rear yard or entirely inside a garage, so the floor takes regular contact with trailer tires, wet gear, and salt residue from anything pulled out of the water. A coated floor handles it and hoses clean in minutes.

Hurricane season adds a practical layer. Impact-rated garage doors (140 to 150 mph) create a protected staging area for shutters, generators, and fuel cans from June through November. Epoxy protects the concrete from fuel spills and standing water during tropical weather.

## What to Know Before Coating in Cape Coral

**Permits:** Cape Coral administers its own building permits through the Building Division. Residential epoxy coating over existing concrete is classified as cosmetic finish work and does not require a permit unless structural modifications or utilities are involved. Commercial projects with structural changes do require one. The city no longer issues local contractor licenses under Ordinance 10-23, consistent with Florida HB 735, but contractors must register through the EnerGov CSS portal and carry general liability insurance.

**Flood zone considerations:** Because more than 60 percent of Cape Coral is in a FEMA Special Flood Hazard Area, moisture beneath the slab is the default condition rather than the exception. Any contractor quoting a floor here should be running an ASTM F2170 probe test before giving a final price. If readings come back above 75 percent relative humidity — which they commonly do — a moisture vapor barrier is added before the base coat. Without one, trapped vapor creates osmotic blisters that can cause full system failure within 12 to 24 months.

**Salt air maintenance:** Canal-front floors collect salt residue faster than inland properties. A monthly rinse with a mild soap keeps the topcoat clear and prevents salt from dulling the surface. The coating itself resists salt corrosion, but the buildup can become cosmetically noticeable if left for months at a stretch.
