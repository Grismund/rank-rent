## Why Lehigh Acres Homeowners Choose Epoxy Flooring

### Climate and Concrete Conditions

Lehigh Acres sits on sandy soil over marine limestone with a water table that stays close to the surface year-round. That combination is the single most important variable for any floor coating in this area. Bare concrete wicks moisture upward through its pores, and in Southwest Florida 85 percent of epoxy flooring failures trace back to sub-surface moisture that was never tested for or addressed before coating.

Annual humidity averages 72 percent in Lehigh Acres, peaking at 77 percent in September. Summer peaks can push past 90 percent during afternoon thunderstorms in the June-through-September wet season. High humidity slows epoxy curing times and increases the risk of adhesion problems — professional installers in this area use 10- to 14-day curing windows with vapor-retarding membranes instead of the standard 7-day protocol that works in drier climates.

One advantage Lehigh Acres has over coastal neighbors: the community sits roughly 15 miles inland from the Gulf of Mexico. Salt air corrosion on concrete is less aggressive here than in Cape Coral or Fort Myers Beach, though the sandy substrate and ground moisture remain the primary challenge for any slab coating.

## What to Know Before Coating in Lehigh Acres

**Permits:** Lehigh Acres is unincorporated Lee County, so building permits go through Lee County Community Development rather than a city office. Most residential epoxy coating over existing concrete does not require a building permit. Commercial installations involving structural changes, floor drains, or fire-rating modifications may require one. Contractors must hold a State of Florida certification registered with Lee County or a Lee County Certificate of Competency, plus current liability and workers' compensation insurance.

**HOA and deed restrictions:** Many Lehigh Acres subdivisions have deed restrictions. Interior garage work rarely triggers architectural review, but check with your homeowners' association before scheduling, particularly in newer communities with active covenant enforcement.

**Moisture testing:** Given Lehigh Acres' sandy soil and high water table, an ASTM F2170 relative humidity probe test is essential before any coating goes down. Readings must be below 75 percent inside the slab. If the number comes back high, a moisture-mitigating primer is added to the system before the base coat — extra cost, but it prevents the floor from failing within a few years.
