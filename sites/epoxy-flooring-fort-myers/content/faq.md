The questions Fort Myers homeowners ask before coating a garage floor, answered plainly. This **epoxy flooring FAQ** covers lifespan, cost expectations, curing time, DIY versus professional installation, and what Southwest Florida humidity means for your floor.

*Answers draw from manufacturer specifications and trade sources. For your specific slab, talk to a local contractor.*

## Frequently Asked Questions About Epoxy Flooring

### How long does epoxy flooring last?

A professionally installed residential garage floor typically holds up for 10 to 20 years. The range is wide because the outcome depends on the quality of the concrete preparation, the coating system used, and how hard the floor works day to day. Commercial floors under heavy equipment may need a recoat in 5 to 10 years. If there is one factor to focus on, make it the surface prep — it determines more about longevity than any other variable.

### Can you epoxy a garage floor yourself?

You can. Kits are available at any home improvement store. Whether you will be satisfied with it after a few years is the real question, and in Fort Myers the answer is usually no. Retail kits go on at 2 to 3 mils compared to 10 to 20 for a professional system, and without grinding the slab first the bond is unreliable. Local contractors report that a significant share of their residential work involves removing a failed DIY application before starting over — costing more than doing it right the first time.

### How long does epoxy take to cure?

Standard epoxy: 24 to 72 hours before foot traffic, five to seven days before parking. Polyaspartic cures much faster — often full use within a day. Temperature and humidity affect those timelines, and Fort Myers summers push them in the wrong direction. A coating applied in August may need an extra day or two compared to one applied in January or February when humidity backs off.

### Is epoxy flooring slippery when wet?

A plain glossy epoxy surface can be slick, yes. Almost nobody installs one that way. Flake broadcast adds texture underfoot, and anti-slip grit can be incorporated into the topcoat wherever water is expected. Both solutions address the issue without sacrificing the finished appearance. If the garage regularly sees rain tracked in from the driveway, mention it to your contractor so they can specify accordingly.

### Does epoxy flooring crack?

Epoxy does not crack on its own. It cracks when the concrete beneath it moves, because the coating is rigid and follows the slab. In Fort Myers, sandy soils generally produce less slab movement than the expansive clays found in other parts of the country, but settling and minor cracking can still occur, especially in older construction. Filling existing cracks before coating and addressing any moisture issues reduces the risk. Normal foot and vehicle traffic will not cause cracking.

### Can epoxy be applied over old concrete?

Yes, and old concrete in good structural condition coats just as well as new. The same preparation applies: strip old paint and sealer, repair cracks and spalling, grind or shot-blast to an adequate profile, and pass a moisture test. Fort Myers slabs from the 1970s and 1980s that lack modern vapor barriers may need a moisture-mitigating primer, but the age of the concrete itself is not a disqualifying factor.

### What is the difference between epoxy and polyaspartic coatings?

Both are resin-based coatings for concrete. Epoxy costs less and cures slowly — 48 to 72 hours before foot traffic. Polyaspartic costs about 20 to 30 percent more, cures in roughly a day, and does not yellow in sunlight the way standard epoxy can. Many Fort Myers contractors install a hybrid system: an epoxy base for adhesion and price, with a polyaspartic topcoat for UV resistance and faster return to use. The [epoxy vs. polyaspartic guide](/guides/epoxy-vs-polyaspartic-garage-floors/) goes deeper.

### How do you maintain epoxy floors?

Minimally. Sweep or dust-mop to keep grit from scratching the surface. Mop with warm water and a mild, pH-neutral cleaner when it looks like it needs it. Avoid bleach and ammonia-based products — they dull the topcoat over time. Wipe up oil and chemical spills when you notice them. The coating resists penetration, but nothing benefits from sitting in a puddle of gasoline indefinitely. That covers the full routine.
