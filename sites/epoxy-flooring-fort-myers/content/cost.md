The short answer on **epoxy flooring cost in Fort Myers**: most two-car garages come in between $1,600 and $3,500 when a professional handles the work. Commercial floors are priced by the square foot and go up from there. The rest of this page explains what moves your number within those ranges.

These figures reflect what Fort Myers-area contractors are quoting in the current market. Your specific slab, finish choice, and the contractor you select will shift it.

*Last updated: November 2026. Costs vary by contractor and site conditions.*

## Average Epoxy Flooring Prices

### One-Car Garage

Roughly 250 square feet. Budget **$1,250 to $2,500**. The low end is a solid color or basic flake with standard prep. The high end means a metallic finish, significant crack repair, or a moisture-mitigating primer.

One thing about small floors: setup does not scale down proportionally. Bringing the grinder, running a moisture test, filling cracks — all of that takes nearly the same time on 250 square feet as on 450. So a one-car garage costs more per square foot than a two-car, even though the total is lower.

### Two-Car Garage

The typical Fort Myers residential job. Four hundred to five hundred square feet, usually running **$1,600 to $3,500**. That range covers a standard flake floor at the bottom through a layered metallic with a polyaspartic topcoat at the top.

Most homeowners go with flake and a clear topcoat. It is the practical middle ground: durable, forgiving of a slab with minor imperfections, and good-looking enough that the garage feels finished. Swapping the standard clear for a polyaspartic topcoat adds a few hundred dollars and buys you a floor you can drive on the following day instead of waiting nearly a week.

### Commercial or Large Area

Warehouses, shops, showrooms, kitchens — anything above about 1,000 square feet is priced by the foot, typically **$3 to $12 per square foot** depending on the system.

- **Solid or flake:** $3 to $7 per square foot
- **Metallic or decorative:** $5 to $12 per square foot
- **Heavy industrial:** quoted per project based on chemical exposure and traffic loads

Larger floors get cheaper per foot as they scale. The crew's mobilization and setup cost stays the same whether they coat 2,000 feet or 10,000. The [commercial epoxy flooring page](/commercial-epoxy-flooring/) covers what goes into a business floor.

## Factors That Influence Epoxy Flooring Cost

Five things, roughly in order of impact:

**Concrete condition.** Fort Myers homes sit on slab-on-grade foundations over sandy soil and a high water table. When the slab has moisture vapor rising through it — common here, even in new construction — the installer needs a vapor-mitigating primer before any coating goes down. Cracks, spalling, and old paint add to the prep time. The condition of your concrete is the single biggest reason two identical garages can receive different quotes.

**Square footage.** More floor means more material and labor, but less per square foot. See the one-car note above.

**Coating system.** Solid-color epoxy is the budget choice at about $3 to $7 per foot installed. Metallic runs $5 to $12 because the material costs more and the application requires more time and precision.

**Number of coats.** Primer, base, topcoat is the basic stack. Premium systems add extra base layers, decorative broadcasts between coats, or a second clear. Each additional layer is more material and another pass across the floor.

**Repairs.** Crack filling, patching spalled areas, and stripping a previous coating are line items on top of the coating itself. On older Fort Myers homes where the slab has settled over decades, that work can add several hundred dollars.

## Epoxy or Polyaspartic: Which Costs More?

Homeowners in Fort Myers end up choosing between two coating chemistries. They differ in price, curing speed, and how they hold up over time.

**Epoxy** is the less expensive option and accounts for the majority of residential floors installed in Southwest Florida. The price ranges above are epoxy ranges. The trade-off is curing time: 48 to 72 hours before foot traffic, five to seven days before parking.

**Polyaspartic** runs 20 to 30 percent higher — roughly $400 to $800 more on a two-car garage. In return you get a floor you can drive on in about a day and a clear coat that resists yellowing from the Florida sun.

Many contractors recommend a hybrid: an epoxy base coat for adhesion and cost control, topped with a polyaspartic clear for speed and UV stability. The detailed comparison is in the [epoxy vs. polyaspartic guide](/guides/epoxy-vs-polyaspartic-garage-floors/).
