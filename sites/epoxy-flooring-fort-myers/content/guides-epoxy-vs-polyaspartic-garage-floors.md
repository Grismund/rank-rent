Comparing **epoxy vs polyaspartic in Fort Myers**? If one contractor recommends epoxy while another recommends polyaspartic, you are in the right place. This is the comparison every homeowner hits eventually, and the two are more similar than most salespeople let on. Both bond to prepared concrete and produce a sealed, durable surface. Where they diverge is cure time, cost, UV behavior, and how they perform in Southwest Florida's heat and humidity.

This guide lays out the differences so you can make an informed choice before a contractor arrives.

*Last updated: November 2026. Based on manufacturer specifications and trade sources. For your specific project, talk to a local contractor.*

## Quick Summary

**Epoxy** has been the default garage floor coating for decades. Resin plus hardener, mixed on site, applied in layers. It is the most-installed system in the country for both residential and commercial applications.

**Polyaspartic** is a type of polyurea engineered to cure much faster. It entered the residential market over the last decade and is now widely available through Fort Myers contractors — either as a complete system or as a topcoat over an epoxy base.

One thing stays the same regardless of which chemistry you choose: the concrete preparation. Grinding or shot-blasting the slab, repairing cracks, running a moisture test, removing old coatings — all identical. The chemistry only starts to matter once the slab is ready to receive it.

## Key Differences

**Cure time.** This is the factor most homeowners care about. Epoxy requires 48 to 72 hours before you walk on it and five to seven days before you park. Polyaspartic takes foot traffic in 6 to 10 hours and a vehicle in about 24. If losing the garage for a week is a real problem, that difference alone may settle the question.

**Price.** Epoxy is cheaper. A two-car garage in Fort Myers typically runs $1,600 to $3,500 depending on size and finish. Polyaspartic adds 20 to 30 percent — roughly $400 to $800 extra on the same garage. The material costs more and the application is less forgiving of mistakes.

**UV stability.** Standard epoxy yellows over time when exposed to direct sunlight. If your garage door faces south or west and stands open during Fort Myers afternoons, you will notice it eventually. Polyaspartic does not yellow — the color you select at installation is the color you keep.

**Temperature tolerance on install day.** Epoxy prefers slab and air temperatures between roughly 50 and 90 degrees. Below that the reaction stalls; above it the material kicks too fast and can bubble or finish unevenly. Polyaspartic handles both extremes comfortably — down to around 20 degrees and well above 100 — giving the contractor more workable days on the calendar. In Fort Myers, where garage slab temperatures can exceed 90 degrees from May through October, that flexibility matters.

**Durability.** Both resist oil, gas, brake fluid, and household chemicals. In testing, polyaspartic edges ahead on abrasion resistance and flexibility, making it somewhat less prone to chipping from a dropped tool. In a home garage the practical gap is small. In a commercial shop with heavy equipment it can be meaningful.

**Appearance.** Solid colors, flake broadcast, and metallic finishes are available in both chemistries. The visual results are comparable. Metallic patterns may settle slightly differently between the two, but either one produces a floor people notice.

## How This Affects Your Decision

Fort Myers has a subtropical climate that shapes how both products behave. From May through October, daytime highs regularly push past 90, the garage slab stores heat well into the evening, and humidity stays elevated around the clock. That changes the calculation.

**Epoxy in summer** is workable, but it requires a contractor who respects the conditions. Crews typically start at dawn, getting material down before the slab heats up. Humidity stretches the cure — a garage that would be ready in 48 hours during a dry January week may need 72 hours or more in August. If your project falls between November and April, the slow cure is mostly an inconvenience rather than a risk.

**Polyaspartic in summer** is simpler. The wider temperature tolerance and rapid set mean the floor hardens before humidity has much time to interfere. For a mid-summer installation, that speed is a genuine advantage.

**Hybrid systems** are what many Fort Myers contractors recommend, and with good reason. An epoxy base coat for strong adhesion and lower cost, topped with a polyaspartic clear for UV stability and a one-day return to use. You pay less than a full polyaspartic system and get most of its advantages.

If you can give up the garage for a week and want to keep the budget tighter, epoxy is a proven floor that will perform well for years. If you need the space back quickly, or the garage door faces the afternoon sun, polyaspartic earns its premium. And whichever chemistry you pick, the preparation underneath it matters more than the product on top. Do not let a contractor skip it to hit a lower price.
