## What Is Commercial Epoxy Flooring?

Same basic chemistry as a residential garage floor — resin plus hardener — but engineered heavier and specified for the conditions. Commercial systems go down thicker, cure on controlled schedules, and can carry properties a homeowner never thinks about: anti-static for electronics, USDA-compliant finishes for food handling, chemical resistance matched to whatever your operation spills.

Thickness is the obvious distinction. A garage floor might run 10 to 20 mils. A commercial system lands between 20 and 60, sometimes more. That build is what lets it handle forklift loads and pallet traffic for years without wearing through to bare concrete at the loading dock.

## Why Choose Commercial Epoxy Flooring

What a commercial system solves:

- **Wear.** Hard wheels and heavy equipment grind bare concrete into dust. Epoxy absorbs the abuse.
- **Chemicals.** Oils, solvents, acids, degreasers stay on the surface where they can be cleaned.
- **Safety.** Anti-slip aggregate broadcast into the topcoat wherever floors get wet. Walkways, forklift lanes, and hazard zones striped directly into the coating rather than painted on top to peel later.
- **Maintenance.** No grout lines, no pores. Sweep, mop, done.
- **Appearance.** A brewery taproom or an auto showroom on US 41 wants a floor that looks finished. Solid colors, quartz blends, and [metallic finishes](/metallic-epoxy-flooring/) all work at commercial scale.

Which system fits depends entirely on what the floor has to survive. A contractor experienced in commercial work will spec it to your actual conditions, not sell you the same product they put in garages.

## Commercial Epoxy Flooring Installation Steps

The stages mirror residential work. The materials, scheduling, and scale do not. Most commercial jobs in Fort Myers are phased around business hours — sometimes overnight — so the operation stays open.

### Concrete Preparation

Preparation is the labor. Crews grind or shot-blast the entire slab to strip old coatings, adhesive residue, and whatever has soaked into the concrete over the years, leaving a profile the coating can grip.

On an older commercial slab, expect joint work. Expansion joints usually stay as joints rather than getting coated over; control joints get filled with a semi-rigid filler for a smooth, continuous coating surface. Then a moisture vapor test. Fort Myers commercial slabs, especially near the river or in older structures without modern vapor barriers, can read high. If the number comes back above threshold, the primer specification changes accordingly.

### Epoxy Application

Primer goes down first, often a moisture-mitigating version on slabs that test above the manufacturer's limit. The base coat follows in one or more passes depending on the target thickness.

Color zones, safety stripes, and line markings get built into the coating during application rather than added afterward. Anti-slip grit goes into the topcoat wherever water or grease will be a daily presence: kitchens, wash bays, loading docks, the area around a walk-in cooler.

### Curing and Final Coat

The final layer is usually polyurethane or polyaspartic, chosen for UV stability, tire-mark resistance, and abrasion performance. Light foot traffic is typically fine after about 24 hours. Full operational loads take five days or more. A contractor experienced in commercial work hands you a phased return-to-service schedule so you can open sections of the floor in order rather than shutting down entirely.
