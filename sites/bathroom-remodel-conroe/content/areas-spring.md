## Bathroom Remodeling Trends in Spring

### Home Ages and Common Bathroom Issues

Spring's neighborhoods create a clear pattern of remodel demand:

- **Champion Forest, Spring Creek Forest (1970s–1980s):** The oldest neighborhoods in Spring. Homes here frequently have original galvanized supply lines, cast-iron drains, and builder-grade bathrooms that are 40 to 50 years old. Full bathroom remodels — plumbing replacement included — are the norm, not the exception.
- **Gleannloch Farms, Klein-area subdivisions (1990s–2000s):** Homes in the 20 to 30 year range with bathrooms that are functionally adequate but dated. Grout failure, corroded fixtures, and worn tile drive most remodel decisions. Mid-range remodels are the sweet spot.
- **Imperial Oaks, Harmony (2010s+):** Modern construction with current plumbing and electrical standards. Remodels here are cosmetic upgrades — updated tile, premium fixtures, glass shower enclosures.

### Local Housing Market Trends

Spring's growth and proximity to both The Woodlands and Houston keep the housing market competitive. Updated bathrooms directly affect home value and time on market. Walk-in showers, double vanities, and modern tile are what buyers in this market expect.

## Permits and Inspections in Spring

Because Spring is unincorporated, the permit process depends on your property's county:

- **Harris County portion:** Permits issued through Harris County building department. Harris County has adopted the International Building Code (IBC) and International Residential Code (IRC).
- **Montgomery County portion:** Permits issued through Montgomery County. Standard bathroom remodel permits with complete documentation are typically approved in 3 to 7 business days in both counties.

In both jurisdictions, bathroom remodels require a permit when plumbing is relocated, new drain lines are added, or electrical circuits are changed. Cosmetic-only work does not require a permit. The contractor confirms which county governs your property and handles the application.

Permitted remodels in Spring require: a rough plumbing inspection before walls are closed, a shower pan/waterproofing inspection before tile is installed, a rough electrical inspection, and a final inspection after all fixtures are installed. Inspections are typically scheduled within 24 to 48 hours of request during active construction.

All plumbing contractors must hold a current license from the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners (TSBPE). All bathroom outlets require GFCI protection per National Electrical Code standards. Exhaust fans must provide a minimum of 1 CFM per square foot.

## Bathroom Design Trends in Spring

Spring homeowners gravitate toward updates that address both aesthetics and the practical realities of this climate:

- **Walk-in showers** replacing tub-shower combos in master baths. The [tub-to-shower conversion](/tub-to-shower-conversion/) is a consistent top request.
- **Large-format porcelain tile** on floors and shower walls. Fewer grout lines means less maintenance in hard water conditions (Spring-area municipal hardness runs 4 to 9 grains per gallon).
- **Double vanities** with quartz countertops. Quartz resists water stains, does not need sealing, and handles the daily moisture of a bathroom.
- **Improved ventilation** to combat Southeast Texas humidity. Upgrading to a properly sized exhaust fan during a remodel prevents long-term moisture damage.
- **Comfort-height toilets and grab-bar blocking** in walls for aging-in-place preparation — increasingly common in the established Champion Forest and Spring Creek Forest neighborhoods.
