## What Is Tub-to-Shower Conversion?

A tub-to-shower conversion removes the bathtub (and usually the surround), modifies the plumbing to accommodate a shower drain and valve placement, waterproofs the area, and installs a walk-in shower in the same footprint. The conversion typically includes:

- Removing the bathtub and surround
- Rerouting the drain from tub configuration to shower configuration
- Repositioning supply lines and the shower valve
- Installing a shower pan (mortar bed, prefabricated, or linear drain)
- Waterproofing the walls and floor
- Tiling the walls and floor
- Installing a glass enclosure or panel
- Adding a showerhead, valve trim, and accessories (niche, bench, grab bars)

In Conroe, any project that reroutes plumbing requires a building permit. The contractor obtains the permit, schedules inspections, and manages the process.

## The Tub-to-Shower Conversion Process

### Planning and Design

The contractor visits the bathroom, measures the tub alcove, inspects the existing plumbing, and discusses your preferences for the new shower. Key decisions include: curbed or curbless threshold, tile material, glass enclosure type, showerhead configuration, and whether to include a bench or niches.

The existing tub drain and the shower drain are in different locations within the same footprint. This plumbing change is what makes a conversion more involved than a simple fixture swap, and it is why a permit is required.

### Demolition and Rough-In

The tub comes out along with the surround, backer board, and any damaged drywall or framing behind it. In Conroe's humid climate, it is common to find moisture damage behind a tub surround that has been in place for 15 to 20 years — especially where caulk has failed along the edges. Damaged framing and subfloor get replaced.

The plumber reroutes the drain to the shower position, repositions the supply lines and valve, and installs the shower rough-in. A rough plumbing inspection is required before closing the walls.

### Finishing and Inspection

The waterproofing membrane covers the shower pan, walls, and seams. Backer board goes up, followed by tile. The glass enclosure is installed — typically after tile is complete and cured. Fixtures, trim, and accessories finish the job. A final inspection covers plumbing, waterproofing, and electrical (the ventilation fan).

Most tub-to-shower conversions in Conroe take one to three weeks, depending on the tile complexity and whether the glass enclosure is stock or custom-ordered.

## Choices for Your Tub-to-Shower Conversion

- **Threshold:** A standard curb (3 to 4 inches) keeps water contained and is the most common. A curbless (zero-threshold) design provides wheelchair and walker access and gives the room a modern, open look — but requires careful slope engineering so water drains properly.
- **Tile:** Large-format porcelain is the most practical choice for shower walls in this climate. Fewer grout lines mean less maintenance against hard water and humidity. Mosaic tile works well on shower floors for traction.
- **Glass:** Frameless glass door or panel ($800 to $2,000+), semi-frameless ($400 to $1,000), or a simple shower curtain for the most budget-conscious conversion.
- **Showerhead:** Fixed, handheld, rain, or combination. A handheld on a slide bar is the most versatile option and the standard accessibility recommendation.
- **Bench and niches:** A built-in tile bench adds function and a safety element. Recessed niches keep the floor clear of bottles.

## Tub-to-Shower Conversion Cost in Conroe

Tub-to-shower conversions in the Conroe area typically range from **$3,000 to $8,000**. A basic conversion with stock tile, a standard curb, and a framed glass door falls at the low end. A custom-tiled curbless shower with a frameless glass enclosure, rain head, and built-in bench is at the high end.

The plumbing modification — rerouting the drain and supply lines — is a fixed cost regardless of finish level, typically $500 to $1,500 of the total. The rest varies with tile, glass, and fixture choices.
