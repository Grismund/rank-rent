### Home Ages and Common Bathroom Issues

Shenandoah's housing stock falls into two main categories:

- **Tuscany Woods, Cantwell Forest, and established neighborhoods (1990s–2000s):** These are Shenandoah's original residential areas. Plumbing systems are approaching 25 to 30 years of age — the point where faucet cartridges fail, supply line fittings corrode, and builder-grade tile grout has broken down. Bathrooms in these homes are prime candidates for mid-range remodels that update fixtures, retile the shower, and replace the vanity.
- **Harper's Reserve and newer construction (2010s+):** Modern homes with PEX plumbing, energy-efficient fixtures, and current building codes. Remodels here are aesthetic — upgraded tile, frameless glass, and premium fixtures — rather than infrastructure-driven.

Both categories share the hard water challenge. After 20 to 30 years of mineral-rich water, even well-maintained fixtures show corrosion and buildup that cleaning cannot resolve. Replacement is the practical solution.

## Permits and Inspections in Shenandoah

The City of Shenandoah requires a permit for remodeling work that goes beyond cosmetic changes. Projects considered cosmetic — new wallpaper, carpet, paint, cabinets, or trim — do not require a permit. Plumbing relocation, electrical modifications, and structural changes do.

Shenandoah has specific requirements that differ from neighboring jurisdictions. Building drawings for residential remodels must be engineered and stamped and signed by a Texas-licensed engineer. Plans must be submitted digitally in PDF format with a minimum plan size of 18 by 24 inches and must include a property survey showing property lines, setbacks, and easements. The building department's target for permit plan review is 10 business days, with 10 additional days for revisions.

One detail worth knowing: the City of Shenandoah accepts cash or check only for permit payments on building projects.

For properties in the Grogan's Forest section of Shenandoah, there is a dual-approval requirement: both the City of Shenandoah and The Woodlands Township covenant administration must approve the work. The contractor handles both submissions.

All plumbing contractors must hold a current Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners (TSBPE) license. Permitted remodels require rough plumbing and final inspections. All bathroom outlets require GFCI protection.

## Bathroom Design Trends in Shenandoah

Shenandoah homeowners share design preferences with the broader Montgomery County market, with some distinctions driven by the community's newer housing stock:

- **Walk-in showers** with frameless glass. The [shower remodel](/shower-remodel/) is the most common project in Shenandoah master baths.
- **Porcelain tile** in large formats for both floors and shower walls. The low porosity resists mineral deposits from the local hard water.
- **Quartz countertops** on vanities. Non-porous, no sealing required, and resistant to water staining — a practical choice for this area.
- **Modern vanity styles** with clean lines and integrated storage, reflecting the newer-construction aesthetic of much of Shenandoah's housing stock.
- **Low-flow fixtures** as mandated by 2025 plumbing standards. Dual-flush toilets and WaterSense-rated showerheads are the standard for new installations.
