Contractors serving Willis routinely specify low-porosity tile, commercial-grade faucet cartridges, and finishes that resist mineral buildup.

## Bathroom Remodeling Trends in Willis

### Home Ages and Common Bathroom Issues

Willis's housing stock splits into three distinct categories:

- **Lakefront and Panorama Village (1970s–1990s):** Established neighborhoods around Lake Conroe and the Panorama Village golf course community have homes dating to the 1970s and 1980s. These properties often have original plumbing — galvanized supply lines and cast-iron drains — that needs replacement during any significant remodel. Bathroom fixtures from this era are dated, and decades of hard water have taken a toll on pipes and fittings.
- **Established Willis neighborhoods (1990s–2010s):** Homes in the 15 to 30 year range with builder-grade bathrooms that are showing wear. Grout failure, corroded faucets, and outdated layouts are the common drivers for remodeling.
- **The Woodlands Hills and new construction (2018+):** Modern homes with PEX plumbing, energy-efficient fixtures, and contemporary floor plans. Remodels here focus on aesthetic upgrades rather than structural issues.

## Permits and Inspections in Willis

The City of Willis requires a building permit for most construction and remodeling work. Permits must be obtained before work begins. Building permit fees are based on the valuation of proposed work, and trade permits (electrical, mechanical, plumbing) are based on the number of required inspections plus a base fee.

Willis has adopted the 2023 National Electrical Code and the 2024 International Code Council family of codes with no amendments. Construction documents for remodels must be submitted digitally in PDF format. Once a complete application is submitted, plan reviews are generally conducted within 10 working days.

Cosmetic work — new paint, carpet, cabinets, or trim — does not require a permit. Plumbing relocation, new drain lines, and electrical circuit changes do.

All plumbing contractors must hold a current license from the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners (TSBPE). Permitted remodels require a rough plumbing inspection before walls are closed and a final inspection after all fixtures are installed. Shower pan and waterproofing inspections are required before tile installation.
