**How long does a bathroom remodel take?** One to six weeks under normal conditions, depending on scope. A cosmetic refresh wraps up in about a week. A mid-range remodel runs two to three weeks. A full gut-and-rebuild with plumbing and electrical changes takes three to six weeks. The wide range exists because "bathroom remodel" covers everything from a vanity swap to a complete room reconstruction.

This guide breaks down the timeline by project scope, explains what extends or shortens it, and covers how Conroe-specific factors like permit scheduling and climate affect the schedule.

*Last updated: November 2026. Based on typical Conroe-area contractor timelines. Your specific project may vary.*

## Overview

The timeline for a bathroom remodel is driven by three things: how much demolition is involved, whether plumbing or electrical changes require permits and inspections, and how long the finishing work (tile, fixtures, glass) takes to install.

Here is the quick breakdown:

| Scope | Typical timeline | Permit needed? |
|-------|-----------------|----------------|
| Budget refresh | 1 week | Usually no |
| Mid-range remodel | 2–3 weeks | Often yes |
| Full remodel | 3–6 weeks | Yes |
| Tub-to-shower conversion | 1–3 weeks | Yes |
| Bathtub replacement (same location) | 2–4 days | Usually no |
| Shower retile | 3–5 days | Usually no |

These timelines cover active construction. They do not include the design and planning phase before work begins, which can take one to four weeks depending on how quickly decisions are made and materials are ordered.

## How They Compare

**Budget refresh (1 week).** New vanity, toilet, faucet, mirror, light fixtures, and paint. No demo beyond removing the old fixtures. No plumbing relocation. No tile work on walls (floor tile may be included). This is the fastest bathroom project because there is no waiting for inspections, no waterproofing cure time, and no tile setting on vertical surfaces. A skilled crew can complete it in three to five working days.

**Mid-range remodel (2 to 3 weeks).** New tile on the floor and in the shower, new vanity with countertop, updated fixtures, possibly a new shower valve. This scope involves demolition (removing old tile and backer board), waterproofing, tile setting, and grouting — each of which requires drying or curing time before the next step.

The timeline breaks down roughly like this:
- Demo and inspection for water damage: 1–2 days
- Plumbing and electrical rough-in: 1–2 days
- Rough inspection (if permit required): scheduling adds 1–2 days
- Waterproofing membrane application and cure: 1 day
- Shower pan flood test: 1 day
- Backer board and tile setting: 3–5 days
- Grout cure: 1 day
- Fixture installation, glass, trim: 2–3 days
- Final inspection: 1 day

**Full remodel (3 to 6 weeks).** Everything comes out to the studs. The room is rebuilt with new framing repairs (if water damage is found), new plumbing rough-in, new electrical, waterproofing, tile, fixtures, and paint. The extended timeline comes from two factors: more demolition and repair work at the front end, and mandatory inspections at multiple stages.

A full remodel in Conroe requires at minimum: a rough plumbing inspection (before walls close), a rough electrical inspection, a shower pan flood test, and a final inspection. Each inspection must be scheduled with the City of Conroe building department, and the contractor cannot proceed until each one passes.

**Tub-to-shower conversion (1 to 3 weeks).** The plumbing modification (rerouting the drain from tub to shower position) requires a permit and inspections, which adds scheduling time. The tile work and glass installation take longer than a simple fixture swap. A basic conversion with stock tile and a standard glass door finishes in about one week of active work plus inspection days. A custom-tiled conversion with frameless glass and a bench takes two to three weeks.

## Practical Takeaways

**What extends the timeline:**

- **Material lead times.** Custom glass enclosures can take two to four weeks from order to delivery. Specialty tile may need to be ordered. The contractor can begin demolition and rough-in while waiting for materials, but finishing stalls if the glass or tile has not arrived.
- **Permit inspection scheduling.** In Conroe, inspections are typically scheduled within 24 to 48 hours of request, but delays during busy periods can add days at each inspection point.
- **Hidden water damage.** Finding rot behind the shower walls or under the subfloor adds demolition and repair time. This is common in Conroe homes with 15 to 20 years of hard water and humidity exposure. A contractor cannot give a firm timeline until demo reveals the actual condition behind the walls.
- **Humidity and drying.** Conroe's humidity — 73 to 77 percent year-round — slows cure times for waterproofing membranes, thin-set mortar, and grout. Contractors account for this in their schedules, but unusually wet weeks can extend drying periods.

**What shortens the timeline:**

- **Keeping the existing layout.** No plumbing relocation means no permit, no rough inspection, and significantly less labor. This is the single biggest time saver.
- **Choosing stock materials.** Stock tile, stock vanities, and standard glass enclosures are available immediately. No waiting for custom orders.
- **Separating the project into phases.** A vanity and toilet swap now, shower remodel later. Each phase is shorter and less disruptive than doing everything at once.

**How to plan around it:** Most contractors recommend scheduling bathroom remodels during drier months (October through March in the Conroe area) when humidity is lower and drying times are shorter. If you have only one bathroom, the contractor can typically keep the toilet functional throughout the project, but the shower will be out of service during the tile and waterproofing phase — usually four to seven days for a mid-range remodel.
