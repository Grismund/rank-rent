## What Is Bathtub Replacement?

Bathtub replacement removes the existing tub and installs a new one. The scope ranges from a simple swap — same location, same size, same plumbing connections — to a project that involves modifying the alcove framing, rerouting drain and supply lines, and retiling the surround.

Common bathtub replacement projects in the Conroe area include:

- **Alcove tub swap:** Removing an old builder-grade tub and installing a new one in the same footprint. The simplest and least expensive option.
- **Freestanding tub installation:** Removing the alcove tub and installing a freestanding soaking tub. Requires relocating the drain and supply lines to match the new tub position.
- **Walk-in tub installation:** Replacing a standard tub with a walk-in model that has a low-threshold door, built-in seat, and grab bars. Designed for accessibility and aging in place.
- **Jetted or whirlpool tub:** Requires a dedicated electrical circuit for the pump. Less common in Conroe's newer homes but still requested for master bath upgrades.

## The Bathtub Replacement Process

### Planning and Design

The contractor measures the existing alcove or space, inspects the plumbing connections, and discusses your preferences. Key decisions include tub type (alcove, freestanding, walk-in), material (acrylic, fiberglass, cast iron, solid surface), and whether the surrounding walls need retiling. If plumbing changes are needed, the contractor pulls a permit from the City of Conroe.

### Demolition and Rough-In

The old tub comes out. For an alcove tub, this means disconnecting the drain and overflow, removing caulk and any tile or surround panels, and lifting the tub out. Some older Conroe homes have cast-iron tubs that weigh 300 to 400 pounds — removing them may require cutting them into pieces.

If the new tub requires different drain placement or supply line locations, the plumber handles the rough-in. For freestanding tubs, the floor drain often moves to the center of the room. The subfloor gets inspected and repaired if moisture has caused damage underneath the old tub.

### Finishing and Inspection

The new tub gets set, leveled, and connected. The surround — whether tile, solid surface panels, or acrylic — goes up around it. Caulk, trim, and fixtures complete the installation. If a permit was pulled, the final plumbing inspection follows.

A straightforward alcove swap takes two to four days. A freestanding tub installation with plumbing relocation takes one to two weeks. Walk-in tub installations, which may involve framing modifications for the wider door opening, also take one to two weeks.

## Choices for Your Bathtub Replacement

- **Acrylic:** Lightweight, warm to the touch, available in many shapes and sizes. The most common choice for alcove and freestanding tubs. Resists staining and is easy to clean.
- **Fiberglass:** The most affordable option. Lighter than acrylic but less durable and more prone to surface wear over time.
- **Cast iron with enamel:** Heavy, durable, and excellent at retaining heat. The classic choice, but weight can be a concern in some installations — a full cast-iron tub can weigh 400 pounds or more.
- **Solid surface (Corian, cultured marble):** Seamless, non-porous, and available in custom colors. Mid to high price range.

For accessibility, walk-in tubs include features like a low-step entry door, built-in grab bars, a contoured seat, and anti-slip flooring. Some models include hydrotherapy jets or heated surfaces. These are the most expensive option but address real mobility concerns.
