### Year-Round Swimming Climate

East Stuart offers historic character and deep local roots with more modest home values, where pool construction adds significant outdoor living value to properties built well before pools were standard.

## Pool Permit Requirements in Stuart

Pool construction in Stuart requires a building permit under the Florida Building Code 8th Edition. The key distinction is the permitting authority:

- **Inside Stuart city limits:** Permits from the City of Stuart Building Department
- **Unincorporated Martin County:** Permits from the Martin County Building Department at 900 SE Ruhnke Street, Stuart, FL 34994, phone (772) 288-5916

Requirements for both jurisdictions:

- Pool plans signed and sealed by a Florida-licensed architect or engineer
- Barrier/fence documentation and site plan required
- Electrical, plumbing, and gas sub-permits required separately
- Separate structural permit for screen enclosures
- Pool permit fees typically range from $200 to $900; Martin County fees are calculated from the value of work under the county's adopted fee schedule
- Plan review typically takes 2 to 6 weeks

For waterfront properties in subdivisions approved after April 1982, pools must be set back at least 5 feet from the Shoreline Protection Zone or wetland buffer. This applies to many Stuart properties along the St. Lucie River and Indian River Lagoon.

The Treasure Coast's high water table means contractors typically hit groundwater at 4 to 6 feet below grade in Stuart's coastal areas. Standard mitigation includes dewatering, French drain installation, gravel systems, and hydrostatic relief valves. Some builders raise the pool's elevation with fill dirt to lift the shell above the high-water mark.

Florida Statute 515 barrier requirements apply the same as in St. Lucie County. Martin County building inspectors administer barrier inspections locally, and field interpretations may vary from other counties even though the state framework controls basic requirements.

## Popular Pool Styles in Stuart

Stuart's waterfront character and varied neighborhoods drive diverse pool preferences:

- **Waterfront homes (North River Shores, Sailfish Point):** Custom gunite pools with spas, water features, and vanishing-edge designs that complement the view. Premium pebble or tile finishes are standard.
- **Standard residential neighborhoods:** Fiberglass and gunite pools in the 12-by-24 to 14-by-28 range with integrated screen enclosures and cool-deck or paver decking.
- **Downtown and East Stuart:** Compact pools, geometric designs, and plunge pools on smaller lots. Fiberglass is popular for these projects because the faster install time minimizes disruption.
- **HOA communities:** Pool designs must conform to ARC guidelines, which may specify fence heights (often 6 feet minimum), setbacks from property lines, and equipment placement.

Saltwater systems and energy-efficient variable-speed pumps are standard upgrades across Stuart. Pool heaters are common for year-round use.
