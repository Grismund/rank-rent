The Treasure Coast's sandy soil over limestone and a water table that typically sits 3 to 12 feet below grade create conditions that affect every pool build. Dewatering during excavation is standard. These are not optional add-ons — they are baseline requirements for a pool that holds up in this environment.

## Local Pool Contractors in Port St. Lucie

### Why Build a Pool?

A pool in Port St. Lucie serves as a year-round outdoor living space. The subtropical climate makes unheated pools comfortable from March through November, and a heater extends that to twelve months. For families, a pool replaces gym memberships and weekend trip costs. For homeowners planning to sell, a well-built pool with a screen enclosure is an expected feature in this market, not a novelty.

The investment is substantial. New pool construction on the Treasure Coast ranges from $25,000 for a basic vinyl liner pool to $65,000 or more for a fully customized gunite pool with spa, screen enclosure, and premium decking. Understanding the differences between pool types, the permit process, and the realistic timeline helps you make an informed decision before signing a contract.

### Pool Types We Build

Three construction methods dominate the Treasure Coast market:

- **Gunite (concrete)** pools offer unlimited shape and depth customization. They are the most expensive option ($35,000 to $65,000) and take the longest to build (8 to 16 weeks), but they are built entirely on site and can be designed to fit any yard.
- **Fiberglass** pools are factory-molded shells installed in a pre-excavated hole. They cost $25,000 to $55,000, install in 3 to 6 weeks, and require less long-term surface maintenance than concrete.
- **Vinyl liner** pools are the lowest-cost entry point ($25,000 to $45,000) with a 4 to 8 week build time. The liner will need replacement every 7 to 12 years, which is a recurring cost to factor in.

Each type works in Treasure Coast soil conditions when properly engineered. The [pool type comparison guide](/guides/gunite-vs-fiberglass-vs-vinyl-pools/) breaks down the trade-offs in detail.

### Step 2: Design and Permits

The contractor evaluates your yard — setbacks, water table depth, existing utilities, HOA requirements — and designs the pool to fit. If your property is in an HOA, the contractor can prepare the Architectural Review Committee (ARC) application materials. Pool construction in St. Lucie County requires a building permit under the Florida Building Code 8th Edition, with signed and sealed plans from a Florida-licensed engineer. The contractor handles the permit application, which includes electrical, plumbing, and structural sub-permits. Permit fees run approximately $461 for the pool plus $180 for a screen enclosure.

### Step 3: Construction and Completion

Excavation, dewatering (standard on most Treasure Coast builds), shell construction, plumbing, electrical, decking, screen enclosure, and barrier installation. The timeline depends on pool type: 3 to 6 weeks for fiberglass, 4 to 8 weeks for vinyl, and 8 to 16 weeks for gunite. Florida Statute 515 requires a compliant safety barrier — minimum 48-inch fence with self-closing, self-latching gates — before the pool passes final inspection.
