## What Is a Pool Screen Enclosure?

A pool screen enclosure is an aluminum-framed, screen-paneled structure built over the pool and surrounding deck area. In Florida, screen enclosures serve multiple practical functions beyond comfort. They reduce leaf and debris accumulation in the water, which cuts down on filter maintenance and chemical use. They block direct sun, lowering water temperature in summer and reducing UV degradation of pool surfaces. And they provide a physical barrier around the pool area.

Under Florida Statute 515, every residential pool requires a compliant safety barrier. A properly constructed screen enclosure can serve as that barrier if it meets the statutory requirements: at least 48 inches high on the exterior side, self-closing and self-latching doors with the latch at least 54 inches above grade, and no gaps larger than 4 inches. Many Treasure Coast homeowners use the screen enclosure as their primary pool barrier rather than installing a separate fence inside the enclosure.

## Screen Enclosure Materials and Styles

**Frame materials** are almost exclusively aluminum on the Treasure Coast. Aluminum resists corrosion in the salt-air environment, requires no painting, and meets Florida Building Code wind load requirements. Frame profiles come in standard (2 by 4 inch) and heavy-duty (2 by 6 inch) options. Heavier frames cost more but withstand higher wind loads and span wider openings without intermediate supports.

**Screen types** include:

- **Standard fiberglass mesh (18x14):** The most common and affordable option. It blocks insects and allows good airflow but provides minimal UV reduction.
- **Super screen (20x20 mesh):** Finer weave blocks smaller insects like no-see-ums, which are common on the Treasure Coast. Slightly reduced airflow.
- **Solar screen (80% or 90% shade):** Blocks significant UV and reduces heat inside the enclosure. Popular for enclosures that cover a patio or outdoor living area in addition to the pool.
- **Pet-resistant screen:** Heavier-gauge mesh that resists tearing from dogs and cats. Typically installed on the lower panels where animals make contact.

**Roof options** include standard peaked (gable), hip, mansard, and dome styles. The roof style affects wind resistance, interior volume, and cost. Gable and hip roofs are the most common on residential pool enclosures in St. Lucie County.

## Pool Screen Enclosure Step by Step

### Design and Permits

The contractor measures the pool area and surrounding deck, assesses the existing pool deck for anchor points, and designs the enclosure to match the home's roofline and the homeowner's coverage preferences. Enclosures can cover just the pool and deck or extend to include an outdoor kitchen, patio, or living area.

A structural permit is required from St. Lucie County for all screen enclosures. The permit fee is approximately $180. Plans must meet Florida Building Code 8th Edition wind load requirements. If you are in an HOA, the contractor can prepare the ARC application, which typically requires elevation drawings and material specifications.

### Frame Installation

Aluminum posts are anchored into the pool deck or surrounding concrete using base plates and concrete anchors. The frame is assembled in sections — posts, beams, rafters, and purlins — and raised into position. The frame must be structurally complete and inspected before screening begins. On most residential enclosures, frame installation takes 1 to 3 days.

### Screening and Inspection

Screen panels are rolled onto the frame and secured with spline in channels along each frame member. Doors are hung with self-closing hinges and self-latching hardware that meets Florida Statute 515 requirements. The building department conducts a structural inspection of the frame and a final inspection of the completed enclosure. Total project timeline from start to final inspection is typically 1 to 2 weeks.
