## What Is Pool Remodeling?

Pool remodeling covers any significant renovation to an existing inground pool. On the Treasure Coast, the most common reasons for remodeling are surface deterioration, outdated tile or coping, aging equipment, and changing how the pool area functions. Florida's hard water, UV exposure, and humid subtropical climate accelerate surface wear. A plaster finish that lasts 10 to 15 years in a drier climate may need attention in 7 to 10 years here.

Remodeling projects range from a focused resurface job to a full redesign that adds features, changes the deck layout, or upgrades the pool to a saltwater system. Most projects fall between $5,000 and $20,000 depending on scope.

## Pool Remodeling Materials and Styles

The scope of a pool remodel depends on what you are changing:

**Resurfacing** is the most common remodel project. Options include standard white plaster ($3,000 to $5,000), quartz aggregate ($5,000 to $8,000), and pebble aggregate ($6,000 to $10,000). Pebble finishes are popular on the Treasure Coast because they hide mineral staining and last longer in hard water conditions.

**Tile and coping replacement** addresses the waterline tile and the coping stones around the pool edge. Cracked or popped tile is common in older pools, especially where the pool shell has shifted slightly from soil movement. Replacement runs $2,000 to $5,000 depending on material.

**Equipment upgrades** include replacing an aging pump with a variable-speed unit (now required by federal efficiency standards for replacement pumps), upgrading to a saltwater chlorine generator ($1,000 to $3,000), adding a pool heater ($2,000 to $5,000), or installing LED lighting.

**Design changes** are the most extensive remodel scope. Adding a spa, sun shelf, water features, or reshaping the pool deck can transform an older pool into a modern outdoor living space. These projects approach $15,000 to $20,000 depending on complexity.

## Pool Remodeling Step by Step

### Assessment and Design

The contractor inspects the existing pool structure, surface condition, plumbing, electrical, and equipment. For a resurface, the assessment determines whether the existing shell is structurally sound or needs repair before new material goes on. For a design change, the contractor evaluates how the existing plumbing and electrical can accommodate the new features.

If the remodel involves structural changes, new plumbing, or electrical modifications, a permit is required from St. Lucie County under the Florida Building Code. Cosmetic-only work — resurfacing with the same shell dimensions, replacing tile, or swapping equipment — typically does not require a permit.

### Surface Removal and Prep

For a resurface, the existing plaster or aggregate is removed using a combination of chipping and sandblasting. The exposed shell is inspected for cracks, delamination, or structural issues. Repairs are made before the new surface is applied. The pool is drained for this work, and on the Treasure Coast, timing matters: an empty pool in an area with a high water table is at risk of lifting if the ground saturates from heavy rain. Contractors schedule drain-downs around weather conditions and work quickly through the exposed-shell phase.

### Finishing and Startup

The new surface is applied, cured according to the manufacturer's specifications, and the pool is filled. Equipment is reconnected or replaced, chemical balancing begins, and the contractor walks you through startup procedures. For a simple resurface, the project takes 1 to 2 weeks. For a full redesign with structural changes, expect 4 to 8 weeks.
