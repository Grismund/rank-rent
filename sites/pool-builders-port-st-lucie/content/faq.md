These are the most common questions homeowners ask about **pool builders FAQ** topics in the Port St. Lucie and Treasure Coast area. Each answer covers the Florida-specific requirements and local conditions that affect pool construction in this market.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Pool Builders

### Do I need a permit to build a pool in Port St. Lucie?

Yes. A building permit is required for any in-ground or above-ground swimming pool in St. Lucie County under the Florida Building Code 8th Edition. Plans must be signed and sealed by a Florida-licensed architect or engineer. Electrical, plumbing, and structural sub-permits are required separately. Screen enclosures require their own structural permit. Permit fees run approximately $461 for the pool and $180 for the enclosure.

Prefabricated above-ground pools under 24 inches deep and under 5,000 gallons are exempt from permitting. Everything else needs a permit before work begins.

### What are the pool fence requirements in Florida?

Florida Statute 515 (the Residential Swimming Pool Safety Act) requires every residential pool or spa to have a safety barrier. The barrier must be at least 48 inches high on the exterior side. All gates must be self-closing and self-latching, swinging away from the pool, with the latch release at least 54 inches above grade. No gaps larger than 4 inches between barrier components, and no more than 2 inches between the fence and the ground. The exterior face must be non-climbable.

A screen enclosure can serve as the safety barrier if it meets all of these requirements. For more details, see the [pool screen enclosure](/pool-screen-enclosure/) page.

### How long does it take to build a pool in Port St. Lucie?

It depends on pool type. Fiberglass pools install in 3 to 6 weeks after the permit is issued. Vinyl liner pools take 4 to 8 weeks. Gunite (concrete) pools take 8 to 16 weeks. Before construction starts, permit plan review adds 2 to 6 weeks depending on project complexity and the jurisdiction.

Rainy season (June through September) can add delays — both for excavation scheduling and for curing concrete on gunite pools. The [build timeline guide](/guides/how-long-does-it-take-to-build-a-pool/) covers each phase in detail.

### How much does a new pool cost in Port St. Lucie?

Most projects fall between $25,000 and $65,000 for the base pool. Vinyl liner pools start at $25,000. Fiberglass pools range from $25,000 to $55,000. Gunite pools range from $35,000 to $65,000. Screen enclosures ($5,000 to $15,000), heaters ($2,000 to $5,000), saltwater systems ($1,000 to $3,000), and deck upgrades add to the base price.

The [cost guide](/cost/) breaks down each range. The [pool cost calculator](/pool-cost-calculator/) gives a quick estimate.

### Does my HOA need to approve pool construction?

If your property is in an HOA, you almost certainly need written Architectural Review Committee (ARC) approval before construction begins. Verbal approvals from individual board members are not binding. HOAs frequently enforce setback rules, fencing heights (often 6 feet minimum, exceeding the state's 4-foot minimum), and noise limits for pool equipment that go beyond what the building code requires.

Review your CC&Rs and submit the ARC application before signing a construction contract. The contractor can prepare the required drawings and material specifications for the ARC submittal.

### What is the high water table and how does it affect pool construction?

The water table in Port St. Lucie and surrounding Treasure Coast areas typically sits 3 to 12 feet below the surface. Sandy soil over limestone makes the water table variable — it rises after sustained rainfall and during wet season. When pool excavation hits groundwater, dewatering equipment pumps the water out and keeps the hole dry during construction.

Hydrostatic relief valves are installed in the pool floor to prevent the shell from floating if the water table rises after the pool is built. French drains and gravel beds around the pool shell are standard. These are routine procedures for Treasure Coast pool contractors, not unusual complications.

### Do I need a screen enclosure for my pool?

Florida does not legally mandate a screen enclosure specifically, but it does require a compliant safety barrier around every residential pool. A screen enclosure can serve as that barrier if it meets all Florida Statute 515 requirements. Practically, screen enclosures are near-universal on Treasure Coast pools because they keep out mosquitoes and no-see-ums, reduce leaf and debris in the water, lower chemical maintenance, and provide UV protection.

Most homeowners include a screen enclosure in the initial pool construction because it is more cost-effective to build it as part of the original project than to add it later. See the [pool screen enclosure](/pool-screen-enclosure/) page for details and pricing.

### What is the best pool type for Florida?

All three construction types — gunite, fiberglass, and vinyl liner — work well in Florida when properly engineered for the local water table. Gunite offers unlimited design flexibility and is the most popular choice for custom pools. Fiberglass has the fastest install time and lowest long-term maintenance. Vinyl has the lowest upfront cost but requires liner replacement every 7 to 12 years.

The best choice depends on your budget, design preferences, timeline, and how you prioritize upfront cost versus long-term maintenance. The [gunite vs. fiberglass vs. vinyl guide](/guides/gunite-vs-fiberglass-vs-vinyl-pools/) covers the full comparison.
