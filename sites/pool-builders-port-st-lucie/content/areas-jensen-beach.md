## What Drives Pool Demand in Jensen Beach

### Year-Round Swimming Climate

Jensen Beach has a tropical monsoon climate with temperatures ranging from 55 to 90 degrees year-round. August is the hottest month with average highs of 90 degrees and lows of 76 degrees. ## Pool Permit Requirements in Jensen Beach

Because Jensen Beach is unincorporated Martin County, all pool permits are processed through the Martin County Building Department at 900 SE Ruhnke Street, Stuart, FL 34994, phone (772) 288-5916. Requirements under the Florida Building Code 8th Edition:

- Pool plans signed and sealed by a Florida-licensed architect or engineer
- Barrier/fence documentation and site plan required
- Electrical, plumbing, and gas sub-permits required separately
- Separate structural permit for screen enclosures
- Pool permit fees typically range from $200 to $900; Martin County fees are calculated from the value of work
- Plan review typically takes 2 to 6 weeks
- Pool enclosure and slab projects must meet Martin County's checklist requirements under the Florida Building Code

For waterfront properties in subdivisions approved after April 1982, pools must be set back at least 5 feet from the Shoreline Protection Zone or wetland buffer. Fence barriers 6 feet or less in height are exempt from district setback limitations under Martin County Land Development Regulations Section 3.204.

Jensen Beach's coastal location creates a high water table, with groundwater commonly encountered at 4 to 6 feet below grade during excavation. Standard mitigation includes dewatering systems, French drains, gravel beds, hydrostatic relief valves, and in some cases raising the pool elevation with fill dirt to lift the shell above the high-water mark.

Florida Statute 515 barrier requirements apply. Martin County building inspectors administer barrier inspections locally, and a Pool Alarm/Barrier Affidavit may be required as part of the final inspection package.

If your property is in an HOA, written ARC approval is required before construction begins. HOAs may enforce setback rules and fencing heights that exceed the state's 4-foot minimum — often requiring a minimum 6-foot fence.
