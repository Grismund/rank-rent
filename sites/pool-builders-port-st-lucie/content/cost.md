The short answer on **pool builders cost Port St. Lucie** homeowners can expect: most new inground pool projects fall between $25,000 and $65,000, depending on pool type, size, features, and site conditions. A fiberglass shell install starts around $25,000. A fully customized gunite pool with spa and screen enclosure can reach $65,000 or more.

These figures reflect what Treasure Coast contractors are quoting in the current market. Your specific yard, pool design, and the contractor you choose will shift the number. The [pool cost calculator](/pool-cost-calculator/) gives a quick estimate based on your selections.

## Average Pool Construction Prices

### Fiberglass Pool

**$25,000 to $55,000.** Fiberglass pools are factory-molded shells installed in a pre-excavated hole. They are the fastest pool type to install (3 to 6 weeks) and have the lowest long-term maintenance cost because the smooth gel-coat surface resists algae and staining. The price range reflects shell size — a 10-by-20 plunge pool sits at the low end, while a 16-by-40 freeform shell with built-in bench and tanning ledge reaches the upper range.

Fiberglass is a strong value option on the Treasure Coast. The shell handles the high water table well because it is a single piece that flexes slightly with ground movement rather than cracking.

### Concrete (Gunite) Pool

**$35,000 to $65,000.** Gunite pools are built on site from steel rebar and sprayed concrete, allowing unlimited shape, depth, and feature customization. They are the most expensive pool type and take the longest to build (8 to 16 weeks), but they are the only option for homeowners who want a fully custom design — freeform shapes, beach entries, integrated spas, negative edges, and water features.

The surface is finished with plaster ($3,000 to $5,000), quartz aggregate ($5,000 to $8,000), or pebble aggregate ($6,000 to $10,000). Pebble finishes are popular on the Treasure Coast because they are durable in hard water and hide mineral staining.

### Vinyl Liner Pool

**$25,000 to $45,000.** Vinyl liner pools use a steel or polymer wall frame with a custom-fitted vinyl liner. They are the lowest-cost entry point for a new inground pool. The trade-off is the liner itself, which needs replacement every 7 to 12 years at a cost of $3,000 to $5,000. Liner pools are less common on the Treasure Coast than fiberglass or gunite but remain a viable option for homeowners on a tighter budget.

## Factors That Influence Pool Cost

Five factors drive the final price, roughly in order of impact:

**Pool type and size.** The construction method (gunite, fiberglass, vinyl) sets the base range. Within each type, a larger pool costs more in materials and excavation. A 12-by-24 pool is a standard residential size; going to 16-by-32 or larger adds significantly to the total.

**Site conditions.** The Treasure Coast's high water table means most builds require dewatering during excavation. Sandy soil is easier to dig than rock, but the dewatering equipment and French drain installation add $2,000 to $5,000 to the project cost. Difficult access — narrow side yards, fences that need temporary removal — can also increase the price.

**Features and upgrades.** A base pool is the shell, plumbing, equipment, and basic deck. Each addition increases the total: screen enclosure ($5,000 to $15,000), pool heater ($2,000 to $5,000), saltwater chlorine generator ($1,000 to $3,000), spa or hot tub ($5,000 to $15,000), LED lighting ($500 to $2,000), and water features ($1,000 to $5,000).

**Deck material and size.** A standard brushed concrete deck is included in most base quotes. Upgrading to cool-deck, pavers, or travertine adds $3,000 to $10,000 depending on square footage. See the [pool deck resurfacing](/pool-deck-resurfacing/) page for material options.

**Permit fees.** St. Lucie County pool permit fees are approximately $461 (including state surcharge). Screen enclosure permits run approximately $180. Plan review takes 14 to 45 days. These are fixed costs that do not vary much between projects.

## Beyond the Base Price

The base pool price does not include several items that most Treasure Coast homeowners end up adding:

- **Screen enclosure:** $5,000 to $15,000. Near-universal on Florida pools. Keeps out insects and debris, reduces chemical use, and can serve as the required safety barrier.
- **Pool heater:** $2,000 to $5,000. Extends the swim season to 12 months. Heat pumps are the most efficient option in this climate.
- **Saltwater chlorine generator:** $1,000 to $3,000. Produces chlorine from dissolved salt, reducing the need to handle and store liquid or tablet chlorine.
- **Spa / hot tub add-on:** $5,000 to $15,000. Integrated spas are most cost-effective when built as part of the original pool construction rather than added later.
- **Pool deck:** $3,000 to $10,000. Cool-deck and spray-deck coatings are popular because they reduce surface temperature in direct sun.
- **Landscaping and fencing:** Variable. HOAs may have specific requirements for landscaping around the pool area.

A fully loaded pool project — gunite pool, screen enclosure, heater, saltwater system, premium deck — can reach $80,000 to $100,000. Most homeowners prioritize the base pool and screen enclosure, then add features over time.
