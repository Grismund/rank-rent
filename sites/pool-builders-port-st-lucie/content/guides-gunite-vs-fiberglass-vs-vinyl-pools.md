Choosing between **gunite vs fiberglass pool Florida** homeowners face is the single biggest design decision in a new pool project. Each construction type has distinct trade-offs in cost, build time, maintenance, design flexibility, and long-term durability. All three work in Treasure Coast conditions when properly engineered for the high water table, but they serve different priorities.

This guide compares the three pool types side by side with Florida-specific considerations, so you can narrow down the right choice before requesting contractor quotes.

## Key Differences

### Construction Method

**Gunite** pools are built entirely on site. A steel rebar framework is shaped to the pool design and concrete is sprayed (gunite) or poured (shotcrete) over it. After curing, the surface is finished with plaster, quartz aggregate, or pebble. Because every element is built on site, the pool can be any shape, depth, or size that fits your yard. This is the only option for truly custom designs — freeform shapes, beach entries, integrated spas, negative edges, and vanishing-edge pools.

**Fiberglass** pools are factory-molded shells. The manufacturer produces the pool as a single piece in a controlled environment, and the contractor installs it by excavating the hole, setting the shell with a crane, backfilling, and connecting plumbing and electrical. You choose from the manufacturer's available shapes and sizes. The gel-coat surface is smooth, non-porous, and comes factory-finished.

**Vinyl liner** pools use a prefabricated wall system — steel or polymer panels — assembled in the excavation and topped with a coping track. A custom-measured vinyl liner is draped over the walls and floor, pulled tight, and sealed. Vinyl allows more shape options than fiberglass (the walls can be configured in various layouts) but fewer than gunite (no curves with tight radii, no negative edges).

### Cost and Long-Term Economics

Upfront cost is lowest for vinyl ($25,000 to $45,000) and highest for gunite ($35,000 to $65,000). But upfront cost does not tell the whole story.

Vinyl liners need replacement every 7 to 12 years at $3,000 to $5,000 per replacement. Over 25 years, that is 2 to 3 liner replacements — $6,000 to $15,000 in additional cost.

Gunite pools need resurfacing every 10 to 15 years. Replastering costs $3,000 to $5,000. Pebble or quartz resurfacing costs $5,000 to $10,000. That is 1 to 2 resurfacings over 25 years.

Fiberglass gel-coat surfaces can last 25 years or more with proper chemical maintenance. The long-term surface cost is the lowest of the three.

When you factor in long-term maintenance, the total cost of ownership over 25 years is closer across all three types than the upfront prices suggest.

### Build Timeline

Fiberglass is the fastest. The shell is manufactured off-site and the on-site work — excavation, setting, backfill, plumbing, deck, enclosure — takes 3 to 6 weeks. Vinyl liner pools take 4 to 8 weeks because the wall system is assembled on site. Gunite takes the longest at 8 to 16 weeks because the concrete must cure before finishing, and the finish itself requires additional curing time.

Add 2 to 6 weeks for permit plan review in St. Lucie County or Martin County before construction starts. The [build timeline guide](/guides/how-long-does-it-take-to-build-a-pool/) covers each construction phase.

### Maintenance Demands

**Fiberglass** requires the least ongoing maintenance. The smooth gel-coat surface does not harbor algae, so chemical demand is lower and brushing is minimal. Calcium buildup can occur at the waterline in hard water areas (like the Treasure Coast), but it is easy to manage.

**Gunite** requires the most. The porous plaster surface absorbs algae and minerals, requiring regular brushing, more aggressive chemical maintenance, and acid washing every few years. Pebble and quartz finishes reduce this burden compared to standard plaster.

**Vinyl** falls in the middle. The smooth liner surface is easy to clean, but the liner is vulnerable to punctures, fading, and staining. Chemical balance must be carefully maintained to avoid liner degradation.

### Florida Water Table Considerations

All three pool types are built successfully on the Treasure Coast's high water table. The key engineering consideration is preventing the pool shell from floating when the water table rises:

**Gunite** handles groundwater well because the shell is heavy. The mass of concrete and rebar resists buoyant forces. Hydrostatic relief valves are still installed as standard practice.

**Fiberglass** shells are lighter but flexible. They resist cracking from ground movement and are designed with hydrostatic relief valves to equalize pressure. The key installation step is proper backfill material (usually gravel or clean sand) that allows groundwater to drain around the shell.

**Vinyl** wall systems rely on the backfill and drain system to manage groundwater pressure. The liner itself is not structural, so the wall panels and backfill must handle the hydrostatic load.

## How This Affects Your Decision

If your priority is **custom design** — a freeform pool, integrated spa, beach entry, or negative edge — gunite is the only option. You are paying for unlimited flexibility and accepting a longer build time.

If your priority is **speed and low maintenance** — you want a pool in the ground fast with minimal long-term upkeep — fiberglass is the strongest choice. You are accepting the shape limitations of factory molds.

If your priority is **lowest upfront cost** — you need an inground pool at the lowest entry price and are comfortable with periodic liner replacement — vinyl is the starting point. Factor in liner replacement costs when comparing to fiberglass.

For most Port St. Lucie homeowners, the decision comes down to gunite vs. fiberglass. Vinyl is a viable option but is less common on the Treasure Coast than in northern markets. Talk to a contractor about your specific yard, design goals, and budget. The right pool type depends on your priorities — there is no universal best answer.
