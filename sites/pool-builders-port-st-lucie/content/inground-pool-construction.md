## What Is Inground Pool Construction?

Inground pool construction is the process of designing, permitting, excavating, and building a permanent swimming pool in your backyard. In Port St. Lucie, that process includes additional steps that do not apply in every market: dewatering during excavation due to the high water table, mandatory safety barrier installation under Florida Statute 515, and near-universal screen enclosure construction to protect the pool area from insects and debris.

A new inground pool on the Treasure Coast is a significant project. Total costs range from $25,000 to $65,000 depending on pool type, size, features, and site conditions. Build timelines range from 3 weeks for a straightforward fiberglass install to 16 weeks for a fully customized gunite pool. The [cost guide](/cost/) breaks down pricing in detail.

## Inground Pool Construction Materials and Styles

Three construction methods are used on the Treasure Coast:

**Gunite (concrete)** is poured and shaped on site, allowing unlimited customization of shape, depth, benches, and water features. Gunite pools cost $35,000 to $65,000 and take 8 to 16 weeks to build. The surface is typically finished with plaster, pebble aggregate, or tile. Gunite is the most popular choice for homeowners who want a custom shape or integrated spa.

**Fiberglass** pools are factory-molded shells delivered to the site and set into a prepared excavation. They cost $25,000 to $55,000 and install in 3 to 6 weeks. The smooth gel-coat surface resists algae growth and requires less chemical maintenance than concrete. Shape options are limited to the manufacturer's molds.

**Vinyl liner** pools use a steel or polymer wall frame with a custom-fitted vinyl liner. They cost $25,000 to $45,000 and take 4 to 8 weeks to build. The liner needs replacement every 7 to 12 years at a cost of $3,000 to $5,000, which is a recurring expense other pool types do not have.

All three types perform well in Treasure Coast conditions when engineered for the local water table. The [gunite vs. fiberglass vs. vinyl guide](/guides/gunite-vs-fiberglass-vs-vinyl-pools/) covers the full comparison.

## Inground Pool Construction Step by Step

### Design and Permits

The process starts with a site evaluation. The contractor measures the yard, checks setbacks (5 to 7.5 feet from side and rear property lines in standard residential zones), identifies underground utilities, and assesses water table depth. If you are in an HOA, the contractor can prepare materials for Architectural Review Committee (ARC) approval — written ARC approval is required before construction begins.

Pool construction in St. Lucie County requires a building permit under the Florida Building Code 8th Edition. Plans must be signed and sealed by a Florida-licensed architect or engineer. Electrical, plumbing, and structural sub-permits are required separately. Screen enclosures require their own structural permit. Plan review typically takes 14 to 45 days depending on project complexity.

### Excavation and Construction

Excavation is where the Treasure Coast's water table becomes a factor. The water table in the Port St. Lucie area typically sits 4 to 12 feet below grade. When the excavation hits groundwater, dewatering equipment pumps the water out and keeps the hole dry during construction. Over-excavation, French drain systems, and gravel beds are standard scope on most builds. Hydrostatic relief valves are installed in the pool floor to prevent the shell from lifting if the water table rises after construction.

For gunite pools, steel rebar is set and concrete is sprayed over the framework. For fiberglass, the shell is craned into the excavation and leveled. For vinyl, the wall panels are assembled and the liner is fitted and sealed.

### Finishing and Inspection

After the shell is complete, the contractor installs plumbing, electrical connections, the pool deck, and the screen enclosure. Florida Statute 515 requires a compliant safety barrier before final inspection: a fence at least 48 inches high, self-closing and self-latching gates, latch release at least 54 inches above grade, and no climbable features on the exterior face. A screen enclosure can serve as the barrier if it meets all statutory requirements.

The building department conducts multiple inspections: structural, plumbing, electrical, barrier, and final. The pool is filled, the equipment is started, and the contractor walks you through operation and maintenance.
