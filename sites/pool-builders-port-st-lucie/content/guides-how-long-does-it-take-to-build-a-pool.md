The answer to **how long to build a pool** in Port St. Lucie depends on three things: pool type, permit timeline, and weather. A fiberglass pool can be swimming-ready in 5 to 12 weeks from contract signing. A gunite pool can take 10 to 22 weeks. This guide breaks down where the time goes so you can set realistic expectations before starting the project.

## Quick Summary

| Phase | Gunite (Concrete) | Fiberglass | Vinyl Liner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Design and permits | 2–6 weeks | 2–6 weeks | 2–6 weeks |
| Construction | 8–16 weeks | 3–6 weeks | 4–8 weeks |
| **Total** | **10–22 weeks** | **5–12 weeks** | **6–14 weeks** |

These ranges include the permit review period. Construction time alone — once the permit is issued and the contractor starts digging — is shorter. But the permit phase is real time that you need to account for in your planning.

## Key Differences

### Pre-Construction: Design and Permits

Every pool project in St. Lucie County starts with the same administrative sequence: site evaluation, design, engineering drawings, and permit application. The contractor evaluates your yard, designs the pool, and commissions signed and sealed plans from a Florida-licensed engineer. The permit package includes the pool structural plan, electrical sub-permit, plumbing sub-permit, and (if applicable) a separate structural permit for the screen enclosure.

Plan review in St. Lucie County typically takes 14 to 45 days depending on project complexity and the department's current workload. Martin County (for Stuart and Jensen Beach projects) and Indian River County (for Vero Beach projects) have similar timelines, typically 2 to 6 weeks. If the plans are returned for revisions, add another 1 to 2 weeks for resubmittal and re-review.

If your property is in an HOA, the Architectural Review Committee (ARC) approval process adds time before you can even apply for a permit. ARC review can take 2 to 6 weeks depending on the community's meeting schedule and review process. Start the ARC application as early as possible.

### Gunite Pool Construction: 8 to 16 Weeks

Gunite pools take the longest because the concrete construction and curing process cannot be rushed. Here is where the weeks go:

**Excavation and dewatering (1 to 2 weeks).** The hole is dug to the engineered dimensions. On the Treasure Coast, the high water table means dewatering equipment is running during excavation and through much of the construction phase. Sandy soil digs quickly, but managing groundwater adds time.

**Steel and plumbing (1 to 2 weeks).** Rebar is tied into the engineered framework. Plumbing lines are installed in the shell floor and walls. The building department inspects the steel and plumbing before concrete is applied.

**Gunite application (1 day to 1 week).** The concrete is sprayed over the rebar framework. The shell takes shape in a single pour session, but scheduling the gunite crew and mobilizing the equipment can take several days.

**Curing (2 to 4 weeks).** The concrete must cure for a minimum of 28 days before the finish surface is applied. During this period, the shell is watered daily to prevent cracking. This is dead time — no construction work happens, but it is a mandatory wait.

**Tile, coping, and decking (1 to 2 weeks).** Waterline tile is set, coping is installed around the pool edge, and the deck is poured or coated.

**Interior finish (1 to 2 weeks).** Plaster, quartz, or pebble finish is applied to the shell interior. Each finish type has its own curing requirements.

**Screen enclosure, equipment, and startup (1 to 2 weeks).** The screen enclosure is built (if not already completed during the curing wait), pool equipment is connected, and the pool is filled and started.

### Fiberglass Pool Installation: 3 to 6 Weeks

Fiberglass is the fastest because the shell is manufactured off-site and arrives ready to install:

**Excavation and dewatering (2 to 4 days).** The hole is dug to the shell dimensions plus clearance for backfill. Dewatering runs during the dig on most Treasure Coast builds.

**Shell setting (1 day).** The fiberglass shell is delivered by truck and craned into the excavation. It is leveled, plumbed, and positioned in a single day.

**Backfill and plumbing (3 to 5 days).** The shell is backfilled with gravel or clean sand while water is simultaneously added to the pool to equalize pressure and prevent the shell from shifting. Plumbing connections are made.

**Deck and enclosure (1 to 2 weeks).** The pool deck is poured and finished. The screen enclosure is built. Equipment is connected.

**Startup (1 to 2 days).** The pool is filled, equipment is tested, and chemical balancing begins.

### Vinyl Liner Pool: 4 to 8 Weeks

Vinyl falls between fiberglass and gunite:

**Excavation (2 to 4 days).** Similar to fiberglass — the hole is dug to wall panel dimensions.

**Wall assembly (3 to 5 days).** Steel or polymer wall panels are assembled in the excavation and secured. Bottom grade is leveled and prepared for the liner.

**Plumbing and electrical (3 to 5 days).** Supply and return lines, skimmer, and main drain are installed through the wall system.

**Liner installation (1 to 2 days).** The custom-measured vinyl liner is draped over the walls, pulled tight with a vacuum to remove wrinkles, and sealed.

**Deck, enclosure, and startup (1 to 2 weeks).** Deck work, screen enclosure, equipment connection, and final fill.

### What Causes Delays

**Weather.** Florida's rainy season runs June through September. Afternoon thunderstorms can shut down excavation and concrete work. Gunite pools are most affected because the shell cannot be poured in rain, and the curing phase is extended if heavy rain oversaturates the concrete. Fiberglass and vinyl are less weather-sensitive because the shell is not poured on site.

**Permit delays.** If plans are returned for revisions, each resubmittal cycle adds 1 to 2 weeks. Working with a contractor experienced in St. Lucie County permitting reduces the chance of revision rounds.

**Inspection scheduling.** Multiple inspections are required: structural, plumbing, electrical, barrier, and final. Each inspection must pass before the next construction phase begins. If the building department is backed up, inspection wait times can add days between phases.

**Supply chain.** Fiberglass shells are manufactured to order and may have lead times of 2 to 6 weeks before delivery. Vinyl liners are custom-measured and fabricated, with lead times of 1 to 3 weeks. Gunite materials (concrete, rebar, plaster) are locally sourced and rarely delayed.

## How This Affects Your Decision

If you want to be swimming by a specific date, work backward from that date and pad the timeline:

- **Need a pool in 3 months?** Fiberglass is the safest choice. Start the permit process now.
- **Planning for 6 months out?** All three pool types are feasible. Gunite is tight but doable if permits move on schedule.
- **Starting during rainy season?** Expect the high end of each timeline range. Fiberglass is least affected by weather delays.
