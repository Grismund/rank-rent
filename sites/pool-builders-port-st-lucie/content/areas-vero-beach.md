## Pool Permit Requirements in Vero Beach

Pool construction in Vero Beach requires a building permit from the Indian River County Building Division (or the City of Vero Beach Building Department for properties within city limits). Key requirements:

- A completed Swimming Pool permit application with signed and sealed plans by a Florida-licensed Design Professional
- TDH (total design head) calculation for pool and/or spa
- **Soils investigation reports are required for all waterfront properties** bordering the Atlantic Ocean, Indian River Lagoon, Sebastian River, and associated canals
- Pools and spas constructed seaward of the 1987 Coastal Construction Control Line require approval from the Florida Department of Environmental Protection
- Pool plans must clearly indicate the proposed pool barrier type and location
- Residential pool permit fee is $225; above-ground pool permit fee is $75
- A separate permit is needed for screen enclosures
- Building division contact: 1801 27th Street, Vero Beach, FL 32960, phone (772) 226-1260

The coastal high water table in Indian River County means groundwater is commonly encountered at 3 to 6 feet below grade. Standard mitigation includes dewatering systems, French drains, gravel beds, and hydrostatic relief valves. The two primary high-water-table risks are shell floating (groundwater lifts an empty buoyant shell) and hydrostatic pressure cracking.

Florida Statute 515 barrier requirements apply. Indian River County building inspectors verify barrier compliance at the final pool inspection. A screen enclosure may serve as the barrier if it meets all statutory requirements.

## Popular Pool Styles in Vero Beach

Vero Beach's barrier island communities and mainland neighborhoods drive different pool preferences:

- **Barrier island properties (Central Beach, South Beach):** Custom gunite pools engineered for coastal soil conditions. Salt-air-resistant equipment and finishes are standard. Soils investigations add to the pre-construction timeline. Compact designs are common on the smaller island lots.
- **Grand Harbor and gated communities:** Premium gunite pools with spas, water features, and upgraded finishes. Pool designs must conform to community ARC guidelines including specific setbacks, fence heights, and equipment placement requirements.
- **West Vero and Florida Ridge:** Standard fiberglass and gunite pools on larger lots. These areas offer the most straightforward builds with fewer regulatory complications than coastal or gated community properties.
- **Downtown and Original Town:** Plunge pools and geometric designs on smaller historic lots. Fiberglass is popular for these projects because of the faster timeline and minimal disruption to neighboring properties.

Saltwater systems are increasingly standard across Vero Beach neighborhoods, and variable-speed pumps are now required for all replacement installations under federal efficiency standards.
