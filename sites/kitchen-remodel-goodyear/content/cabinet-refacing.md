New doors, drawer fronts, and hardware go on. The result looks like a new kitchen at roughly half the cost and a fraction of the disruption.

## About Cabinet Refacing

Refacing makes sense when the cabinet boxes are structurally sound but the style is wrong. If the shelves hold weight without sagging, the frames are square, and the hinges still close properly, the boxes have years of life left in them. What typically looks dated is the door style, the finish, and the hardware.

This is a common situation in Goodyear. Many homes built in the mid-2000s came with raised-panel doors in honey oak, dark cherry, or medium maple. The cabinets were builder-grade but functional. Twenty years later, the boxes are fine, but the aesthetic belongs to a different era.

Refacing addresses the visible surfaces without tearing into walls, plumbing, or electrical. The kitchen stays partially functional during the work, and the project wraps up in roughly three to five days for a standard-size kitchen.

## How Cabinet Refacing Works

### Planning and Material Selection

The contractor inspects every cabinet box to confirm it is in good structural condition. Boxes with water damage, warped frames, or failed joints need repair or replacement before refacing begins. If more than a few boxes are compromised, a full cabinet replacement may be the better investment — the [refacing vs. replacement guide](/guides/cabinet-refacing-vs-replacement/) covers how to evaluate that decision.

You choose the new door style, material, and finish. Options range from thermofoil (the budget choice) through solid wood (maple, oak, birch) in painted or stained finishes. Hardware selection happens at the same time — pulls, knobs, hinges, and soft-close mechanisms.

### Demolition and Installation

The contractor removes the old doors, drawer fronts, and hardware. If the exposed face frames are being veneered — common when changing from a stain to a painted look — that layer goes on first.

New doors and drawer fronts install next. Each piece is fitted, aligned, and adjusted so everything closes flush. New hinges and hardware go on last. Some projects include new crown molding, end panels, or a matching skin for the dishwasher and refrigerator to unify the look.

Because plumbing and electrical remain untouched, no permit is typically required for a pure refacing job. If you are adding under-cabinet lighting or modifying outlets at the same time, those changes may need a permit from the City of Goodyear.

### Final Walkthrough

The contractor checks door alignment, drawer operation, hinge tension, and hardware placement. You verify everything before the project closes out.

## Popular Options for Cabinet Refacing

Goodyear homeowners refacing builder-grade cabinets tend to choose:

- **Shaker doors in white or gray paint** — the dominant trend in the Phoenix metro. Clean, bright, and pairs well with quartz countertops.
- **Soft-close hinges and drawer slides** — an upgrade that eliminates slamming and extends hardware life. Standard on most refacing projects now.
- **New crown molding and trim** — ties the refaced cabinets to the room and gives a more finished look than the flat soffits many 2000s-era Goodyear kitchens have.
- **Matching end panels and appliance skins** — makes the refaced cabinets look built-in rather than retrofitted.

## How Much Does Cabinet Refacing Cost?

Cabinet refacing in Goodyear typically runs between $4,000 and $8,000 for a standard kitchen. The low end covers thermofoil doors with basic hardware on a smaller kitchen. The high end covers solid wood doors, soft-close upgrades, crown molding, and end panels on a larger kitchen.

For comparison, full cabinet replacement on the same kitchen starts at $8,000 and can exceed $20,000 for custom cabinetry. Refacing delivers a similar visual result at roughly 40 to 60 percent of the replacement cost.
