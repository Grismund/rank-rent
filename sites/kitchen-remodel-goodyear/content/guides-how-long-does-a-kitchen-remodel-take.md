The answer to **how long does a kitchen remodel take** depends almost entirely on scope. A cosmetic refresh can be done in a week. A mid-range remodel with new cabinets and countertops runs three to five weeks. A full renovation with layout changes takes four to eight weeks, sometimes longer if permits, material lead times, or inspections cause delays.

This guide breaks down each phase so Goodyear homeowners can set realistic expectations before the first cabinet comes off the wall.

## Quick Summary

| Scope | Typical Duration | Kitchen Out of Service |
|-------|-----------------|----------------------|
| Minor update | 1-2 weeks | Partial — usable most of the time |
| Mid-range remodel | 3-5 weeks | Yes — during demolition and installation |
| Full renovation | 4-8 weeks | Yes — entire duration of construction |

These timelines assume materials are ordered and available, permits are approved without revision, and no unexpected issues arise during demolition. Add one to two weeks if custom cabinets have a long lead time or if the city requires a plan revision.

## Timeline by Scope

**Minor update (1-2 weeks).** The scope includes painting or refacing cabinets, replacing hardware, installing a new backsplash, and upgrading light fixtures. No plumbing or electrical changes. No permits needed. The contractor may work in sections, keeping parts of the kitchen accessible throughout. Cabinet refacing alone takes three to five days.

**Mid-range remodel (3-5 weeks).** New or refaced cabinets, new countertops, new backsplash, updated flooring, and potentially new appliances. The layout stays the same. Week one is demolition and prep. Week two through three is cabinet installation and countertop templating. The fabricator takes one to two weeks to cut and polish the stone. Week four to five is countertop installation, backsplash, flooring, appliance hookup, and final details.

If the project includes plumbing or electrical modifications — adding a disposal, moving an outlet, or upgrading the dishwasher circuit — the contractor pulls a permit from the City of Goodyear and schedules a rough inspection before closing up walls. That inspection adds a day or two to the schedule depending on the city's availability.

**Full renovation (4-8 weeks).** Everything changes: layout, cabinets, countertops, plumbing, electrical, flooring, and possibly walls. The timeline breaks down roughly like this:

- **Week 1:** Demolition and structural changes (removing walls, framing new openings)
- **Weeks 2-3:** Rough plumbing and electrical; city rough inspection
- **Weeks 3-4:** Cabinet installation; countertop templating
- **Week 4-5:** Countertop fabrication (off-site, 1-2 week lead time)
- **Weeks 5-6:** Countertop installation, backsplash, flooring
- **Weeks 6-7:** Appliance installation, trim, paint, cleanup
- **Week 7-8:** Final inspection, punch list, walkthrough

Custom cabinets can extend the schedule significantly. Lead times for custom orders range from four to ten weeks from order to delivery. If you are ordering custom cabinets, that lead time happens before construction begins, not during it.

## What Affects the Schedule

**Permit review time.** The City of Goodyear adopted the 2024 International technical codes. Permit review for residential kitchen remodels through the Development Services Department typically takes one to two weeks. If the plans require revision, add another week. Contractors familiar with Goodyear's process can often streamline the application.

**Material lead times.** Stock cabinets are available in one to two weeks. Semi-custom takes three to six weeks. Custom takes four to ten weeks. Countertop fabrication (after templating) takes one to two weeks for granite and quartz. Specialty materials like quartzite or imported tile may have longer lead times.

**Existing conditions.** Demolition sometimes reveals problems that were hidden behind the old cabinets or flooring: water damage to the subfloor, outdated plumbing that needs rerouting, or electrical wiring that does not meet current code. Each issue adds time and cost. Homes in Goodyear are generally newer (median year built 2007), so surprise conditions are less common than in older markets, but they still occur — especially water damage near dishwashers and sinks caused by the area's very hard water.

**Inspection scheduling.** Goodyear requires inspections at the rough-in stage and again at completion for permitted work. The contractor schedules these with the city. Wait times are usually a few business days, but they can be longer during busy construction periods.

**Contractor availability.** The West Valley's population growth has increased demand for remodeling contractors. Scheduling the project may require a lead time of two to six weeks from signing a contract to the start of demolition, depending on the contractor's workload.

## Living Without a Kitchen

For mid-range and full remodels, the kitchen is out of service for most of the construction period. Goodyear homeowners handle this in several ways:

- **Temporary kitchen station.** Set up a folding table in the dining room, garage, or patio with a microwave, electric kettle, slow cooker, and portable cooktop. Keep a cooler or mini-fridge nearby for perishables.
- **Paper products.** Disposable plates, cups, and utensils reduce dishwashing when the sink is unavailable.
- **Outdoor grill.** Arizona's dry climate makes the patio grill a reliable cooking option for most of the year. During summer months (when temperatures exceed 105 degrees), early morning or late evening is more practical.
- **Meal planning.** Stock up on no-cook meals and easy-prep items before demolition begins. Know which restaurants and delivery services you will rely on during the heaviest construction phases.
- **Water access.** The bathroom sink becomes the dishwashing station. If you have a utility sink in the laundry room or garage, that is even better.

The key is accepting that the first week (demolition) is the worst, and each subsequent week gets progressively closer to a finished kitchen. Plan for the disruption and it becomes manageable.
