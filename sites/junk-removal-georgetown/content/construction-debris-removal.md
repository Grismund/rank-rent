## What Is Construction Debris Removal?

Construction debris removal is the pickup and disposal of waste materials generated during building, renovation, or demolition projects. A hauler arrives with a truck, loads the debris from wherever it has been staged — driveway, yard, inside the structure — and transports it to the appropriate disposal facility.

This service is distinct from a dumpster rental. A dumpster sits on your property for days while you fill it yourself. A hauler comes once, handles the loading, and clears the site in a single visit. For jobs that are already complete or for one-time cleanups, hauler pickup is typically faster and requires less coordination. ## What We Haul for Construction Debris Removal

Local haulers take common renovation and construction waste:

- Drywall and sheetrock (intact sheets or broken pieces)
- Lumber, plywood, and framing scraps
- Tile, ceramic, and stone
- Carpet, padding, and vinyl flooring
- Concrete, brick, and block (weight surcharges may apply)
- Roofing material (shingles, underlayment, flashing)
- Siding, stucco, and exterior trim
- Plumbing fixtures (sinks, tubs, toilets)
- Cabinetry and countertops
- Windows and doors

Materials haulers cannot take include asbestos-containing materials, lead paint waste, and chemical solvents. If your renovation involves a pre-1980 structure, check with the hauler about material testing before scheduling removal.

The crew arrives with the truck and loads the debris from wherever it sits — driveway, backyard, inside the structure, or the curb. For renovation projects still in progress, haulers can schedule recurring pickups as debris accumulates. The crew clears the area, sweeps loose material, and leaves the space ready for the next phase of work.

### Responsible Disposal

Construction debris goes to the Georgetown Transfer Station on W.L. Walden Drive or the Williamson County Landfill in Hutto. Metal components — copper pipe, steel framing, aluminum flashing — are separated for scrap recycling at the Williamson County Recycling Center. Clean wood and concrete may be diverted to recycling where facilities accept them. The hauler handles sorting and routing.
