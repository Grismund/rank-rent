Most household items are fair game for a junk removal hauler — furniture, appliances, electronics, yard debris, and construction waste all go on the truck without issue. But there is a category of materials that haulers in the Georgetown area cannot legally or safely transport. Knowing **what junk removal won't take** off your hands before you schedule a pickup saves time and helps you route restricted items to the correct disposal facility in Williamson County.

## Overview

The restrictions come from three sources: federal EPA regulations on hazardous materials, Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) disposal rules, and local ordinances in Georgetown and surrounding cities. Haulers follow these rules because they are liable for what they transport and what they deliver to the landfill or transfer station. The Georgetown Transfer Station and the Williamson County Landfill both reject certain materials at the gate.

The common thread across all restricted items: they can harm people, contaminate groundwater, release toxic gases, or create fire and explosion risks if handled improperly.

## Restricted Materials and Where to Bring Them

### Hazardous Chemicals and Solvents

Haulers will not take chemical solvents, pesticides, herbicides, pool chemicals, acids, or any container with a hazardous material warning label. These materials require special handling and cannot go into a standard landfill.

**Where to bring them:** Williamson County holds periodic Household Hazardous Waste Cleanup Events for residents. These events accept chemicals, solvents, pesticides, and similar materials at no charge. Taylor residents are eligible for these events. Check the Williamson County website for the next scheduled date. Between events, the Travis County Household Hazardous Waste Facility on Todd Lane in Austin accepts Williamson County residents by appointment.

### Paint

Liquid paint — both latex and oil-based — is restricted. Oil-based paint is classified as hazardous waste. Latex paint in liquid form creates spill risk during transport. However, dried latex paint is accepted at the landfill. If you have a few cans, remove the lids and let the paint dry completely (mixing in cat litter speeds this up), then put the dried cans in the trash.

**Where to bring liquid paint:** Williamson County Household Hazardous Waste Events accept all types of liquid paint. Some local paint stores accept unused paint for recycling — call ahead to confirm.

### Medical and Biohazardous Waste

Needles, syringes, sharps, blood-contaminated materials, and pharmaceutical waste are regulated medical waste. Standard haulers are not equipped to transport them, and landfills will not accept them.

**Where to bring them:** Sharps go in a puncture-proof container (many pharmacies sell sharps disposal containers or accept full ones). Prescription drugs can be dropped at the Georgetown Police Department or any DEA-authorized take-back location. The Williamson County Household Hazardous Waste Events also accept pharmaceuticals.

### Propane Tanks and Pressurized Cylinders

Propane tanks, helium tanks, butane canisters, and other pressurized vessels are explosion risks if punctured or crushed during transport or at the landfill. Haulers will not load them.

**Where to bring them:** Most propane retailers (hardware stores, gas stations) accept old tanks for exchange or recycling. Small camping-size canisters can go to the Williamson County Recycling Center at 600 Landfill Road in Hutto if fully empty with the valve open.

### Tires

Standard household junk removal haulers typically exclude tires because landfills charge special fees and have separate processing areas for them. A few tires mixed into a large load may be accepted at the hauler's discretion with an extra charge.

**Where to bring them:** The City of Taylor hosts an annual Citywide Spring Cleanup that accepts tires for free. Keep Hutto Beautiful's annual Junk Round Up in October also accepts tires. The Williamson County Landfill accepts tires at the gate for a per-tire fee. Auto repair shops and tire retailers often accept old tires during a purchase.

### Asbestos-Containing Materials

If your home was built before 1980, insulation, floor tiles, pipe wrap, and certain siding materials may contain asbestos. Asbestos is a regulated hazardous material that requires certified abatement and specialized disposal. No standard hauler will touch it.

**Where to bring it:** You cannot handle asbestos yourself. Texas law requires a licensed asbestos abatement contractor to remove, bag, and transport the material to an approved disposal site. Contact TCEQ for a list of licensed abatement contractors in the Central Texas area.

### Automotive Fluids and Batteries

Motor oil, transmission fluid, brake fluid, antifreeze, and car batteries are restricted from standard junk removal loads. These materials contaminate landfill groundwater and require separate disposal channels.

**Where to bring them:** The Williamson County Recycling Center at 600 Landfill Road in Hutto accepts used motor oil, oil filters, and batteries. AutoZone, O'Reilly Auto Parts, and other auto retailers in Georgetown accept used oil and batteries for recycling at no charge.

### Electronics (in some cases)

Most haulers take standard electronics — TVs, computers, monitors, printers — as part of a junk removal load. However, some municipalities restrict certain electronics from landfill disposal. Cedar Park's bulk trash program explicitly prohibits TVs and computers from curbside pickup. Haulers route electronics to the Williamson County Recycling Center or Goodwill, which accepts working electronics for resale.

**Where to bring them:** Goodwill locations throughout Williamson County accept electronics. The Williamson County Recycling Center in Hutto takes old computers, printers, and peripherals. Keep Hutto Beautiful's Junk Round Up accepts electronics annually.
