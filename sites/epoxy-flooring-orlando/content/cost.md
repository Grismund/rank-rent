The direct answer on **epoxy flooring cost in Orlando**: a professionally installed two-car garage typically falls between $1,600 and $3,500. Commercial floors are priced per square foot and scale upward from there. The rest of this page explains what determines where your project lands within those ranges.

These numbers reflect current pricing from Orlando-area contractors. Your specific concrete condition, finish selection, and the contractor you choose will shift the final figure.

*Last updated: November 2026. Costs vary by contractor and site conditions.*

## Epoxy Flooring Price Ranges

### One-Car Garage

Approximately 250 square feet. Plan on **$1,250 to $2,500**. A solid color or basic chip broadcast with standard preparation sits at the lower end. A metallic finish, extensive crack repair, or a moisture-mitigating primer pushes toward the top.

Small floors cost more per square foot than larger ones. Setup — bringing the grinder, running a moisture test, filling cracks — takes roughly the same effort on 250 square feet as on 500. The total is lower, but the per-foot rate is higher.

### Two-Car Garage

The standard Orlando residential project. Four hundred to five hundred square feet, running **$1,600 to $3,500**. That range spans a standard chip floor with routine prep at the bottom to a layered metallic with a polyaspartic topcoat at the top.

Chip or flake broadcast with a clear topcoat is the most common selection. It balances durability, appearance, and price. Upgrading the clear to a polyaspartic topcoat adds a few hundred dollars and reduces the return-to-use time from roughly a week to about a day.

### Commercial or Large Area

Warehouses, shops, showrooms, restaurant kitchens — any floor above about 1,000 square feet is priced per foot.

- **Solid or chip:** $3 to $8 per square foot
- **Metallic or decorative:** $5 to $12 per square foot
- **Heavy industrial:** quoted per project based on chemical exposure, traffic loads, and specialty requirements

Larger floors bring the per-foot cost down because mobilization and setup remain fixed regardless of whether the crew coats 2,000 or 10,000 square feet. The [commercial epoxy flooring](/commercial-epoxy-flooring/) page covers what goes into a business floor system.

## Why Epoxy Flooring Prices Vary

Five factors, roughly in order of impact:

**Concrete condition.** Orlando homes sit on slab-on-grade foundations over sandy soil with a water table that commonly sits 2 to 4 feet below grade. When moisture vapor is rising through the slab — standard here, even in newer construction — the installer needs a vapor-mitigating primer before coating. Cracks, spalling, and old paint or sealer remnants add preparation time. The state of the concrete is the primary reason two similar-looking garages can receive different quotes.

**Square footage.** More floor requires more material and labor but costs less per square foot due to fixed setup costs.

**Coating system.** Single-color epoxy is the entry-level option at approximately $3 to $7 per foot installed. Chip broadcast runs slightly higher. Metallic costs $5 to $12 per foot because the material is more expensive and the application demands more precision and time.

**Number of coats.** Primer, base coat, and topcoat form the basic stack. Premium systems add extra base layers, decorative broadcasts between coats, or a second clear. Each additional layer adds material cost and another pass across the floor.

**Repairs.** Crack filling, spalling patches, and stripping a previous coating are line items above the coating itself. On older Orlando-area homes where the slab has absorbed decades of moisture cycling and thermal stress, that work can add several hundred dollars.

## Epoxy vs Polyaspartic: Cost Comparison

Orlando homeowners typically choose between two coating chemistries. They differ on price, curing speed, and long-term performance.

**Epoxy** is the more affordable option and accounts for the majority of residential installations. The price ranges above are epoxy-based. The trade-off is curing time: 48 to 72 hours before foot traffic and five to seven days before parking.

**Polyaspartic** costs 20 to 30 percent more — roughly $400 to $800 extra on a two-car garage. The benefit is a floor you can drive on within about a day and a topcoat that resists UV yellowing, which matters if your garage door faces afternoon sun.

Many Orlando-area contractors recommend a hybrid approach: an epoxy base coat for adhesion and cost control, topped with a polyaspartic clear for UV stability and faster return to service.
