Answers to the questions Orlando homeowners ask most often before coating a garage floor. This **epoxy flooring FAQ** covers lifespan expectations, curing timelines, DIY considerations, maintenance, and what Central Florida's climate means for your floor.

*Answers reflect manufacturer specifications and industry trade sources. For your specific concrete, consult a local contractor.*

## Common Epoxy Flooring Questions

### How long does epoxy flooring last?

A professionally installed residential garage floor typically performs for 10 to 20 years. The wide range reflects differences in concrete preparation quality, coating system thickness, and daily use. Commercial floors under heavy equipment may need a fresh topcoat in 5 to 10 years. If there is one variable to focus on, make it the surface preparation — it determines more about longevity than the product brand or the finish type.

### Can you epoxy a garage floor yourself?

Retail kits are available at home improvement stores, so technically, yes. Whether the result holds up in Central Florida's conditions is a different matter. Store-bought products apply at 2 to 3 mils versus 10 to 20 for a professional system, and without properly grinding the slab first, the bond is unreliable. Orlando contractors report that a meaningful share of their residential work involves stripping a failed DIY application before starting fresh — which ends up costing more than hiring a professional the first time.

### How long does epoxy take to cure?

Standard epoxy: 24 to 72 hours before foot traffic, five to seven days before parking a vehicle. Polyaspartic coatings cure much faster — often ready for full use within a day. Temperature and humidity affect those timelines directly. An Orlando garage in August, with slab temperatures exceeding 90 degrees and humidity near 87 percent, may need an extra day or two compared to the same project done in December when conditions stabilize.

### Is epoxy flooring slippery when wet?

A plain high-gloss epoxy surface can be slick when wet. In practice, almost nobody installs one that way. Flake or chip broadcast adds texture underfoot, and anti-slip aggregate can be incorporated into the topcoat anywhere water is expected. Both approaches address traction without compromising the finished appearance. If rain regularly tracks into your garage from the driveway, mention it to the contractor during the estimate.

### Does epoxy flooring crack?

Epoxy itself does not crack spontaneously. It cracks when the concrete beneath it moves, because the coating is rigid and follows the slab. Orlando's sandy soils generally produce less dramatic slab movement than the expansive clays found in other regions, but settling and hairline cracking still occur, particularly in older construction. Filling existing cracks before coating and addressing any moisture issues reduces the risk substantially. Normal vehicle and foot traffic does not cause cracking.

### Can epoxy be applied over old concrete?

Yes. Old concrete in sound structural condition coats just as well as new. The same preparation applies: strip old paint and sealer, repair cracks and spalling, grind to an adequate profile, and pass a moisture test. Orlando slabs from the 1970s and 1980s that were built without modern vapor barriers may require a moisture-mitigating primer, but the age of the concrete itself is not a disqualifying factor.

### What is the difference between epoxy and polyaspartic coatings?

Both are resin-based coatings for concrete. Epoxy costs less and cures slowly — 48 to 72 hours before foot traffic. Polyaspartic costs approximately 20 to 30 percent more, cures in about a day, and does not yellow under sunlight the way standard epoxy can. Many Orlando-area contractors install a hybrid system: an epoxy base coat for adhesion and cost control, topped with a polyaspartic clear for UV stability and faster return to use. The [epoxy vs. polyaspartic guide](/guides/epoxy-vs-polyaspartic-garage-floors/) covers the comparison in detail.

### How do you maintain epoxy floors?

The routine is minimal. Sweep or dust-mop to prevent grit from scratching the surface. Mop with warm water and a mild pH-neutral cleaner when the floor needs it. Avoid bleach and ammonia-based products, which dull the topcoat over time. Clean up oil and chemical spills when you notice them. The coating resists penetration, but no surface benefits from sitting in a puddle of gasoline or solvent indefinitely. That covers the full maintenance program.
