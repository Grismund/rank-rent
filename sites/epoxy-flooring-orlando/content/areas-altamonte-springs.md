## What Drives Epoxy Demand in Altamonte Springs

### Climate and Concrete Conditions

Altamonte Springs shares the Orlando metro's humid subtropical climate: approximately 72 degrees average annual temperature, 52 inches of rainfall concentrated in the June-through-September wet season, and average annual humidity of roughly 74 percent. Summer morning humidity regularly exceeds 80 percent, while winter and early spring months drop to the low 60s.

The city's housing stock was largely built during the 1960s-through-1980s suburban boom, and over 90 percent of Florida single-family homes from that era sit on poured concrete slab-on-grade foundations. Seminole County's sandy soils cause uneven moisture retention, leading to differential settlement and shrinkage cracks in older garage slabs. Homes in Spring Oaks and Sanlando Springs that are now 40 to 60 years old are especially susceptible to slab cracks from decades of thermal cycling and soil movement.

Sub-slab moisture is one of the most overlooked issues in Florida floor coating. If a coating system cannot handle the vapor pressure from below the slab, it will bubble, peel, or delaminate from underneath — regardless of how well the surface preparation was done. In Altamonte Springs, where the water table sits 2 to 4 feet below grade and many older homes lack modern vapor barriers, a moisture test before coating is the single most important step in the process.

## Local Considerations

**Permits:** Cosmetic epoxy coating over an existing concrete slab generally does not require a building permit in Seminole County or the City of Altamonte Springs. Florida HB 735 eliminated local occupational licensing for flooring trades statewide. Under Florida Statute 489.103, any contract exceeding $2,500 or requiring a building permit must be performed by a state-licensed contractor. A local business tax receipt is still required to operate within the city.

**Aging slabs and moisture:** Altamonte Springs homes built in the 1960s through 1980s are more likely to have concrete degraded from decades of hydrostatic pressure. At 70 to 90 percent ambient humidity, Central Florida slabs absorb atmospheric moisture, weakening concrete from within. Scheduling installation during November through January — when humidity consistently falls below 70 percent — provides the most stable curing conditions.
