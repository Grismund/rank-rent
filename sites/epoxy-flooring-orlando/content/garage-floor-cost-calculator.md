This **garage floor cost calculator for Orlando** generates a price range for an epoxy floor before you speak to anyone. Select your garage size, the finish you are considering, and the current condition of your concrete. The estimate appears immediately.

## How This Calculator Works

Three inputs drive the estimate:

1. **Garage size.** Single-car is roughly 250 square feet, double-car 400 to 500, triple-car 600 to 750. This sets the footage.
2. **Finish type.** Solid color, chip broadcast, metallic, or polyaspartic. Each option carries different material and labor costs.
3. **Slab condition.** New concrete, minor cracking, or an old coating that needs removal. More preparation work means a higher estimate.

From those three inputs, the tool produces a low and high range. The estimate accounts for material, standard concrete preparation, and installation labor.

## How We Calculate These Ranges

The pricing behind this tool reflects what Orlando-area contractors are currently quoting. Solid-color and chip systems install for approximately $3 to $8 per square foot. Metallic runs $5 to $12. Polyaspartic adds roughly 20 to 30 percent over a comparable epoxy system. Costs vary by contractor and site conditions.

A typical double-car garage falls between $1,600 and $3,500, consistent with what local contractors quote after an initial inspection. Those figures assume standard preparation: diamond grinding, minor crack filling, and a moisture vapor test.

What the calculator cannot factor in is the actual state of your slab. A floor with serious damage, active moisture intrusion, or a stubborn old coating will likely come in above the range shown. Use the result as a budget guide, not a binding estimate. The [cost guide](/cost/) explains each pricing factor in depth.

## Cost Estimator FAQ

### How accurate is this garage floor cost calculator?

Accurate enough for budgeting, not precise enough to serve as a contract price. It uses typical Orlando metro pricing and assumes a slab in reasonable condition. A contractor's on-site estimate will account for your concrete's actual state and any additional preparation it requires.

### Does the estimate include concrete prep work?

It covers the standard scope: diamond grinding, minor crack filling, and a moisture vapor test. If the slab has significant damage, an active moisture problem, or an old coating that requires chemical stripping, those are additional costs that may push the contractor's real price above what the tool shows.

### What is the difference between solid color, chip, and metallic finishes?

Solid color is a single tone and the least expensive option. Chip (also called flake or broadcast) adds colored vinyl pieces for texture, visual depth, and improved traction on wet surfaces. Metallic uses pigments that swirl during curing to create a unique pattern on every floor — the premium choice at $5 to $12 per square foot. All three are applied over the same prepared concrete base.

### How long does a garage floor coating take to install?

One to two days for most residential garages. Standard epoxy then needs 24 to 72 hours before foot traffic and five to seven days before parking. Polyaspartic is substantially faster — often ready for full use within 24 hours. Your contractor will provide specific timing based on the product and current weather conditions.

### Why does slab condition affect the price?

Concrete preparation is the most labor-intensive part of any coating project. A clean slab with minor hairline cracks requires standard grinding and filling. A slab with deep cracks, spalling, old paint, or high moisture vapor readings needs additional corrective work before any coating can go down. In Orlando, where the water table sits close to the surface and humidity is persistently high, moisture-related prep is common and adds to the project cost.
