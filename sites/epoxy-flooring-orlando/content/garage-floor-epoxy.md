## About Garage Floor Epoxy

Garage floor epoxy is a two-component coating — resin and hardener — mixed on the slab and applied in layers. The chemical bond locks the material directly into the concrete pores, creating a rigid shell that becomes the floor surface rather than sitting loosely on top of it.

The distinction between a professional installation and a retail kit is in the build thickness. Store-bought products go on at 2 to 3 mils. A professional system builds to 10 to 20 mils through primer, base coat, and topcoat layers. That added material is where the impact resistance and decade-plus longevity come from. The thin version might look fine for a year. The thick version performs for 10 to 20.

## Advantages of Garage Floor Epoxy

What it does for an Orlando garage in practical terms:

- **Chemical resistance.** Oil, gas, brake fluid, fertilizer — nothing penetrates the surface. Clean up whenever you get to it.
- **Dust elimination.** Bare concrete sheds fine particles constantly. A sealed floor stops it, keeping the garage and anything stored in it noticeably cleaner.
- **Moisture control.** Orlando sits on sandy soil over a shallow water table, and over 90 percent of area homes use slab-on-grade construction. Bare slabs let ground vapor pass through freely. A primed epoxy system resists that transfer, reducing mold risk and protecting stored items.
- **Easier cleaning.** Sweep and mop. No more scrubbing stains out of raw concrete.
- **Better light.** Gloss finishes reflect overhead lighting back into the room, brightening a space that is typically dim.

Standard epoxy can yellow under sustained UV exposure, and hot tires from a vehicle that just came off I-4 on a July afternoon can leave marks on lower-grade clears. Both have solutions: a polyaspartic or UV-stable topcoat handles yellowing, and a quality clear resists tire pickup. ## The Garage Floor Epoxy Process

The coating is the visible result. The preparation underneath determines whether the floor lasts two years or two decades.

### Concrete Preparation

Everything comes off the slab first. The contractor inspects for cracks, spalling, moisture stains, and remnants of old coatings or sealers. In Orlando, moisture testing is not optional. The water table here commonly sits 2 to 4 feet below grade, and after a summer downpour, sub-slab humidity can approach saturation. An ASTM F2170 relative humidity probe confirms whether the concrete falls below the 75-percent threshold that manufacturers require before coating.

Cracks get filled with a semi-rigid compound. The crew diamond-grinds the full surface, removing the top skin and exposing the pores the primer needs for a proper bond.

### Epoxy Application

Primer soaks into the opened concrete and bridges to the base coat. The base coat carries the color and the majority of the system's thickness.

If you choose flake or chip broadcast — the most popular residential option in this market — the crew scatters it into the wet base by hand. Flake adds texture, hides minor slab imperfections, and provides traction when the floor gets wet from rain tracked off the driveway.

### Curing and Final Coat

A clear topcoat locks the decorative layer in place and provides abrasion and chemical resistance. Standard epoxy needs 24 to 72 hours before walking on it and five to seven days before parking. Central Florida humidity can stretch those windows, and contractors here schedule around the forecast accordingly. November through January, when humidity consistently drops below 70 percent, is the most reliable installation window.
