## Window Tinting Questions Answered

### What are the window tint laws in Arizona?

Arizona Revised Statute 28-959.01 requires front driver and passenger windows to allow at least 33 percent visible light transmission (VLT), with a 3 percent tolerance. Back side windows and the rear windshield have no VLT minimum as long as the vehicle has dual side mirrors. Reflectivity is capped at 35 percent on front and rear side windows, and no reflective tint is allowed on the windshield. These rules apply equally to sedans, SUVs, and trucks — Arizona makes no distinction by vehicle type. Violation is a traffic offense, and officers verify VLT with a tint meter during stops.

### How long does window tint last?

Quality automotive film installed by a professional lasts 10 to 15 years under typical use. Ceramic film tends toward the higher end because it resists fading and degradation better than dyed or carbon alternatives. Dyed film may last 5 to 7 years before fading to purple, especially on south- and west-facing glass that takes constant sun in the Goodyear area. Residential and commercial film has a similar or longer lifespan depending on the product and exposure.

### Can window tint be removed?

Yes. A professional removes tint by heating the film to soften the adhesive and peeling it off the glass. Any remaining adhesive residue is cleaned with a solvent. The process takes one to three hours depending on how many windows have film and how old the adhesive is. Older film with degraded adhesive is harder to strip and costs more. Expect to pay $100 to $200 for full-vehicle tint removal before new film goes on.

### Does window tinting reduce heat?

It depends on the film type. Dyed film absorbs some solar energy and provides modest heat reduction — roughly 15 to 30 percent of infrared heat rejection. Carbon film performs better at 35 to 50 percent. Ceramic film leads at 50 to 70 percent heat rejection. In Goodyear, where summer temperatures exceed 105 degrees and parked vehicles can reach interior temperatures well above that, the difference between film types is not academic — it determines whether you can comfortably get into the car. The [ceramic vs. carbon vs. dyed guide](/guides/ceramic-vs-carbon-vs-dyed-tint/) covers the comparison in detail.

### What is ceramic window tint?

Ceramic tint uses nano-ceramic particles embedded in the film to block infrared heat. Unlike metallic film, it does not interfere with cell phone signals, GPS, or Bluetooth. Unlike dyed film, it does not fade or turn purple over time. Ceramic film can reject 50 to 70 percent of infrared heat while still allowing a relatively high percentage of visible light through, which makes it possible to run a lighter shade on front windows for Arizona's 33 percent VLT requirement and still block the majority of heat. It costs more than dyed or carbon — typically $200 to $400 for a full vehicle — but it is the film Goodyear-area installers recommend most for the climate.

### How dark can I tint my windows?

In Arizona, the front side windows must let at least 33 percent of light through. Beyond that, it is your choice. Back side windows and the rear windshield can be as dark as you want, including full limo tint at 5 percent VLT, as long as you have side mirrors on both sides. Keep in mind that darker is not necessarily better for heat rejection — ceramic film at a moderate shade rejects more heat than dyed film at a very dark shade. The shade controls privacy and appearance; the film chemistry controls heat performance.

### How long does window tint take to install?

Automotive tint on a standard vehicle takes two to four hours. Larger vehicles with more glass — full-size SUVs and crew-cab trucks — take longer. After installation, the film cures over several days. Some haziness and small water pockets during the first week are normal and clear as the adhesive dries completely. Your installer will tell you when it is safe to roll the windows down and when to clean the glass for the first time.

### Does window tinting block UV rays?

Quality window tint blocks up to 99 percent of ultraviolet radiation regardless of how dark or light the shade is. That includes both UVA (which penetrates glass naturally and causes skin aging) and UVB. In Goodyear, where the UV index reaches Extreme levels (10 to 11) from April through September, UV protection through tinted glass is a meaningful health consideration for daily commuters. It also protects dashboards, leather seats, and interior trim from cracking and fading.
