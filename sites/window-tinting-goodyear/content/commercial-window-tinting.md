## What Is Commercial Window Tinting?

Commercial window film is applied to the interior or exterior surface of building glass. The film is engineered for flat glass in commercial frames and rated for the thermal loads and glass types found in storefronts, curtain walls, and office partitions. Products range from solar control films that reject heat and glare to security films that hold glass in the frame after impact.

The installation process scales with the building. A single storefront might take a few hours. A multi-story office building may require scaffolding or lift equipment and span several days. The installer evaluates glass type, orientation, and existing coatings before recommending a product.

## Why Choose Commercial Window Tinting

What it does for a Goodyear business:

- **Reduces energy costs.** Solar heat gain through windows is one of the largest cooling loads in a commercial building. Quality film can reject 35 to 70 percent of solar energy, reducing HVAC run time and lowering utility bills in a market where cooling accounts for a significant share of operating expense.
- **Cuts glare.** West-facing offices and retail spaces become productive after midday. Employees working on screens, customers browsing merchandise, and visitors in lobbies all benefit from reduced solar glare.
- **Adds security.** Security film holds shattered glass in the frame, resisting break-in attempts, storm debris, and accidental impact. It does not make glass unbreakable, but it creates a barrier that buys time.
- **Provides privacy.** Frosted and reflective films control visibility into ground-floor offices and meeting rooms without blocking natural light entirely.
- **Protects interiors.** UV radiation fades flooring, furniture, displays, and merchandise. Commercial film blocks up to 99 percent of UV.

## Choose the Right Film for Commercial Window Tinting

Commercial applications use the same film categories as residential, but the selection criteria shift toward energy performance and building codes.

### Dyed Film

Rarely the first choice for commercial projects. Dyed film provides privacy and modest heat absorption at a low price, but its limited heat rejection (15 to 30 percent) underperforms in Goodyear's climate where the goal is real energy savings. Best suited for interior partitions where aesthetics matter more than thermal performance.

### Carbon Film

The workhorse for most commercial installations. Carbon film rejects 35 to 50 percent of solar heat, maintains a consistent appearance over time, and does not interfere with electronic signals. For offices, retail spaces, and mixed-use buildings, carbon delivers a solid return on the energy savings without the premium cost of ceramic.

### Ceramic Film

The premium option for glass-heavy facades, west-facing exposures, and buildings where maximum heat rejection justifies the higher cost per square foot. Ceramic film rejects 50 to 70 percent of infrared energy while allowing more visible light through than darker carbon products. For a building where natural light is a design feature, ceramic preserves it while blocking the heat behind it.
