Understanding **Arizona window tint laws** is the first step before getting any vehicle tinted in Goodyear. The legal limits are straightforward — Arizona is more permissive than many states — but getting them wrong means a traffic citation and the cost of re-tinting to comply. This guide covers the specific VLT percentages, where they apply on the vehicle, enforcement, and what the law means for your film and shade choices.

This information applies to all vehicles registered or driven in Arizona, regardless of whether the tint was applied in-state or elsewhere.

*Last updated: November 2026. Based on Arizona Revised Statute 28-959.01 and current enforcement guidance.*

## Quick Summary

Arizona law sets one main number: **33 percent VLT** (visible light transmission) on the front driver and passenger windows. That means at least 33 percent of outside light must pass through the glass. There is a 3 percent tolerance built in, so a measurement of 30 percent or above will pass a meter test during a traffic stop.

Rear side windows and the rear windshield have **no VLT restriction** — you can go as dark as you want, including full limo tint, provided the vehicle has side mirrors on both sides.

The windshield allows a non-reflective tint strip along the top, above the AS-1 line (the manufacturer's mark that indicates the top few inches of the windshield). No tint below that line.

Reflectivity is capped at **35 percent** on front and rear side windows. Mirrored and highly metallic tints are not permitted.

Arizona does not distinguish between sedans and SUVs — the same rules apply to all vehicle types.

## Key Differences

Here is where Arizona's law differs from neighboring states and common misconceptions:

**No rear window restriction.** Many states set a minimum VLT for rear glass. Arizona does not, as long as dual side mirrors are present. This gives vehicle owners significant freedom on rear privacy and heat rejection.

**No vehicle-type distinction.** Some states allow SUVs and trucks to run darker front windows than sedans. Arizona treats all vehicles the same: 33 percent VLT on the front, no limit on the rear.

**No annual inspection.** Arizona has no vehicle safety inspection requirement. There is no mandatory tint check after installation. Enforcement happens during traffic stops, where an officer may use a handheld tint meter to verify VLT if the windows appear out of compliance.

**Medical exemptions.** Arizona allows darker front window tint with a medical exemption signed by a licensed physician. The exemption must be carried in the vehicle. If you have a medical condition that requires reduced light exposure, talk to your doctor and your installer about the process.

**Reflectivity limit.** The 35 percent reflectivity cap eliminates highly mirrored and metallic films from front and rear side windows. Modern carbon and ceramic films are not reflective in this sense — they reject heat through absorption and infrared filtering, not mirror-like reflection. This is rarely an issue with current film products.

**What the law does not regulate.** Arizona tint law applies to vehicle glass. It does not regulate residential or commercial window film. Homeowners and business owners can apply any film they choose to building glass, subject only to HOA rules and energy code compliance for commercial buildings.

| Front side windows | 33% (with 3% tolerance) | 35% | Same for all vehicle types |

## How This Affects Your Decision

For Goodyear vehicle owners, Arizona's law creates a practical framework:

**Front windows: choose film for performance, not darkness.** At 33 percent VLT, the front windows let a moderate amount of light through. The shade is noticeable but not dark. What matters at this level is the film chemistry — a ceramic film at 33 percent VLT rejects far more heat than a dyed film at the same shade. Since you cannot go darker on the front, investing in ceramic where it counts (heat rejection per dollar) makes the most difference.

**Rear windows: match darkness to your needs.** No legal limit means you can run 5 percent limo tint across the back if privacy and maximum heat rejection are the priorities, or keep it moderate for better nighttime visibility. For families with children in rear-facing car seats, a darker rear window reduces sun exposure without any legal complication.

**SUV and truck owners get the same flexibility.** Arizona's uniform rules mean the full-size trucks and SUVs that dominate Maricopa County traffic — nearly half of new Arizona vehicle purchases are SUVs and crossovers — follow the same 33 percent front / no-limit rear formula as every sedan on the road.

**Enforcement is real but not aggressive.** Officers typically look for obviously non-compliant front windows — dark, reflective, or clearly below 33 percent. A properly installed film at or near the legal limit does not draw attention. If you are stopped, the officer may test with a meter, and if the reading falls at 30 percent or above (accounting for the 3 percent tolerance), you pass.

**The installer handles compliance.** Any qualified Goodyear-area tint shop knows Arizona's limits and will recommend shades that fall within legal bounds. If you have a specific shade in mind, the installer can verify it against the statute before cutting film.
