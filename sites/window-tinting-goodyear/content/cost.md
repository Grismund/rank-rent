The short answer on **window tinting cost Goodyear** residents can expect: most automotive jobs come in between $150 and $400 when a professional handles the work. Residential film is priced by the square foot. Commercial projects scale with the building. The rest of this page explains what moves your number within those ranges.

These figures reflect what Goodyear-area installers are quoting in the current market. Your specific vehicle, windows, film choice, and the installer you select will shift it.

## Window Tinting Price Ranges

### Sedan or Coupe

The most straightforward automotive job. Smaller glass area, fewer windows, and faster installation. Budget **$150 to $350** for a full set of side and rear windows. The low end is dyed film; the high end is ceramic. Front side windows at Arizona's 33 percent VLT limit and darker film on the rear glass is the typical configuration.

Setup time is similar across vehicle sizes, so a sedan costs slightly less per window than a larger vehicle, but the base labor charge keeps the floor from dropping much lower.

### SUV or Truck

Larger glass area means more film and more time. SUVs and crossovers — which make up nearly half of Arizona's new vehicle purchases — have wide rear windows, cargo area glass, and sometimes a panoramic roof. Full-size trucks with crew cabs add four full-size rear doors.

Budget **$200 to $500** for a standard SUV or truck. Full ceramic on a large SUV with rear cargo glass and a panoramic roof can reach the upper end of that range. Costs vary by installer.

### Home Windows

Residential window film is priced per square foot, typically **$5 to $15 installed**. A standard Goodyear home with 10 to 15 windows might cost $1,500 to $4,000 for a full installation. The number, size, and accessibility of windows drive the total. Second-story glass that requires ladder work costs more per window than ground-level sliders.

For commercial projects, see the [commercial window tinting page](/commercial-window-tinting/). Pricing runs $6 to $20 per square foot depending on film type and project complexity.

## Why Window Tinting Prices Vary

Five factors, roughly in order of impact:

**Film type.** This is the biggest variable on most jobs. Dyed film is the least expensive. Ceramic is the most. The difference between the cheapest and most expensive film on the same vehicle can be $200 or more. The [film comparison section below](#film-types-and-cost-differences) lays out the details.

**Vehicle size and glass area.** More glass means more material and more labor. A compact sedan has less than half the tintable glass area of a full-size SUV with a panoramic roof. Pricing scales accordingly.

**Number of windows.** Some vehicle owners tint only the rear windows. Others do the full set including a windshield visor strip. Each additional window is an incremental cost.

**Existing tint removal.** If the vehicle already has film that needs to come off — faded, bubbling, or the wrong shade — stripping it adds time and cost before the new film goes on. Removal on a full vehicle can add $100 to $200 depending on how stubbornly the old adhesive holds.

**Installer and location.** Pricing varies between shops. A tint shop with high overhead in a premium retail space may quote higher than a mobile installer who comes to your location. Neither is inherently better — what matters is the quality of the work and the warranty behind it.

## Film Types and Cost Differences

The film you choose determines both the price and the performance of the tint. Here is how the three main categories compare for automotive applications:

**Dyed film: $50 to $150 per vehicle.** The entry-level option. Dye absorbs visible light and provides privacy. Heat rejection is modest (15 to 30 percent). The film fades over time. Best for tight budgets or vehicles that do not spend much time in direct sun — which is a narrow category in Goodyear.

**Carbon film: $150 to $250 per vehicle.** Mid-range. Carbon particles block more infrared heat (35 to 50 percent rejection) without fading to purple. Consistent appearance over the life of the film. The practical choice for daily drivers who want real heat reduction without the premium cost.

**Ceramic film: $200 to $400 per vehicle.** Premium. Nano-ceramic particles reject 50 to 70 percent of infrared heat while allowing more visible light through than darker films at similar performance levels. No signal interference, no fading, no purple shift. The film Goodyear installers recommend most, and the one that earns its price in a climate with 300-plus sunny days and a UV index that hits Extreme.

Full ceramic on a larger vehicle (SUV, truck with crew cab) can reach $300 to $600. The [ceramic vs. carbon vs. dyed tint guide](/guides/ceramic-vs-carbon-vs-dyed-tint/) covers the performance comparison in detail.
