Wondering **what junk removal won't take** in Port St. Lucie? Most household items are fair game — furniture, appliances, electronics, construction debris, yard waste. But certain materials are restricted or outright prohibited because they pose health, environmental, or legal risks that standard haulers are not equipped to handle.

This guide lists the common exclusions and tells you where to bring each type of restricted material in the Port St. Lucie area.

*Last updated: November 2026. Disposal options reflect current St. Lucie County, Martin County, and Indian River County programs.*

## What You Need to Know

Junk removal companies haul non-hazardous solid waste. The line between accepted and refused items comes down to whether the material requires specialized containment, certified handling, or disposal at a licensed hazardous waste facility. Standard haulers carry general liability insurance and use standard waste disposal permits — they are not licensed for hazmat.

The good news: the items on the exclusion list make up a small fraction of what most homeowners need to get rid of. The vast majority of a typical cleanout — furniture, boxes, clothes, toys, appliances, drywall, lumber — goes on the truck without issue.

## Items Junk Removal Haulers Will Not Take

**Hazardous chemicals and household products:**

- Paint (latex and oil-based)
- Paint thinner, turpentine, and solvents
- Pesticides, herbicides, and fertilizers
- Pool chemicals (chlorine, muriatic acid)
- Cleaning products containing bleach, ammonia, or acid
- Aerosol cans that are not empty

**Automotive fluids:**

- Motor oil and transmission fluid
- Brake fluid and power steering fluid
- Antifreeze and coolant
- Gasoline, diesel, and kerosene

**Batteries and electronics with restricted materials:**

- Car batteries (lead-acid)
- Rechargeable lithium-ion batteries (fire risk during compaction)
- Fluorescent bulbs and tubes (contain mercury)
- CFL bulbs

**Medical and biological waste:**

- Sharps (needles, syringes, lancets)
- Prescription medications
- Biological waste and contaminated materials
- Medical devices with batteries or radioactive components

**Construction hazards:**

- Asbestos-containing materials (older insulation, floor tiles, pipe wrap)
- Lead-based paint (chips, dust, or painted surfaces being demolished)
- Contaminated soil

**Other restricted items:**

- Propane tanks (all sizes)
- Fire extinguishers
- Ammunition and explosives
- Tires (some haulers accept a limited number; ask first)
- Soil and dirt (some haulers accept clean fill; ask first)

## Where to Take Restricted Items in Port St. Lucie

**St. Lucie County Household Hazardous Waste Facility** at 6120 Glades Cut-Off Road, Fort Pierce, FL 34981 — accepts paint, chemicals, pesticides, motor oil, antifreeze, batteries, fluorescent bulbs, and electronics at no charge with proof of St. Lucie County residency. This is the primary drop-off point for most restricted household materials.

**Martin County HazMobile Program** — Martin County residents (Stuart, Jensen Beach, Palm City) can use the HazMobile collection events for household hazardous waste. Contact Martin County Solid Waste at (772) 288-5772 for the current schedule.

**Indian River County Landfill** at 1325 74th Ave SW, Vero Beach — the county operates five Customer Convenience Centers in Roseland, Winter Beach, Gifford, Oslo, and Fellsmere for residential drop-off including certain restricted materials.

**Automotive fluids:** AutoZone, O'Reilly Auto Parts, and most auto service shops accept used motor oil, transmission fluid, and car batteries at no charge.

**Propane tanks:** Blue Rhino exchange locations at Home Depot, Lowe's, and gas stations accept expired or damaged tanks during exchange.

**Medications:** CVS, Walgreens, and Publix pharmacies participate in drug take-back programs. The St. Lucie County Sheriff's Office also maintains a prescription drop box.

**Sharps:** St. Lucie County provides sharps containers and accepts filled containers at the hazardous waste facility. Many pharmacies accept sealed sharps containers as well.

## What This Means for Your Cleanout

If your cleanout includes any of the items above, separate them before the hauler arrives. Set the restricted items aside and take them to the appropriate facility. Everything else goes on the truck.

For most households, the restricted items amount to a few cans of old paint in the garage, a container of motor oil, and maybe some batteries. A single trip to the county hazardous waste facility on Glades Cut-Off Road handles all of it at no cost. The hauler takes care of the other 95 percent.

If you are not sure whether a specific item qualifies, ask the hauler when you call for a quote. They deal with this question daily and will tell you on the spot what goes on the truck and what does not.
