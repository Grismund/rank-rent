## What Is Construction Debris Removal?

A hauler brings a truck to the job site, loads the debris, and takes it to the appropriate disposal facility. This is the alternative to renting a dumpster — particularly useful when the renovation is already finished and the debris is piled in the garage or driveway, or when the volume does not justify a multi-day dumpster rental.

Construction debris is heavier per cubic yard than household junk. Concrete, tile, and wet drywall are dense, and the weight affects both how much fits in a truck and where it can be legally disposed of. Haulers who work the Port St. Lucie area know the disposal requirements at the St. Lucie County landfill on Glades Cut-Off Road and the Martin County Transfer and Recycling Facility on Busch Street in Palm City.

## Construction Debris Removal Step by Step

### Booking and Estimates

Call **[PHONE]** or submit a request. Describe the type of material, the approximate volume, and where it is located (inside the house, in the garage, on the driveway, in the yard). Photos help the hauler size the job over the phone. Larger or mixed-material jobs may require a free on-site visit.

### Pickup Day

The crew arrives with a truck rated for the weight. Construction debris loads differently than household junk — concrete goes on the floor of the truck, drywall stacks, and lumber fits around the edges. Haulers who work renovation sites routinely stage and load efficiently to avoid unnecessary trips. You do not need to sort or organize the pile beforehand.

### Responsible Disposal

Clean wood, metal framing, and concrete are separated for recycling where facilities accept them. Scrap metal goes to recyclers like Atlantic Coast Recycling in Fort Pierce. Lumber and wood waste may be diverted from the landfill depending on condition and volume. Mixed debris that cannot be separated goes to the county landfill.

If your renovation produces reusable materials — cabinets in good condition, unbroken tile, solid-core doors — the Habitat for Humanity ReStore in Fort Pierce accepts building materials and offers free donation pickup at (772) 249-0792.

## Construction Debris Removal Cost in Port St. Lucie

Construction debris is heavier than household junk, so pricing reflects the weight as well as the volume. Costs vary by hauler, but typical ranges in the Port St. Lucie area:

- **Quarter truckload** (small bathroom demo or flooring tear-out): $150 to $250
- **Half truckload** (kitchen or bathroom renovation): $250 to $400
- **Three-quarter truckload**: $350 to $500
- **Full truckload** (large renovation or multiple rooms): $450 to $700

Concrete and masonry are the heaviest materials and may push the price above these ranges if the volume is large. The hauler quotes the total before work begins.
