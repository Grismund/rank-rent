## Typical Pressure Washing Costs

### Driveway Washing

The most common standalone request. A standard two-car concrete driveway of 400 to 600 square feet runs **$100 to $200** at $0.10 to $0.25 per square foot. The low end is a driveway in fair condition needing routine algae removal. The high end is a heavily stained surface requiring pre-treatment for oil spots, red clay, or deep biological growth.

Longer driveways, circular drives, and brick paver surfaces push toward the upper end of the range. Sealing after the wash — which extends the clean appearance — is an additional service priced separately.

### House Exterior Washing

A full house wash covers siding, trim, soffits, and fascia from ground to roofline. For a typical 1,500- to 2,500-square-foot Conroe home, expect **$225 to $600** at $0.15 to $0.40 per square foot. Vinyl and Hardie board are on the lower end. Brick with deep mortar joints and stucco with heavy biological growth are on the higher end.

Multi-story homes cost more because of the access equipment required. Homes under dense tree canopy with extensive mold buildup may need a stronger solution and longer dwell time, adding to the labor. The [house washing page](/house-washing/) covers the process in detail.

### Full Property Package

Many Conroe homeowners bundle multiple surfaces into a single visit. A typical package includes the driveway, house exterior, and one or two additional surfaces — a deck, patio, or fence. Bundling generally costs less than scheduling each service separately because the contractor is already on site with equipment running.

A representative full-property package for a mid-sized Conroe home:

- Driveway: $100–$200
- House exterior: $225–$600
- Deck or patio (200–400 sq ft): $100–$250
- Fence (100–200 linear ft): $100–$400

**Total range: $525–$1,450**, depending on size, materials, and condition. Commercial properties — office exteriors, strip mall sidewalks, restaurant patios — are priced individually based on scope and typically fall between $850 and $3,500.

## What Affects Pressure Washing Pricing?

Five factors, roughly in order of impact:

**Surface type and size.** A 500-square-foot driveway is a different job than 2,500 square feet of siding on a two-story home. Each surface type carries a different per-square-foot rate, and total square footage is the primary driver of the final number.

**Biological growth severity.** A house that gets cleaned annually is a faster, cheaper job than one that has gone three years. Heavy mold, algae, and mildew require stronger solutions, longer dwell times, and sometimes multiple passes. The first cleaning after years of neglect always costs more than the maintenance cleanings that follow.

**Surface material.** Vinyl siding washes faster than brick with deep mortar joints. Poured concrete is simpler than pavers with sand joints. Composite decks clean differently than natural wood. The material determines the technique, which determines the time, which determines the price.

**Access and height.** Single-story homes are simpler than two-story. Ground-level patios are simpler than elevated decks. Any surface that requires ladder work, extension equipment, or additional safety measures adds to the quote.

**Wastewater management.** Proper wastewater capture and disposal — required under the Clean Water Act — adds modest cost to the job. Contractors who cut this corner are cutting a legal requirement, not a legitimate savings.

## Comparing Pressure and Soft Wash Methods

Conroe contractors use two methods, often on the same property:

**Pressure washing** uses high-pressure water (2,000–4,000 PSI) to blast dirt, algae, and stains from hard surfaces. It is the standard method for concrete driveways, sidewalks, and stone surfaces. The equipment does the work — no chemicals are needed for routine concrete cleaning, though a pre-treatment helps on severe staining.

**Soft washing** uses low pressure (under 1,000 PSI) combined with a cleaning solution that kills biological growth chemically before rinsing it away. It is the standard method for roofs, siding, painted surfaces, and wood. High pressure on these surfaces causes damage — water driven behind siding, granules stripped from shingles, wood fibers torn.

A qualified contractor selects the right method for each surface. If someone quotes one approach for the entire property, ask questions.
