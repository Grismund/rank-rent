Conroe Pressure Washing connects homeowners with independent local contractors who handle **deck cleaning Conroe** properties need more frequently than any other exterior surface. East Texas humidity, persistent rainfall, and the organic debris from the surrounding Piney Woods canopy create conditions where wood decks, composite surfaces, and stone patios develop mold, mildew, and algae in as little as three to six months.

## About Deck and Patio Cleaning

Deck and patio cleaning uses lower pressure than driveway or concrete work — typically 1,000 to 1,500 PSI for wood and 1,500 to 2,500 PSI for composite and stone. The reduced pressure protects the material while still removing the biological growth that Conroe's climate produces year-round. A cleaning solution is applied before rinsing to kill algae and mildew at the root, not just wash them off the surface.

Wood decks in this area need cleaning every 6 to 12 months. Composite and stone patios can go slightly longer — 12 to 18 months — but shaded patios under tree canopy often match the wood timeline. The cleaning cycle is the shortest of any residential surface because decks and patios are horizontal, exposed to rain and debris, and frequently shaded — the three conditions that accelerate biological growth the fastest.

## Advantages of Deck and Patio Cleaning

**Safety comes first.** A deck coated in algae or mildew is a slip hazard, particularly when wet. Conroe receives 47 inches of rain annually, and a deck that stays damp under tree shade develops a slick biofilm that is genuinely dangerous. Children, elderly family members, and guests at a backyard gathering are all at risk on an uncleaned deck.

**Preserving the wood.** Mold and mildew are not cosmetic problems on a wood deck — they are structural ones. These organisms feed on the cellulose in the wood, breaking down fibers over time. A deck that goes years without cleaning in this climate will show soft spots, graying, and premature rot that shortens its useful life by years. Regular cleaning stops that degradation before it starts.

**Composite decks need cleaning too.** Composite materials resist rot, but they still collect algae, mold, and staining. The textured surface that makes composite slip-resistant also traps organic material in its grain pattern. Cleaning restores the appearance and prevents the buildup from becoming permanent.

**Stain and seal preparation.** If the deck is due for staining or sealing — recommended every one to two years for wood decks in this climate — cleaning is the mandatory first step. No stain adheres properly to a surface covered in mildew, pollen, and embedded dirt. Contractors often schedule the cleaning and staining as a combined job.

## The Deck and Patio Cleaning Process

### Surface Assessment

The contractor inspects the surface material, checks for loose boards, protruding fasteners, and areas of soft or damaged wood. On patios, they evaluate the mortar or joint condition between pavers or flagstone. The assessment determines the appropriate pressure, nozzle, and cleaning solution.

### Pre-Treatment

A cleaning solution is applied to the entire surface and allowed to dwell for 10 to 15 minutes. For wood, this is typically a sodium percarbonate solution (oxygen bleach) that kills biological growth without damaging the wood fibers or altering the color. For composite and stone, sodium hypochlorite solutions at calibrated concentrations are common. The dwell time is what does the actual killing — the pressure wash that follows is primarily removal.

### Washing and Rinse

The contractor works with the grain on wood surfaces, using a fan-tip nozzle at consistent distance to avoid gouging. Composite and stone surfaces are cleaned in overlapping passes. Railings, spindles, and stair treads get individual attention. The entire surface is rinsed clean, and the difference is visible immediately.

After cleaning, wood decks benefit from 24 to 48 hours of drying time before any stain or sealant is applied. The contractor can advise on the best product for the wood species and exposure conditions on your property.
