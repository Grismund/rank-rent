## Local Considerations

**Wastewater management.** Harris County storm drains in Spring flow untreated to Spring Creek and Cypress Creek. Wash water containing soap, degreaser, oil, or biological material counts as illegal discharge under the Clean Water Act. Fines can reach $10,000 per day for stormwater discharge violations in the Houston area. Contractors in our network capture and manage wastewater appropriately.

**Dual-county jurisdiction.** Spring is unincorporated and straddles Harris and Montgomery counties. There is no municipal pressure-washing permit — county-level business licensing applies. HOA-managed communities like Gleannloch Farms and Imperial Oaks may impose their own vendor registration and insurance requirements that contractors must meet.

**Water restrictions.** Spring-area water comes from various MUDs in both counties, each with its own drought contingency plan. Montgomery County MUDs enforce tiered drought stages, and Harris County MUDs implement similar restrictions during drought conditions. Pressure washing is typically classified as non-irrigation activity but should be confirmed with the local MUD during active drought stages.

**No special licensing.** Texas does not require a specific contractor's license for pressure washing. A general business registration and sales tax permit from the Texas Comptroller cover the requirements.
