Comparing **soft wash vs pressure wash** methods for your Conroe property? Both clean exterior surfaces, but they work in fundamentally different ways and are designed for different materials. Using the wrong method on the wrong surface causes damage that costs more to fix than the cleaning was worth. This guide explains the difference so you can evaluate what a contractor recommends before they start.

*Last updated: November 2026. Based on manufacturer specifications and trade sources. For your specific surfaces, talk to a local contractor.*

## What You Need to Know

**Pressure washing** uses water delivered at high pressure — typically 2,000 to 4,000 PSI for residential work — to physically blast dirt, algae, stains, and biological growth off hard surfaces. The force of the water does the work. It is effective, fast, and appropriate for surfaces that can take the impact: poured concrete, natural stone, brick, and uncoated masonry.

**Soft washing** uses water delivered at low pressure — typically under 1,000 PSI, comparable to a garden hose — combined with a cleaning solution that kills algae, mold, mildew, and lichen chemically. The solution does the work, not the water. The low-pressure rinse that follows simply removes the dead material. Soft washing is designed for surfaces that high pressure would damage: asphalt shingle roofs, vinyl siding, painted wood, stucco, composite decking, and similar materials.

The distinction matters more in Conroe than in many other places. East Texas humidity averages 73 percent year-round, and the Piney Woods canopy surrounding the city drops organic debris onto every surface. Biological growth is not an occasional problem here — it is a constant. That means homeowners need exterior cleaning more frequently, and using the right method on each surface prevents the cumulative damage that comes from repeated cleanings with the wrong approach.

## Side-by-Side Comparison

**PSI range.** Pressure washing: 2,000–4,000 PSI. Soft washing: under 1,000 PSI. The gap is significant. At 3,000 PSI, water can strip paint, gouge wood, crack stucco, and drive moisture behind siding panels. At 500 PSI with a cleaning solution, none of that happens.

**How it cleans.** Pressure washing relies on mechanical force — the water impact dislodges and removes material from the surface. Soft washing relies on chemical action — the solution kills biological growth at the cellular level, and the rinse carries away the dead material. This is why soft washing produces longer-lasting results on roofs and siding: it kills the algae roots rather than just knocking the visible growth off the surface.

**Appropriate surfaces.** Here is where the two methods divide:

| Surface | Recommended Method |
|---------|-------------------|
| Concrete driveway | Pressure washing |
| Concrete sidewalk | Pressure washing |
| Stone patio | Pressure washing |
| Brick (unpainted) | Pressure washing |
| Vinyl siding | Soft washing |
| Hardie board siding | Soft washing |
| Painted wood | Soft washing |
| Stucco | Soft washing |
| Asphalt shingle roof | Soft washing |
| Tile roof | Soft washing |
| Wood deck | Soft washing (low-pressure) |
| Composite deck | Soft washing |
| Wood fence | Soft washing or low-pressure |

**Cost.** Soft washing tends to cost slightly more per square foot than straight pressure washing because the cleaning solution is a significant cost component. Roof soft washing runs $0.20 to $0.50 per square foot in the Conroe area, while driveway pressure washing runs $0.10 to $0.25. The [cost guide](/cost/) has the full breakdown.

**Durability of results.** Soft washing generally keeps surfaces cleaner longer because the chemical treatment kills biological growth at its root structure. A pressure-washed driveway may show algae regrowth in a few months as surviving organisms recolonize. A soft-washed surface stays cleaner because the organisms were killed, not just displaced. In Conroe's high-humidity environment, this difference is noticeable.

**Risk of damage.** Pressure washing carries the higher risk. A wand at 3,500 PSI in the wrong hands can strip siding, etch concrete unevenly, damage window seals, and void roof warranties. Soft washing carries minimal risk when the cleaning solution is properly diluted — the main concern is protecting landscaping from chemical runoff, which responsible contractors handle with pre-rinsing and post-rinsing of plant beds.

## What This Means for Your Project

Most residential pressure washing jobs in Conroe use both methods on the same property. A typical visit looks like this:

1. **Driveway and sidewalks:** Pressure washing at 3,000–3,500 PSI with a surface cleaner attachment for even results. Pre-treatment for oil spots or deep biological growth.
2. **House siding, trim, and soffits:** Soft washing with a cleaning solution applied at low pressure, dwelling for 10–15 minutes, then rinsed from top to bottom.
3. **Roof (if included):** Soft washing exclusively — low-pressure chemical application followed by a gentle rinse working with the shingle lap direction.
4. **Deck or patio:** Soft washing for wood and composite at 1,000–1,500 PSI. Stone patios may get higher pressure depending on the material.
5. **Fence:** Soft washing or low-pressure washing for wood. Vinyl fences handle moderate pressure.

A contractor who uses the same high-pressure setting on every surface is not adjusting for the material, and that is a warning sign. Ask what PSI they plan to use on the siding and roof. If the answer is the same as the driveway, find someone else.

The cleaning solution used in soft washing matters too. The standard is sodium hypochlorite diluted to the appropriate concentration for the surface material. Some contractors use proprietary blends. What you want to avoid is a contractor who uses no solution at all and simply sprays water at low pressure — that is not soft washing, it is an ineffective rinse.
