## About Driveway Pressure Washing

Driveway pressure washing uses commercial-grade equipment delivering 3,000 to 4,000 PSI through a surface cleaner attachment — a rotating disc that distributes pressure evenly across the concrete. This approach eliminates the visible striping that a handheld wand creates when used alone. The result is uniform cleaning across the entire surface.

Most Conroe driveways are poured concrete, with some paver installations in newer subdivisions like Grand Central Park. Both surfaces respond well to pressure washing, though pavers require attention to joint sand that can be displaced by high-pressure water. A contractor who works this area knows the difference and adjusts accordingly.

Plan on driveway cleaning every 12 to 18 months in the Conroe area. Properties under dense tree canopy or near Lake Conroe may need it annually.

## Advantages of Driveway Pressure Washing

**Safety.** Algae-covered concrete is slippery, especially when wet. Conroe receives 47 inches of rain per year, and a driveway that has gone a year without cleaning develops a biofilm that becomes hazardous during and after storms.

**Stain removal.** Oil drips from vehicles, red clay tracked in from construction sites and unpaved shoulders, tire marks, and the dark algae streaks fed by East Texas humidity — pressure washing removes all of it. Some oil stains may require a degreaser pre-treatment, but the base cleaning addresses the majority of common driveway staining.

**Longevity.** Algae and mildew are not just surface problems. They produce acids as they metabolize organic material, and those acids slowly etch concrete. Regular cleaning removes the organisms before they cause pitting or surface degradation that shortens the life of the slab.

**Appearance.** A clean driveway is the first thing visitors and potential buyers see. The difference between a pressure-washed driveway and one that has gone untreated is dramatic and immediate.

## The Driveway Pressure Washing Process

### Surface Assessment

The contractor evaluates the driveway's material, condition, and staining. Cracks, spalling, and expansion joint condition are noted. Oil stains are identified for pre-treatment. The assessment determines the pressure setting, nozzle selection, and whether any chemical application is needed before the wash.

### Pre-Treatment

Heavily stained areas — particularly oil spots, rust marks, and concentrated algae growth — receive a targeted pre-treatment solution. For biological growth, a sodium hypochlorite solution kills the algae and mildew before the pressure wash displaces it. For oil, a commercial degreaser breaks down the petroleum so the pressure wash can flush it from the concrete pores.

### Washing and Rinse

The contractor works the surface cleaner across the entire driveway in overlapping passes. Edges, expansion joints, and the apron where the driveway meets the street get additional attention with a wand. The process moves from the highest point to the lowest, directing wastewater toward collection rather than into the storm drain — a Clean Water Act requirement that responsible contractors follow without being asked.

A standard two-car driveway takes 30 to 60 minutes depending on the staining severity.
