The question of **how often pressure wash house Texas** homeowners ask comes down to climate. The national guidance says every one to three years. In Conroe and Montgomery County, the answer is closer to every 12 to 24 months for the house itself, and shorter for other surfaces. East Texas humidity, heavy tree canopy, and nearly 48 inches of annual rainfall compress the cleaning cycle compared to drier regions of the state.

This guide breaks down recommended frequency by surface type, explains what accelerates the timeline, and helps you plan a cleaning schedule that prevents damage without overspending.

*Last updated: November 2026. Based on manufacturer recommendations adjusted for East Texas conditions.*

## What You Need to Know

Pressure washing frequency is not one number for the entire property. Each surface type has its own timeline based on the material, its orientation, and its exposure to moisture and debris. In Conroe, those timelines run shorter than national averages because the climate never gives surfaces a break.

The key factors:

**Humidity.** Conroe averages 73 percent relative humidity year-round. At those levels, moisture clings to surfaces and biological growth — algae, mold, mildew — has the conditions it needs to establish itself continuously. Homes in drier climates get a reprieve during winter. Conroe does not.

**Rainfall.** With 47.9 inches of annual precipitation, surfaces stay wet frequently. Rain itself does not clean — it deposits organic material, keeps surfaces damp, and feeds the biological growth that is already present.

**Tree canopy.** The East Texas Piney Woods surrounding Conroe and the dense canopy in communities like [The Woodlands](/areas/the-woodlands/) drop needles, leaves, pollen, and bark debris year-round. This organic material settles on surfaces and provides nutrients for mold and algae. Homes under heavy canopy need cleaning more frequently than those in open areas.

**Sun exposure.** North-facing walls, shaded driveways, and surfaces under canopy stay damp longer and develop biological growth faster. South-facing surfaces that receive direct sun throughout the day tend to stay cleaner longer. The same house can have two different cleaning timelines depending on which side you are looking at.

## Side-by-Side Comparison

Here is the recommended cleaning frequency for each surface type in the Conroe area, alongside the national standard for context:

| Surface | Conroe Recommendation | National Average |
|---------|----------------------|------------------|
| House exterior | 12–24 months | 12–36 months |
| Driveway | 12–18 months | 12–24 months |
| Deck / Patio | 6–12 months | 12–24 months |
| Roof (soft wash) | 24–36 months | 36–60 months |
| Fence | 12–18 months | 24–36 months |

The gap is widest on decks and patios. National guidance assumes moderate humidity and seasonal weather. In Conroe, a wood deck under tree canopy that stays damp through the summer can develop visible mildew in three to four months. Composite decking lasts longer but still collects algae in the textured grain pattern.

Roofs have the longest cycle because soft washing kills biological growth at the cellular level, providing longer-lasting results than pressure washing alone. Even so, Conroe roofs under heavy canopy may need attention every 18 to 24 months rather than the standard 24 to 36.

**Homes that need cleaning more often** (toward the low end of each range):

- Under heavy tree canopy — especially loblolly pine
- Near Lake Conroe or along greenbelts and creek corridors
- North-facing walls that stay shaded throughout the day
- Properties where sprinkler systems hit the siding regularly
- Older homes with porous siding materials

**Homes that can go longer** (toward the high end):

- In open areas with minimal tree coverage
- South- and west-facing with direct sun exposure
- Newer construction with modern, smooth-surfaced siding
- Properties on higher ground with good air circulation

## What This Means for Your Project

Building a cleaning schedule saves money over time. The first cleaning after years of neglect is always the most expensive because heavy biological growth requires stronger solutions, longer dwell times, and sometimes multiple passes. Maintenance cleanings on a regular cycle are faster, cheaper, and prevent the cumulative damage that algae and mildew cause to surfaces.

A practical approach for a typical Conroe home:

**Annual service (every 12 months):**
- Driveway pressure washing
- Deck or patio cleaning
- Spot-check the house siding — if biological growth is visible, add a house wash

**Every two years:**
- Full house exterior wash (or annually if the home is under heavy canopy)
- Fence cleaning and inspection

**Every two to three years:**
- Roof soft washing
- Gutter exterior cleaning (often bundled with the house wash)

Bundling surfaces into a single contractor visit reduces cost. A contractor already on-site with equipment running can add a fence or deck cleaning to a house wash for less than scheduling them separately. Ask about package pricing when you request a quote.

The [cost guide](/cost/) has pricing by surface type, and the [cost calculator](/pressure-washing-cost-calculator/) gives you a quick budget estimate. The [soft washing vs. pressure washing guide](/guides/soft-washing-vs-pressure-washing/) explains which method is used on each surface and why.

One thing to avoid: cleaning too frequently with the wrong method. Pressure washing a wood deck every three months at high PSI wears the surface down faster than the mildew would. The goal is the right method at the right interval — not maximum frequency.
