## Professional Epoxy Flooring Services

Epoxy is a resin and a hardener mixed on site and spread over concrete in layers. It cures into a hard shell bonded to the slab, so you end up with a seamless floor that shrugs off tires, dropped tools and whatever leaks out of the mower. You will find it in garages, shops, warehouses, restaurant kitchens, showrooms and a fair number of laundry rooms.

The contractors in our network handle the whole thing themselves, from the first look at your slab to the last coat. They bring their own grinders, their own material, their own schedule. You are not dealing with a national call center that subcontracts the job to whoever is free.

### Why Choose Epoxy Flooring?

Oil, gas, brake fluid, that mystery puddle under the water heater. It all wipes up. A broom and an occasional mop with mild soap is the maintenance plan, and a properly installed floor will take years of daily parking without complaint.

There is a local reason too. Summers here are humid, and when the ground under a slab stays wet after a storm, that moisture wants to come up through the concrete. Bare concrete lets it. A coating system with the right vapor-blocking primer slows it down considerably, which protects whatever you store in the garage as much as the floor itself.

One thing worth knowing before you call anyone: the epoxy is the cheap part. Material runs somewhere around $0.30 to $1 a square foot. What you are paying a professional for is the prep. Diamond grinding to open the surface, a moisture test to make sure the slab is ready, crack repair before the first coat. Skip that and the floor peels inside a year. Do it right and it does not.

### Where Epoxy Flooring Works Best

Any clean, sound concrete slab is a candidate. Around New Braunfels the usual jobs look like this:

- **Residential garages.** By far the most common request. Hides tire marks, oil, dropped sockets.
- **Workshops.** Woodworkers and home mechanics like it because sawdust sweeps right off and solvents do not stain it.
- **Warehouses and shops.** Forklift traffic eats through paint. It does not eat through a commercial epoxy system.
- **Showrooms and retail.** A decorative finish that looks like money without the price of tile or polished concrete.
- **Laundry and utility rooms.** Standing water is not a problem.

If a slab has deep structural cracks, an active moisture issue, or an old coating that was never fully removed, a good contractor will tell you so on the first visit and price the repair before the coating. You want the one who tells you that up front.

## How It Works

Three steps. None of them complicated.

### Step 1: Request a Quote

Call or send a request through the site. Tell us where you are, what kind of space it is, roughly how big, and what the floor looks like right now. A local contractor follows up to set a time to come look at it. The visit and the quote are free, and nobody is going to pressure you on the driveway.

### Step 2: Surface Prep and Color Selection

Once you pick a contractor and agree on a price, the real work starts, and it starts with the concrete. The crew grinds or shot-blasts the slab so the epoxy has something to grip. They test for moisture. In this part of Texas that test is not a formality; clay under a slab after a wet spring will push vapor through concrete for weeks.

Cracks get filled. Spalling gets patched. Anything left of an old coating comes off. Somewhere in there you pick your finish: solid color, flake, quartz or metallic. Most installers carry sample boards and will tell you which ones hold up best for how you plan to use the room.

### Step 3: Installation and Curing

Primer, base coat, decorative layer, clear topcoat. A standard two-car garage usually takes a day or two of application.

Then you wait, and the waiting is where people get impatient. Figure 24 to 72 hours before you walk on it and up to a week before you park on it. Heat and humidity both change the cure, so contractors here tend to work around the weather, starting early before a July afternoon turns the garage into a sauna, or holding off during one of the rare hard freezes.
