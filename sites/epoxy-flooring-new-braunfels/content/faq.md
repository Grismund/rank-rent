## Frequently Asked Questions About Epoxy Flooring

### How long does epoxy flooring last?

A professionally applied epoxy garage floor typically lasts 10 to 20 years in a residential setting. Lifespan depends on the quality of concrete preparation, the coating system used, and how much traffic the floor sees. Commercial floors with heavy vehicle or foot traffic may last 5 to 10 years before needing a recoat. Proper surface prep is the single biggest factor in long-term durability.

### Can you epoxy a garage floor yourself?

DIY epoxy kits are available at hardware stores, but the results rarely match professional work. Retail kits apply at 2 to 3 mils thick, compared to 10 to 20 mils for professional-grade systems. Without diamond grinding or shot blasting to profile the concrete, adhesion is weaker and the coating is more likely to peel. Most contractors in the New Braunfels area report that a significant share of their work involves stripping failed DIY coatings and starting over.

### How long does epoxy take to cure?

Standard epoxy coatings require 24 to 72 hours before light foot traffic and five to seven days before parking vehicles on the surface. Cure times vary by product formulation and temperature. Polyaspartic coatings cure faster -- often within 24 hours for full use. Humidity and temperature in the New Braunfels area can affect cure times, especially during the hotter and more humid summer months.

### Is epoxy flooring slippery when wet?

A plain epoxy surface can be slick when wet due to its smooth, glossy finish. Most professional installations include an anti-slip additive in the topcoat or use a decorative flake broadcast that adds texture to the surface. Both options improve traction without sacrificing the look of the floor. If slip resistance is a priority -- for example, in a commercial kitchen or a garage where water enters during rain -- ask your contractor about available anti-slip options.

### Does epoxy flooring crack?

Epoxy itself is a rigid coating, and it can crack if the concrete slab beneath it moves. In New Braunfels, expansive clay soils cause slabs to shift seasonally as moisture levels change. If the slab develops a new crack after coating, the epoxy over that area will likely crack as well. Proper crack repair before installation and choosing a slightly flexible coating system can reduce this risk. Epoxy does not crack on its own from normal foot or vehicle traffic.

### Can epoxy be applied over old concrete?

Yes, epoxy can be applied to existing concrete as long as the surface is properly prepared. The slab needs to be free of old paint, sealers, and contaminants. Cracks and spalling should be repaired, and the surface must be profiled through diamond grinding or shot blasting so the epoxy can bond. A moisture test confirms that vapor transmission rates are within the product's specifications. Older concrete in good structural condition coats just as well as new slabs.

### What is the difference between epoxy and polyaspartic coatings?

Epoxy and polyaspartic are both resin-based coatings applied to concrete, but they differ in cure time, cost, and UV resistance. Epoxy cures in 48 to 72 hours and costs less upfront. Polyaspartic cures in about 24 hours and resists UV yellowing better, but costs roughly 20 to 30% more. Some contractors use a hybrid approach -- an epoxy base coat with a polyaspartic topcoat -- to get the benefits of both. For a full comparison, read our [epoxy vs. polyaspartic guide](/guides/epoxy-vs-polyaspartic-garage-floors/).

### How do you maintain epoxy floors?

Epoxy floors require minimal maintenance. Sweep or dust-mop regularly to remove grit and debris that could scratch the surface over time. For deeper cleaning, mop with warm water and a mild pH-neutral cleaner. Avoid harsh chemicals like bleach or ammonia-based cleaners, which can dull the topcoat. Wipe up oil, gasoline, or chemical spills promptly -- epoxy resists these substances but prolonged contact can affect the finish. With basic upkeep, an epoxy floor maintains its appearance for years.
