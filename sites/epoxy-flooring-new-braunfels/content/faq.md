## Frequently Asked Questions About Epoxy Flooring

### How long does epoxy flooring last?

Ten to twenty years in a residential garage, assuming a professional installed it and the slab was prepped properly. That is a wide range because so much depends on the prep, the system and how hard the floor works. A commercial floor under forklifts might need a recoat in five to ten. If you want one factor to obsess over, make it surface preparation; it decides more about lifespan than anything else.

### Can you epoxy a garage floor yourself?

You can. The kit is at the hardware store. The question is whether you will be happy with it in three years, and usually the answer is no. Retail kits go on at 2 or 3 mils against 10 to 20 for a professional system, and without grinding the concrete first the bond is weak. Contractors around New Braunfels say a meaningful share of their work is stripping a failed weekend job and starting over, which costs more than doing it right the first time.

### How long does epoxy take to cure?

Standard epoxy: 24 to 72 hours before you walk on it, five to seven days before you park. Polyaspartic is much faster, often full use within a day. Temperature and humidity push those numbers around, and New Braunfels summers push them the wrong direction, so a July install may need an extra day or two compared with one in October.

### Is epoxy flooring slippery when wet?

A plain glossy epoxy floor can be, yes. Almost nobody installs a plain glossy epoxy floor. Flake broadcast adds texture, and anti-slip grit can go into the topcoat where water is expected. Both fix the problem without ruining the look. If the garage sees rain tracked in, or the space is a commercial kitchen, bring it up with your contractor and they will spec accordingly.

### Does epoxy flooring crack?

Epoxy does not crack on its own. It cracks when the concrete under it cracks, because it is rigid and it moves with the slab. That is the honest answer in New Braunfels, where clay soil shifts slabs with the seasons. Filling existing cracks before coating and, on active slabs, choosing a slightly more flexible system both reduce the risk. Normal traffic, foot or vehicle, will not crack it.

### Can epoxy be applied over old concrete?

Yes, and old concrete in good structural condition coats just as well as new. It needs the same prep: strip old paint and sealer, repair cracks and spalling, grind or shot-blast to a profile, and pass a moisture test. If an older slab is structurally sound, its age is not a problem.

### What is the difference between epoxy and polyaspartic coatings?

Both are resin coatings for concrete. Epoxy costs less and cures slowly, 48 to 72 hours before foot traffic. Polyaspartic costs about 20 to 30 percent more, cures in roughly a day, and does not yellow in sunlight the way standard epoxy can. A lot of contractors use an epoxy base with a polyaspartic top to get the best of each. The [epoxy vs. polyaspartic guide](/guides/epoxy-vs-polyaspartic-garage-floors/) goes deeper.

### How do you maintain epoxy floors?

Barely. Sweep or dust-mop to keep grit from scratching the surface. Mop with warm water and a mild, pH-neutral cleaner when it needs it. Skip bleach and ammonia-based products; they dull the topcoat over time. Wipe up oil and gas spills when you notice them. The coating resists them, but nothing benefits from sitting in gasoline for a month. That is the whole routine.
