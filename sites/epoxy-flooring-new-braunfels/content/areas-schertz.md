## Why Schertz Homeowners Choose Epoxy Flooring

### Climate and Concrete Conditions

Schertz sits within the I-35 Expansive Clay Corridor. The predominant soils — Eddy very gravelly clay loam and Houston Black clay — can swell 10 to 30 percent in volume when wet and crack up to 4 inches wide when dry. Houston Black clay contains 46 to 60 percent smectite clay, placing it among the most reactive soil types in North America.

Slab cracking from clay shrink-swell is common in Schertz even in relatively new construction. Homeowners in Belmont Park, Rhine Valley, and other subdivisions report slab cracking, wall cracks, and door misalignment as foundations respond to seasonal moisture changes. For epoxy flooring, this means proper crack repair and flexible coating systems are not optional extras — rigid coatings over unaddressed slab cracks will re-crack and delaminate.

Average annual humidity in Schertz runs around 64%, peaking at 75% in May. That puts it in the zone where concrete moisture vapor transmission testing is critical before any epoxy application. Slabs above 3 to 5 pounds of moisture emission per 1,000 square feet per 24 hours require a moisture-mitigating primer. Older Schertz homes built before 2010 may lack a proper under-slab vapor barrier, which causes blistering, clouding, and delamination within 6 to 12 months if not addressed during installation.

## Local Considerations

**Permits:** Applying an epoxy coating over an existing concrete floor does not require a building permit in Schertz. The city does require a permit ($100, two inspections) for new concrete flatwork such as driveways and sidewalks, but coating an existing slab is not classified as new flatwork. All contractors doing business in Schertz must register through the city's online portal — registration is free. Planning and Community Development: 210-619-1750. [City of Schertz — Building Permits](https://www.schertz.com/456/Building-Permits)

**Building codes:** Schertz has adopted the 2018 International Building Code, International Residential Code, and related 2018 I-codes. Texas does not require a state-level license for epoxy flooring installers, and no local epoxy-specific certification exists beyond the general contractor registration.

**HOAs:** The Crossvine, Homestead, Forest Ridge, and other master-planned communities have homeowner associations with architectural guidelines. Garage floor coating is typically interior work that falls outside HOA review, but check your community's covenants to confirm — some associations have rules about contractor access hours or exterior staging.

**Three-county note:** Because Schertz spans three counties, property tax rates and some jurisdictional details vary by address. This does not affect permit requirements for epoxy work.
