## Professional Epoxy Flooring Services

Epoxy is a catalyzed resin system — Part A and Part B mixed on site, applied over mechanically prepared concrete, and cured into a monolithic shell bonded to the slab. It is not paint. It does not peel off like a sticker. The chemical bond locks into the pore structure of the concrete and becomes the wearing surface.

Contractors in our network handle projects from first inspection through final cure. They bring their own diamond grinders, moisture testing equipment, and materials. Every installer working through Miami Garage Floor Co. operates independently — you deal with one person who owns the outcome.

### Why Choose Epoxy Flooring?

Oil drips, tire marks, salt residue tracked in from coastal roads, the puddle that collects under a pressure washer after weekend driveway work — none of it stains an epoxy surface. Cleanup is a broom and an occasional mop with diluted soap.

The structural argument matters more in Miami than almost anywhere else in the country. Moisture vapor transmission through South Florida slabs averages 4 to 8 pounds per 1,000 square feet per 24 hours. That exceeds the 3-pound threshold where unprotected epoxy begins to fail. A proper coating system with a moisture-mitigating primer creates a vapor barrier that slows that transfer, protecting stored belongings and the slab itself from the relentless upward push of groundwater.

Material cost is the smallest part of any quote. Epoxy resin runs $0.30 to $1 per square foot. What drives the price is preparation — diamond grinding to open the concrete pore structure, ASTM F2170 moisture probe testing, crack repair, and removal of any previous coating. That prep work is what separates a floor that lasts 15 years from one that blisters inside 12 months.

### Where Epoxy Flooring Works Best

Any structurally sound concrete slab is a candidate. In the Miami market the most common installations fall into these categories:

- **Residential garages.** The most requested service. Covers tire marks, blocks moisture vapor, and turns the garage from raw concrete into usable living space.
- **Condo parking structures.** Miami Beach and Brickell towers coat their parking decks with commercial-grade systems to handle vehicle traffic, salt, and water intrusion.
- **Workshops and home gyms.** Weights do not crack it, sawdust sweeps off, and chemical spills wipe clean.
- **Warehouses and distribution centers.** Forklift traffic, pallet jacks, and constant foot traffic demand a system engineered for load.
- **Retail and restaurant floors.** A seamless surface with coved bases where health code requires it, in a finish that looks polished without tile or stone.

If a slab shows active moisture — white efflorescence deposits, damp patches, or standing water — a qualified installer will identify that on the first visit and recommend mitigation before quoting the coating. That honesty saves you money and prevents a callback.

## How It Works

Three steps from first call to finished floor.

### Step 1: Request a Quote

Call or submit a request through the site. Provide the location, type of space, approximate square footage, and what the floor looks like now. A local contractor follows up to schedule a free on-site inspection. No obligation.

### Step 2: Surface Prep and Color Selection

After you agree on a price, the crew starts with the concrete. Diamond grinding or shot-blasting opens the slab surface so the epoxy achieves a mechanical bond. The installer runs an ASTM F2170 moisture probe test — in Miami-Dade that test is mandatory for every industrial installation and strongly recommended for residential work, because the shallow water table pushes vapor through concrete year-round regardless of the season.

Cracks get filled. Spalling gets patched. Previous coatings get stripped. Then you select a finish: solid color, flake, quartz, or metallic. Most contractors carry physical sample boards and will advise which systems perform best under your specific use conditions.

### Step 3: Installation and Curing

Moisture-mitigating primer, base coat, decorative layer, clear topcoat. A standard two-car garage takes one to two days of active application.

Then the floor cures. Plan on 24 to 72 hours before foot traffic and up to seven days before parking a vehicle. Miami's heat accelerates chemical reaction but the humidity slows solvent release, so experienced installers here schedule applications for early morning and use dehumidification equipment when ambient readings exceed 70 percent. Timing depends on the product system and that week's weather.
