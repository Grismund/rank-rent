## About Metallic Epoxy Flooring

Metallic epoxy starts with the same two-part catalyzed resin base as any epoxy system. The difference is in the pigment. Standard epoxy uses opaque colorants that produce a uniform, predictable surface. Metallic systems use mica-based or aluminum pigment particles that remain suspended in the wet resin and shift position as the material levels. The installer manipulates these particles with rollers, brushes, or heat to create depth, movement, and three-dimensional visual effects.

The result is a reflective, high-gloss floor with a depth that flat-color coatings cannot achieve. It is a finish that belongs in spaces where appearance matters — not just durability.

## Advantages of Metallic Epoxy Flooring

**Visual impact.** The swirling, three-dimensional pattern creates a floor that draws attention. Each installation is unique — the pigment distribution, color density, and flow pattern are unrepeatable. For finished garages, entryways, and commercial showrooms, the floor becomes a design element rather than just a surface.

**All the structural benefits of standard epoxy.** Moisture blocking, chemical resistance, dust elimination, and easy cleaning all carry over from standard systems. The metallic pigment does not reduce the epoxy's performance — it only changes its appearance.

**UV stability when paired with polyaspartic clear.** Miami sunlight reaches garage floors through open doors for most of the day. A polyaspartic topcoat on metallic epoxy resists the yellowing that standard polyurethane develops under sustained UV exposure. The metallic color beneath stays true for years.

**High perceived value.** Metallic floors look expensive because they are. In a market where Brickell one-bedrooms sell for $500,000 and Coral Gables homes clear $1.5 million, a metallic garage floor fits the property's overall presentation.

## The Metallic Epoxy Flooring Process

### Concrete Preparation

The process begins identically to standard epoxy — diamond grinding to profile the slab, ASTM F2170 moisture probe testing, crack repair, and removal of any previous coating. Metallic systems are unforgiving of surface defects because the high-gloss, reflective finish magnifies every imperfection beneath it. The slab must be flat, clean, and free of patching marks that would telegraph through the metallic layer.

In Miami, the moisture mitigation step is especially critical for metallic. A blister under metallic epoxy is more visible and more destructive to the visual pattern than under a flake or solid coat. The vapor-mitigating primer is non-negotiable here.

### Epoxy Application

The installer applies a tinted base coat in a color that complements the metallic layer. After the base tacks, the metallic coat goes down. This is where the craft happens. The installer works the wet resin with a spiked roller or squeegee to direct pigment flow, then uses a heat gun or torch to accelerate resin movement in targeted areas. These manipulations create the veins, swirls, and depth patterns that define the final look.

Timing is compressed. Metallic epoxy must be worked before the resin begins to gel, and in Miami's heat that window can close in 15 to 20 minutes. Experienced metallic installers manage slab temperature, ambient humidity, and material temperature simultaneously. It is the most technique-dependent epoxy application.

### Curing and Final Coat

Two coats of clear topcoat protect the metallic layer. Polyaspartic clear is the standard recommendation for metallic floors in South Florida because of its UV resistance and fast cure. The first clear coat seals the metallic surface. The second builds thickness and scratch resistance.

Foot traffic in 12 to 24 hours. Vehicle parking in 48 to 72 hours for polyaspartic systems. Standard clear extends those timelines to 72 hours and seven days respectively.
