The quick answer on **epoxy flooring cost Miami** homeowners can expect: a professionally installed two-car garage runs between $1,600 and $3,500. Commercial floors are priced per square foot and scale from there. The rest of this page explains what determines where your project lands within those ranges.

These numbers reflect current pricing from Miami-Dade contractors. Your final quote depends on the slab's condition, finish selection, and whether moisture mitigation is required — which, in Miami, it almost always is.

*Last updated: November 2026. Costs vary by contractor and site conditions.*

## Epoxy Flooring Price Ranges

### One-Car Garage

Around 250 square feet. Budget **$1,250 to $2,500**. A basic solid-color or flake finish with standard preparation sits at the low end. A metallic finish, extensive crack repair, or a moisture-mitigating primer pushes toward the top.

Setup costs do not shrink proportionally with floor size. Transporting diamond grinders, running moisture probes, filling cracks, and masking surfaces takes roughly the same effort on 250 square feet as on 500. The per-square-foot price on a one-car garage runs higher even though the total is lower.

### Two-Car Garage

The standard Miami residential project. Four hundred to five hundred square feet, typically running **$1,600 to $3,500**. That range spans a standard flake floor at the bottom through a layered metallic system with a polyaspartic topcoat at the top.

Most Miami homeowners choose flake broadcast with a clear topcoat. It hides minor slab imperfections, provides grip when wet, and creates a finished appearance without the cost of metallic. Upgrading the clear coat from standard polyurethane to polyaspartic adds a few hundred dollars and reduces the return-to-service wait from about a week to a single day.

### Commercial or Large Area

Warehouses, restaurant kitchens, retail showrooms, condo parking decks — anything above roughly 1,000 square feet is priced by the foot.

- **Solid or flake system:** $3 to $7 per square foot
- **Metallic or decorative finish:** $5 to $12 per square foot
- **Heavy-use industrial:** quoted per project based on chemical exposure, traffic loads, and compliance requirements

Larger floor plates bring the per-foot price down because crew mobilization, grinder transport, and equipment setup are fixed costs regardless of area. The [commercial epoxy flooring page](/commercial-epoxy-flooring/) covers what distinguishes business-grade installations.

## Why Epoxy Flooring Prices Vary

Five factors move the price, roughly in order of impact:

**Slab condition and moisture level.** This is the dominant cost variable in Miami. The city sits on porous oolitic limestone over the Biscayne Aquifer, and slab moisture vapor transmission here averages 4 to 8 pounds per 1,000 square feet per 24 hours. When ASTM F2170 moisture probe readings exceed 75 percent relative humidity — a common result in Miami-Dade — a vapor-mitigating primer is added to the system. That primer is a separate line item that can add $1 to $2 per square foot. Crack repair, spalling patches, and removal of old coatings increase prep time further.

**Square footage.** More floor means more material and labor, but less cost per foot due to fixed setup expenses.

**Finish type.** Solid-color epoxy is the budget option at $3 to $7 per square foot. Metallic finishes run $5 to $12 because the pigment is more expensive, the application technique is more complex, and two coats of clear topcoat are standard.

**Number of coating layers.** The basic stack is primer, base coat, and topcoat. Premium systems add additional base layers, decorative broadcasts between coats, or a second clear for added thickness and scratch resistance.

**Concrete repairs.** Routing and filling cracks, patching spalled areas, and stripping previous coatings are priced as line items above the base coating cost. Older Miami homes — particularly those built from the 1950s through 1970s — often carry decades of slab wear that adds to the preparation scope.

## Epoxy vs Polyaspartic: Cost Comparison

Miami homeowners typically choose between two coating chemistries. Both produce a durable, sealed floor, but they differ in price, cure speed, and long-term performance.

**Epoxy** is the less expensive option and accounts for the majority of residential installations across Miami-Dade. The price ranges above reflect epoxy systems. The trade-off is cure time: 48 to 72 hours before foot traffic, five to seven days before parking.

**Polyaspartic** runs 20 to 30 percent higher — roughly $400 to $800 more on a standard two-car garage. The payoff is a floor you can drive on within about 24 hours and a clear coat that resists UV yellowing. In a city where sunlight reaches the garage floor through open doors year-round, UV stability has practical value.

Many Miami contractors recommend a hybrid approach: an epoxy base coat for adhesion strength and cost control, topped with a polyaspartic clear for fast cure and UV resistance.
