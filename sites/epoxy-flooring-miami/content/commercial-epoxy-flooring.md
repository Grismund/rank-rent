## About Commercial Epoxy Flooring

Commercial epoxy is a multi-layer resin system engineered for heavier loads, faster turnaround, and stricter compliance than residential garage coatings. The chemistry is the same two-part catalyzed reaction, but commercial builds are thicker — typically 10 to 20 mils compared to 6 to 10 mils for residential — and often include anti-slip aggregate, chemical-resistant topcoats, or coved base systems where health code demands seamless wall-to-floor transitions.

Miami-Dade County requires building permits for all commercial floor coating projects. Surface preparation on commercial sites may also trigger permit review when shot-blasting or structural modifications are involved. Every industrial floor installation in the county requires ASTM F2170 in-situ relative humidity testing before the first coat of any system goes down. These are not optional steps — they are code.

The contractors in our network handle permitting, testing, compliance documentation, and scheduling. Many commercial installations happen overnight or on weekends so the business never closes.

## Advantages of Commercial Epoxy Flooring

**Load capacity.** Pallet jacks, loaded forklifts, rolling carts, and constant foot traffic destroy paint and thin coatings. A commercial epoxy build absorbs compressive and impact loads without cracking, chipping, or delaminating.

**Chemical resistance.** Restaurants need floors that survive degreasers and hot grease. Auto shops need resistance to brake fluid and transmission oil. Warehouses handling cleaning products or industrial solvents need a barrier between chemicals and the concrete substrate. Commercial epoxy provides that barrier in a seamless, joint-free surface.

**Health code compliance.** Miami-Dade food service facilities require coved bases — radiused transitions from floor to wall that eliminate the 90-degree corners where bacteria and debris collect. Commercial epoxy systems include integral coving as part of the installation. ADA compliance and fire code adherence apply to every commercial project in the county.

**Moisture management.** The Biscayne Aquifer sits close beneath every slab in Miami-Dade, pushing 4 to 8 pounds of moisture vapor per 1,000 square feet through the concrete every 24 hours. Commercial buildings with large floor plates magnify this exposure. A vapor-mitigating primer beneath the epoxy build controls that moisture and prevents the osmotic blistering that destroys commercial floors from below.

**Minimal downtime.** Polyaspartic topcoats on commercial systems cure in hours. A phased installation — coating one zone per night — keeps the facility operational throughout the project.

## The Commercial Epoxy Flooring Process

### Concrete Preparation

Shot-blasting is the standard preparation method on commercial floors. It removes existing coatings, opens the pore structure, and creates the ICRI CSP-3 to CSP-5 profile required for thick commercial builds. Diamond grinding handles detail work around columns, walls, and equipment bases.

The crew runs ASTM F2170 moisture probes across the slab at the spacing specified for the floor's square footage. In Miami, readings above 75 percent relative humidity are routine, and the mitigation primer is factored into nearly every commercial quote.

Expansion joints, control joints, and slab cracks are addressed individually. Flexible fillers allow for movement without telegraphing through the finished surface. Structural defects get repaired before coating begins.

### Epoxy Application

The primer coat bridges the prepared concrete to the base system. For heavy-traffic commercial spaces, the base coat is applied in multiple passes to build thickness. Anti-slip aluminum oxide or quartz aggregate is broadcast into the wet coat in areas exposed to water, grease, or food service foot traffic.

Color coding by zone — different colors for pedestrian aisles, forklift lanes, and staging areas — is a standard commercial option. It improves safety, organizes workflow, and costs only the additional material.

### Curing and Final Coat

A commercial-grade clear topcoat seals the system. Polyaspartic clear is the default for most Miami commercial installations because it cures fast enough to return the floor to service within 12 to 18 hours and resists the UV exposure that yellows standard polyurethane in sun-drenched loading bays.

Full cure to rated load capacity typically takes 72 hours. The installer coordinates the phased return of equipment, vehicles, and operations to match the cure schedule.
