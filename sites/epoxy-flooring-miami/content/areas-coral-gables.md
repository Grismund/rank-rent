## What Drives Epoxy Demand in Coral Gables

### Climate and Concrete Conditions

Coral Gables sits on the same porous oolitic limestone that underlies all of Miami-Dade. The Biscayne Aquifer beneath the city has a hydraulic conductivity of 5,000 feet per day — meaning water moves through the limestone fast and pushes upward into every slab built on grade. Research from the American Concrete Institute indicates 67 percent of epoxy failures in Florida trace directly to inadequate moisture management.

Water vapor rises through concrete via capillary action, producing mold, mildew, and flooring failure when left unchecked. The standard epoxy application maximum is 3 pounds of moisture vapor per 1,000 square feet per 24 hours; South Florida slabs frequently exceed that threshold. Efflorescence — the white mineral deposits visible on many Coral Gables garage floors — is an early indicator of moisture migration and a warning sign that any coating applied without mitigation will fail.

Humidity averages 73 percent year-round and peaks at 78 percent in September. At those levels, high humidity slows epoxy curing and raises the risk of amine blush, a waxy film that destroys adhesion between coats and requires complete sanding before recoating.

## Local Considerations

**Historic preservation:** Coral Gables enforces a historic preservation overlay on portions of the city. Exterior modifications to designated buildings require Board of Architects approval. Interior floor coatings are not affected by this overlay, but contractors should be aware of it when scheduling and accessing properties in the historic core.

**Permits:** Residential interior coating is permit-exempt in Miami-Dade for single-family homes. Commercial projects require building permits. Surface preparation on commercial sites may trigger additional review.

**VOC limits:** Florida limits surface coating VOC to 5.0 percent by volume or 6.0 gallons per day averaged monthly. Miami-Dade additionally requires ASTM D 6886 lab reports for low-VOC component testing. Contractors must document compliance for commercial and condo installations.
