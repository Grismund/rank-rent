## About Epoxy Floor Coating

Epoxy floor coating is a broad category that covers every application where a two-part epoxy resin is applied to concrete to protect and finish the surface. The chemistry is the same whether the floor is in a one-car garage or a 10,000-square-foot distribution center. What changes is the build thickness, the aggregate or decorative media used, and the topcoat selected to match the traffic the floor will see.

In San Antonio, the soil beneath the slab is part of the conversation on every job. Houston Black clay dominates Bexar County, and its seasonal expansion and contraction stresses slabs in ways that many coating systems are not designed to absorb. A coating contractor who works in this market tests for moisture, repairs cracks with flexible fill and chooses a system that tolerates minor slab movement without delaminating.

## Advantages of Epoxy Floor Coating

Bare concrete is porous. It absorbs oil, dusts under foot traffic and cracks under thermal stress. Epoxy seals those pores and creates a continuous, non-porous surface that is easy to clean and resistant to the chemicals found in a typical garage or workshop. For San Antonio homeowners dealing with seasonal humidity swings, a sealed floor also blocks the moisture vapor that migrates upward through the slab after spring rains saturate the clay below.

The finish options range from utilitarian to decorative. A clear coat over bare concrete provides basic protection at the lowest cost. A solid-color base coat covers imperfections and gives the space a clean, uniform look. Vinyl flake broadcast adds texture and hides minor dirt between cleanings. And [metallic epoxy](/metallic-epoxy-flooring/) turns a floor into a design statement. Each option carries a different price, which the [cost guide](/cost/) lays out by square foot.

## The Epoxy Floor Coating Process

### Concrete Preparation

Surface preparation determines whether the coating bonds or fails. The contractor grinds or shot-blasts the concrete to remove laitance, stains, old sealers and the smooth troweled skin that prevents adhesion. On San Antonio slabs, this step also reveals cracks that need flexible fill and areas where moisture readings warrant a mitigation primer.

Moisture testing is not optional in this climate. ASTM F1869 calcium chloride tests measure surface vapor emission, and ASTM F2170 probes read relative humidity inside the slab at 40 percent depth. Slabs that read above 75 percent internal RH or above 3 to 5 pounds per 1,000 square feet per 24 hours need a moisture-blocking primer before the base coat.

### Epoxy Application

The base coat goes down after the slab is clean, profiled and dry. Two-part epoxy is mixed in small batches to control working time, especially important during San Antonio summers when heat accelerates the chemical reaction. The crew spreads the resin with rollers or squeegees at the specified thickness, then broadcasts decorative media if the system calls for it.

### Curing and Final Coat

A topcoat seals the decorative layer and provides the final wear surface. Options include standard epoxy clear, polyaspartic clear, or urethane topcoats, each with different hardness, gloss and cure-time characteristics. Standard epoxy needs one to three days before foot traffic. Polyaspartic cures in hours, which makes it the go-to for projects where downtime is expensive.
