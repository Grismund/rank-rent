## Why Converse Homeowners Choose Epoxy Flooring

### Climate and Concrete Conditions

The ground beneath Converse is Houston Black clay, one of the most reactive soil types in North America. Smectite content runs 46 to 60 percent, and the clay can shift 4 to 6 inches between wet and dry seasons. That movement heaves slabs in spring when the clay absorbs rain and opens shrinkage cracks in summer when the soil dries out. Post-tension slab-on-grade foundations are standard in Converse new construction specifically because of this soil.

For floor coatings, the clay's behavior means two things. First, every slab needs crack inspection and flexible fill before coating. A rigid coating over an active crack re-cracks and peels. Second, moisture vapor transmission testing is non-negotiable. After heavy rains, the swelling clay forces water upward through the slab at rates that can exceed the 3 to 4 pounds per 1,000 square feet per 24 hours that standard epoxy tolerates. If testing shows elevated readings, a moisture mitigation primer must go down before the base coat.

Annual humidity in Converse averages about 67 percent, with morning readings reaching 84 percent before falling to 52 percent by afternoon. That swing affects curing differently depending on when the crew starts. Fall and winter installations benefit from consistently lower humidity. Summer work is possible but may call for polyaspartic products or dehumidification equipment in the garage during application.

## What to Know Before Coating in Converse

**Permits.** Epoxy coating over an existing concrete floor does not require a building permit in Converse. The city falls within the San Antonio metro's permitting framework, and cosmetic surface treatments are on the No Permit Required list. Texas does not require a state-level license for epoxy floor installers.

**VOC regulations.** Texas Commission on Environmental Quality Chapter 115 limits floor coating VOC content to 250 grams per liter, stricter than the federal EPA standard. All professional-grade epoxy and polyaspartic products used by our contractors meet this requirement.

**Jurisdiction check.** Some Converse addresses fall in unincorporated Bexar County rather than within city limits. Permit jurisdiction can vary by address, though it rarely matters for residential epoxy work since no permit is required from either jurisdiction for a standard coating job.

**Military timing.** If you are coating a garage before a PCS listing, schedule the job at least two weeks before photography and showings. Standard epoxy needs about a week to reach full vehicle-traffic cure. Polyaspartic can cut that to 24 hours if the timeline is tight.
