## About Commercial Epoxy Flooring

Commercial epoxy uses the same resin chemistry as residential systems but at higher build thicknesses and with additives tailored to the traffic each facility sees. A warehouse floor might call for a quartz-filled broadcast at 30 to 60 mils for impact resistance. A restaurant kitchen needs antimicrobial additives and a textured anti-slip profile that still cleans easily under health inspections.

San Antonio's commercial slabs sit on the same expansive clay that affects residential foundations. Large-footprint buildings settle unevenly as the clay cycles between wet and dry seasons, so commercial prep work includes crack mapping, joint repair and sometimes moisture mitigation over thousands of square feet. The scale is bigger, but the soil does not care about the building's zoning.

## Advantages of Commercial Epoxy Flooring

A coated commercial floor reduces maintenance costs immediately. Sealed concrete does not dust, so HVAC filters last longer and product stays cleaner on warehouse shelves. It resists oil, solvents and food acids that would etch bare concrete and create slip hazards. OSHA compliance gets simpler when you can zone traffic with color-coded lanes and designated walkways built into the floor itself.

For businesses along the I-10 logistics corridor, where trailers back up to loading docks around the clock, epoxy withstands the rolling loads and abrasion that eat through paint-grade coatings in months. And because the surface is seamless, contaminants cannot hide in cracks the way they do on untreated slabs, a point that matters during TDSHS inspections at food-handling facilities.

## The Commercial Epoxy Flooring Process

### Concrete Preparation

Commercial prep starts with a site survey. The contractor evaluates the slab age, existing coatings, joint layout, crack patterns and drainage slope. Shot-blasting is the standard profiling method on commercial floors because it handles large areas efficiently and produces a consistent surface profile across the entire slab.

Moisture testing follows the same ASTM standards used on residential work, but commercial facilities often have larger areas of concern. Slabs poured without adequate vapor barriers, common in older San Antonio industrial buildings, may need full-coverage moisture mitigation before coating.

### Epoxy Application

Base coats go down in sections, often overnight or over weekends so the business can keep operating. The crew works in zones, applying primer, filling joints with flexible polyurea, then rolling the epoxy base and broadcasting aggregate or flake depending on the system. Anti-slip additives, aluminum oxide or polymer grit, get incorporated into the topcoat for high-traffic zones and wet areas.

### Curing and Final Coat

Commercial topcoats are selected for the specific traffic the floor will see. Polyaspartic clear coats cure in hours and are ready for foot traffic the same day, which limits the downtime a business absorbs. Standard epoxy topcoats are less expensive per square foot but require 24 to 72 hours before traffic returns. Your contractor will recommend the system that balances budget and operational needs.

## How Much Does Commercial Epoxy Flooring Cost?

Commercial projects vary widely based on square footage, slab condition and system complexity. A straightforward 2,000-square-foot shop floor with a flake finish might fall in the $3 to $7 per square foot range. Large warehouses with quartz broadcast or multi-coat systems can run higher.
