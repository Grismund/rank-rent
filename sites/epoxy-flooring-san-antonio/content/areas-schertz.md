## Why Schertz Homeowners Choose Epoxy Flooring

### Climate and Concrete Conditions

Schertz shares San Antonio's expansive clay challenge. The soil beneath the Bexar County side is predominantly Houston Black clay, a deep Vertisol that swells when it absorbs moisture and contracts during the long dry stretches of a San Antonio summer. That movement puts constant stress on slab-on-grade foundations, opening cracks that propagate through any rigid surface coating applied without proper preparation.

Annual humidity in Schertz averages 64 percent and peaks near 75 percent in May. For epoxy installation, that means moisture vapor testing is not optional. Slabs on the Bexar County side sit closer to the low-lying drainage paths that feed Cibolo Creek, and after sustained rain events the water table can push vapor emission rates above the 3 to 5 pound threshold where standard epoxy starts to fail. Professional installers run ASTM F1869 and F2170 tests before committing to a product system.

New concrete in Schertz must cure at least 30 days before coating. Homes in active subdivisions sometimes hit the market with slabs that have not had time to fully hydrate, which creates a hidden risk for homeowners who want floors coated as part of their move-in upgrades.

## What to Know Before Coating in Schertz

**Permits.** No building permit is required for epoxy coating over an existing concrete floor in Schertz. The city does require new concrete flatwork to be permitted ($100, two inspections), but coating an existing slab does not fall under that requirement. Every contractor working in the city must register through the Schertz online portal; registration is free.

**Building codes.** Schertz has adopted the 2018 International Building Code and related I-codes. Texas does not require a state-level license for epoxy flooring installers, and Schertz has no epoxy-specific certification beyond the general contractor registration.

**HOA rules.** Subdivisions on the Bexar County side, including The Parklands, Saddlebrook Ranch and Greenfield Village, have homeowner associations with architectural guidelines. Garage floor coatings are interior work and typically fall outside design review, but some HOAs regulate contractor working hours, equipment staging on driveways and debris removal. Check your association's covenants before scheduling.

**Scheduling.** The best installation window is October through April, when humidity drops and slab temperatures stay manageable. Summer jobs are routine but may require a polyaspartic system or on-site dehumidification to keep conditions within spec.
