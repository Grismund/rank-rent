Understanding epoxy flooring cost San Antonio homeowners face starts with two numbers: the size of the space and the type of finish you want. Most residential garage projects in Bexar County fall between $1,600 and $3,500 installed, though that range shifts based on slab condition, coating system and whether the concrete needs moisture mitigation before anything goes down. This page breaks down the pricing so you can budget before calling for a quote.

## Average Epoxy Flooring Prices

San Antonio pricing tracks closely with the South Texas market. Material cost runs $0.30 to $1.00 per square foot at wholesale; the rest of the installed price covers labor, surface preparation, equipment and topcoat. Here is what each finish type costs per square foot installed:

- **Basic solid coat:** $3 to $7 per square foot
- **Vinyl flake blend:** $4 to $7 per square foot
- **Metallic epoxy finish:** $5 to $12 per square foot
- **Polyaspartic topcoat system:** $4 to $10 per square foot

The chart above puts these ranges side by side. Solid and flake finishes overlap significantly because flake adds decorative media but does not change the application method. Metallic runs higher because the pigment costs more and the installation requires hands-on pattern work. Polyaspartic systems cost more per gallon than standard epoxy, but their fast cure time often reduces total labor hours, which narrows the gap on larger floors.

### One-Car Garage

A single-car garage is roughly 250 square feet. At $3 to $7 per square foot for a solid or flake finish, expect $750 to $1,750 for the coating itself. Add crack repair, moisture testing and a primer coat on a slab that needs it, and the total installed price typically lands between $1,250 and $2,450. One-car garages are the most cost-efficient size because setup and mobilization costs spread over a smaller area still represent a floor the contractor can complete in a single day.

### Two-Car Garage

The standard San Antonio two-car garage runs about 450 square feet. Installed cost for a solid or flake system falls between $1,650 and $3,450. This is the sweet spot for most residential projects: big enough to justify a professional crew, small enough to complete in one to two days. If you upgrade to metallic, the same 450-square-foot floor can reach $5,400 at the top of the range.

### Commercial or Large Area

Commercial floors and three-car residential garages (around 700 square feet and up) price on a sliding scale. Per-square-foot rates tend to drop slightly on larger areas because setup costs spread further. A 700-square-foot three-car bay runs roughly $2,450 to $5,100 for a standard system. Commercial warehouse floors at 2,000 to 10,000 square feet negotiate pricing directly with the contractor, and the [commercial epoxy flooring](/commercial-epoxy-flooring/) page covers what to expect from those projects.

## Factors That Influence Epoxy Flooring Cost

Slab condition is the biggest variable. A clean, crack-free, newer slab in a [Schertz](/areas/schertz/) subdivision needs minimal prep and comes in near the bottom of the range. An older slab with multiple cracks, a previous coating that must be removed, or high moisture readings adds prep labor and material cost that can push the job $500 to $1,000 above baseline.

San Antonio's Houston Black clay soil makes moisture mitigation more common here than in many other markets. If the calcium chloride test returns readings above 3 to 5 pounds per 1,000 square feet per 24 hours, a moisture-blocking primer adds to the cost but prevents coating failure that would cost more to fix.

Other factors include floor area, the number of coats, decorative media selection, accessibility (a finished garage with shelving and workbenches takes longer than an empty slab) and time of year. Demand peaks in the fall when humidity drops, so scheduling a job in January or February sometimes yields better availability.

## Epoxy or Polyaspartic: Which Costs More?

Polyaspartic systems generally cost $1 to $3 more per square foot in material than standard epoxy. On a 450-square-foot garage, that translates to roughly $450 to $1,350 extra. The tradeoff is a floor that cures in hours instead of days, which matters in San Antonio's humid summer months when extended cure windows increase the risk of amine blush and moisture contamination.

The [epoxy vs. polyaspartic guide](/guides/epoxy-vs-polyaspartic-garage-floors/) compares the two systems in detail, including cure times, durability and long-term maintenance. If budget is the primary concern, standard epoxy with a flake finish delivers the best value per dollar. If you need to park on the floor the same day it is coated, polyaspartic is worth the premium.
