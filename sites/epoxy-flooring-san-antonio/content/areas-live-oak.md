## Why Live Oak Homeowners Choose Epoxy Flooring

### Climate and Concrete Conditions

Live Oak sits on the same Houston Black clay that runs beneath most of northeast Bexar County. This deep Vertisol has 46 to 60 percent smectite content, giving it one of the highest shrink-swell capacities of any soil type in the country. During wet periods the clay heaves upward, pushing the slab with it. During San Antonio's prolonged summer dry spells the soil contracts, pulling support away from the edges of the foundation and opening cracks.

The practical consequence for floor coatings is that slab cracks are common and expected. Any coating applied without first addressing cracks with flexible fill material will telegraph those cracks to the finished surface within a season. Moisture testing is equally critical. Some pre-2010 construction in the San Antonio metro was built without proper under-slab vapor barriers, and in Live Oak's older neighborhoods, Woodcrest and Coronado Village, that omission can produce moisture vapor emission rates that exceed the 3 to 5 pounds per 1,000 square feet per 24 hours that standard epoxy can handle.

Annual humidity in the San Antonio metro averages 67 percent, with morning readings reaching 84 percent. The best installation window runs from October through April, when lower humidity and stable temperatures produce the most reliable curing conditions.

## What to Know Before Coating in Live Oak

**Permits.** No building permit is required for epoxy coating over an existing concrete floor. Live Oak has its own municipal government, but residential surface coatings fall outside the permitting threshold for both the city and Bexar County. Texas does not require a state license for epoxy flooring work.

**Older homes.** Woodcrest was originally platted before the city incorporated in 1954. Some homes in the original subdivision may have poured foundations without modern vapor barriers. If you own one of these older properties, let the contractor know so they can prioritize moisture testing during the slab assessment.

**Jurisdiction.** Some properties near the city boundary sit in unincorporated Bexar County rather than within Live Oak city limits. For epoxy work this distinction has no practical effect, since neither jurisdiction requires a permit for coating an existing slab.

**VOC limits.** Texas TCEQ Chapter 115 caps floor coating VOC content at 250 grams per liter. All professional-grade products used by our contractors comply.
