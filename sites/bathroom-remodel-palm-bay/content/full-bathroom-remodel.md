## What Is Full Bathroom Remodel?

A full remodel strips the bathroom to its framing. Walls are opened, old tile and backer board are removed, and all existing fixtures come out. Plumbing supply lines and drains are inspected, updated, or rerouted as needed. Electrical wiring is brought up to current code. The room is then waterproofed, tiled, fitted with new fixtures, and finished.

Typical scope includes: demolition, plumbing rough-in, electrical rough-in, waterproofing membrane installation, backer board, tile on floors and walls, shower or tub installation, vanity, toilet, faucets, lighting, ventilation fan, paint, and trim. In Palm Bay, any project that relocates plumbing or modifies electrical circuits requires a building permit from the City of Palm Bay Building Department, which enforces the Florida Building Code 8th Edition.

## The Full Bathroom Remodel Process

### Planning and Design

The contractor visits the bathroom, checks the condition of the existing plumbing and electrical, and looks for signs of hidden moisture damage. You discuss layout options, tile material and pattern, fixture brands, vanity style, and whether to keep the tub or convert to a walk-in shower. The contractor then produces a detailed scope of work, timeline, and price.

If a permit is needed — and most full remodels in Palm Bay require one — the contractor files the application with the City's building department and obtains approval before any demolition begins.

### Demolition and Rough-In

Demo typically takes one to two days. The crew removes all fixtures, tile, drywall, and flooring down to the studs. They check for water damage, mold, and subfloor deterioration. In Brevard County's humid climate, finding moisture behind a 30-year-old tub surround is routine.

Plumbing rough-in follows: new PEX or copper supply lines, drain relocations, and valve placements. Many Palm Bay homes from the 1980s and 1990s still have polybutylene supply pipes or galvanized steel drains, both of which commonly need replacement during a full remodel. Electrical rough-in updates wiring, adds GFCI outlets (required by code in all bathrooms), and installs the ventilation fan. A rough inspection is required before the walls are closed.

### Finishing and Inspection

The waterproofing membrane is applied to walls and the shower pan area — this is the most critical step in a Florida bathroom. Cement board goes up, then tile. Fixtures, vanity, toilet, mirrors, and trim follow. The final inspection verifies plumbing connections, electrical work, ventilation termination, and waterproofing compliance.

A full remodel in Palm Bay typically takes three to six weeks from demolition to completion, depending on project scope, material availability, and inspection scheduling.

## Choices for Your Full Bathroom Remodel

Material selections drive the look, durability, and cost:

- **Tile:** Porcelain is the practical choice for Brevard County bathrooms — lower porosity than ceramic means less moisture absorption in a 75-percent-humidity environment. Large-format tiles reduce grout lines and simplify maintenance in hard water. Natural stone requires sealing and ongoing upkeep.
- **Vanity:** Stock vanities start around $200. Semi-custom options run $500 to $1,500. Double vanities are the standard request in master bath remodels.
- **Shower:** Tiled walk-in showers with glass enclosures are the most popular upgrade. Frameless glass adds cost but opens the room visually. Curbless designs improve accessibility.
- **Tub:** Standard alcove tubs start around $200. Freestanding soaking tubs range from $500 to $3,000+. Walk-in tubs for accessibility run $2,000 to $5,000 for the fixture alone.
- **Fixtures:** Ceramic-disc valves and spot-resistant finishes are recommended for Brevard County's hard water. Budget fixture sets start around $100. Mid-range name-brand sets run $300 to $800.

## Full Bathroom Remodel Cost in Palm Bay

A full bathroom remodel in the Palm Bay area typically ranges from **$10,000 to $15,000** for a standard master bathroom. The final figure depends on bathroom size, the extent of plumbing and electrical work, tile and fixture selections, and whether the layout changes.
