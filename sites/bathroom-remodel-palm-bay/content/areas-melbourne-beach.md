## Local Bathroom Remodel in Melbourne Beach

Melbourne Beach is a small barrier island town of 3,177 residents, situated between the Atlantic Ocean to the east and the Indian River Lagoon to the west. The town's 1,809 housing units — 75.4 percent of which are detached single-family homes — sit on a narrow strip of land where every building faces salt-air exposure that mainland properties do not.

That coastal location creates a unique set of conditions for bathroom remodeling. Melbourne Beach bathrooms contend with the same Brevard County hard water (182 mg/L) and high humidity (75 percent annual average) as the rest of the region, but they also face constant salt-air corrosion. Metal plumbing components, fixture finishes, and even shower hardware degrade faster on the barrier island than on the mainland. A chrome faucet that lasts 15 years in Palm Bay may show corrosion in 8 to 10 years in Melbourne Beach.

## Why Melbourne Beach Homeowners Are Remodeling Bathrooms

### Home Ages and Common Bathroom Issues

Melbourne Beach has one of the oldest housing stocks in the Palm Bay service area. The median construction year is 1974, and 33.8 percent of homes were built between 1940 and 1969. Only 7.7 percent were built after 2000. That age profile means the majority of Melbourne Beach bathrooms are running on plumbing and fixtures that are 45 to 80+ years old.

Homes from the 1960s and 1970s commonly have original cast iron drain pipes and galvanized steel supply lines. In Brevard County's hard water, these materials corrode from the inside, restricting flow and eventually failing. Whole-home repiping is a frequent component of full bathroom remodels in Melbourne Beach. The combination of salt air from outside and mineral-laden water from inside creates a dual corrosion environment that shortens the service life of every metal component in the bathroom.

Neighborhoods like Aquarina Beach and Country Club, Floridana Beach, Crystal Lakes, and Beach Woods each have their own mix of construction eras and conditions, but the barrier island location is the common thread.

### Local Housing Market Trends

Melbourne Beach properties carry a premium for their ocean proximity, and the limited buildable land on the barrier island means the existing housing stock is what the market has to work with. Renovating rather than rebuilding is the practical path for most homeowners. A bathroom remodel that uses corrosion-resistant materials and addresses aging plumbing protects both the home's function and its value.

## Melbourne Beach Bathroom Remodel Permits

The Town of Melbourne Beach Building Department at 507 Ocean Avenue issues permits for all construction within town limits. Contact the department at 321-724-5860 or building@melbournebeachfl.org. Online permitting is available through the Town's BS&A portal.

Permits are required for any bathroom remodel involving plumbing relocation, electrical changes, structural modifications, or shower and tub retiling. Cosmetic changes — painting, hardware replacement — are exempt. Work valued under $7,500 on an owner-occupied single-family home may qualify for exemption under Florida HB 803.

Melbourne Beach enforces the Florida Building Code, 8th Edition (2023), and also applies additional coastal construction regulations under Chapter 4A of its municipal code. Barrier island properties in flood zones may face additional elevation and construction requirements that affect ground-floor bathroom renovations. The contractor confirms these requirements during the planning phase.

Bathroom exhaust must terminate outside the building envelope. When shower or tub-surround tile is removed, a code-listed waterproofing membrane is required behind the new tile, verified by inspection. All plumbing and electrical work requiring permits must be performed by Florida-licensed contractors. Permit fees run $200 to $700.

## What Melbourne Beach Homeowners Are Choosing

Barrier island conditions push Melbourne Beach homeowners toward materials and fixtures designed to resist both hard water and salt-air corrosion:

- **Stainless-steel or brushed-nickel fixtures** that hold up better than chrome in a salt-air environment. Ceramic-disc valves are preferred over compression types for the same reason.
- **Porcelain tile** over natural stone. Porcelain has lower porosity, resists moisture absorption, and does not require the ongoing sealing that stone demands in a coastal humidity environment.
- **Frameless glass shower doors with protective coatings** designed to resist both mineral deposits from hard water and salt residue from the coastal atmosphere.
- **PVC and composite trim** instead of wood in wet areas. Wood trim in a barrier island bathroom absorbs moisture and deteriorates faster than on the mainland.
- **Mold-resistant drywall** (cement board or green board) behind all shower and tub walls. The combination of 75 percent humidity and ocean air makes standard drywall inadequate in Melbourne Beach bathrooms.
