What does a **tub to shower conversion cost Palm Bay** homeowners typically pay? The range runs $3,000 to $8,000 for most projects, but that span reflects a wide variety of materials, designs, and plumbing conditions. This guide explains where the money goes, compares conversion costs to related bathroom projects, and identifies the factors that push a Palm Bay conversion toward the lower or upper end of the range.

*Last updated: November 2026. Based on Brevard County contractor pricing. Costs vary by contractor and site conditions.*

## Quick Summary

A tub-to-shower conversion removes an existing bathtub and installs a walk-in shower in the same footprint. Every conversion requires a plumbing modification because the tub drain and shower drain sit in different positions. That plumbing work, combined with waterproofing, tile, and a glass enclosure, defines the cost.

Three tiers cover the typical range in Palm Bay:

- **Standard conversion ($3,000 to $4,500):** Stock porcelain tile, a prefabricated shower pan, a standard curbed threshold, a framed glass door, and builder-grade fixtures. The plumbing modification is included. This tier delivers a functional, clean result.
- **Upgraded conversion ($4,500 to $6,500):** Upgraded porcelain tile in a larger format, a mortar-bed shower pan with a linear drain, a semi-frameless glass enclosure, name-brand showerhead and valve, a built-in niche, and a bench. This is the tier most Palm Bay homeowners select.
- **Premium conversion ($6,500 to $8,000+):** Large-format or natural stone tile, a curbless zero-threshold entry, a frameless glass panel or door, a rain showerhead with handheld, multiple niches, and a full-width tile bench. The curbless design demands precise slope work and increases labor costs, but it creates barrier-free access.

The plumbing modification is a fixed cost across all tiers, typically $500 to $1,500. The remaining variation comes from tile selection, glass type, and fixture grade.

## Key Differences

How does a tub-to-shower conversion compare to other bathroom projects on cost?

**Conversion vs. bathtub replacement:** Replacing a tub with a new tub of the same type runs $1,500 to $5,000 and does not require the plumbing rerouting that a conversion does. A conversion costs more ($3,000 to $8,000) because the drain moves and the walls receive full tile treatment. The trade-off is gaining a shower designed for daily use where a rarely used tub once sat.

**Conversion vs. shower remodel:** A shower remodel ($2,000 to $7,000) updates an existing shower without changing the fixture type. A conversion is a larger project because the fixture type changes entirely — a tub comes out and a shower goes in. The plumbing rerouting is the primary cost difference.

**Conversion vs. complete bathroom remodel:** A full remodel ($10,000 to $15,000+) includes the conversion as one element alongside new tile throughout, vanity replacement, toilet, lighting, and potentially layout changes. When the conversion is the only project, it stands alone at the lower cost point. If the rest of the bathroom is being remodeled simultaneously, the conversion folds into the larger scope.

## How This Affects Your Decision

**What keeps costs at the lower end:** Keeping the curbed threshold (no curbless engineering), choosing a prefabricated shower pan rather than a custom mortar bed, selecting stock porcelain tile, and opting for a framed or semi-frameless glass enclosure. A conversion in a standard 60-inch alcove with these specifications typically falls in the $3,000 to $4,500 range.

**What drives costs higher:** Curbless zero-threshold design (requires precise floor slope construction), large-format or natural stone tile, frameless glass, a linear drain in place of a standard center drain, and adding a built-in bench. Each upgrade is a reasonable choice, but they compound.

**Palm Bay-specific factors:** Brevard County's hard water at 182 mg/L means contractors recommend low-porosity tile and fixtures with mineral-resistant finishes. Porcelain tile is generally preferred over natural stone for this reason. Many Palm Bay homes from the 1980s and 1990s have polybutylene supply pipes or galvanized steel drains that may need replacement when the walls are open during a conversion — an additional cost the contractor identifies during the initial assessment.

**Permit requirements:** Every tub-to-shower conversion in Palm Bay requires a building permit because the plumbing is modified. The City of Palm Bay Building Department enforces the Florida Building Code 8th Edition. The contractor handles the permit application, rough plumbing inspection, and final sign-off. Permit fees represent a small fraction of the total project cost, typically $200 to $700.
