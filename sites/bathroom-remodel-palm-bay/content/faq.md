Common questions Palm Bay homeowners ask before starting a **bathroom remodel FAQ**, answered directly. This page covers timelines, permits, budget options, contractor considerations, and what to expect during the process.

*Answers reflect industry standards and Brevard County building requirements. For details specific to your bathroom, consult a local contractor.*

## Frequently Asked Questions About Bathroom Remodel

### How long does a bathroom remodel take?

A cosmetic update — new vanity, toilet, fixtures, and paint with no layout changes — takes roughly one week. A mid-range renovation with new tile, an updated shower, and upgraded fixtures runs two to three weeks. A complete reconstruction with plumbing and electrical modifications takes three to six weeks, including permit inspections. Lead times for custom glass enclosures or specialty tile can add to the schedule. The [timeline guide](/guides/how-long-does-a-bathroom-remodel-take/) provides a detailed breakdown by scope.

### Do I need a permit for a bathroom remodel?

In Palm Bay, any project that relocates plumbing, adds new drain lines, or changes electrical circuits requires a building permit from the City of Palm Bay Building Department, which enforces the Florida Building Code 8th Edition. Plumbing and electrical work must be performed by Florida-licensed contractors. Surface-level updates — replacing a vanity, swapping a toilet in the same location, painting — typically do not need a permit. Work valued under $7,500 on an owner-occupied single-family home may qualify for exemption under Florida HB 803.

### Can I remodel a bathroom on a budget?

Yes. A cosmetic refresh in the $3,000 to $6,000 range covers a new vanity, faucet, toilet, light fixtures, mirror, floor tile, and paint. The essential strategy is preserving the existing layout: when supply lines and drains stay in their current positions, labor costs drop substantially and permits are usually not required. Stock materials from home improvement stores keep fixture costs manageable. The [small bathroom remodel](/small-bathroom-remodel/) page covers this scope in detail.

### Should I replace or refinish my bathtub?

Refinishing applies a new surface coating to the existing tub. It costs $300 to $600, takes a day, and lasts 5 to 10 years under normal use. Replacement costs $1,500 to $5,000 and delivers a completely new fixture with a full lifespan. Refinishing makes sense when the tub is structurally intact and you need a cosmetic improvement on a tight budget. Replacement is the better choice when the tub has cracks, deep chips, or when you want a different type (such as upgrading to a walk-in model). In Brevard County's hard water (182 mg/L hardness), mineral deposits can shorten the lifespan of a refinished surface more quickly than in soft-water areas.

### What is the most expensive part of a bathroom remodel?

Labor, consistently. It represents 40 to 50 percent of a typical remodel budget, covering tile installation, plumbing, electrical, and carpentry. After labor, tile and countertop materials are the next largest category at 15 to 25 percent. The single most expensive individual decision is usually the shower configuration: a custom-tiled walk-in with frameless glass costs substantially more than a prefabricated insert. The [cost guide](/cost/) provides a full breakdown by component.

### What adds the most value to a bathroom remodel?

Updated tile (particularly in the shower), a modern vanity, and improved lighting deliver the most visible impact. Among functional upgrades, converting a tub to a walk-in shower is the most commonly requested value-adding project because it appeals to a wide range of buyers. Proper ventilation — a 50 CFM exhaust fan terminating outdoors — protects the investment by preventing moisture damage. In the Palm Bay market, where many homes are approaching 30 years old, addressing visible wear and replacing dated fixtures improves marketability.

### Do I need a contractor for a bathroom remodel?

For anything beyond surface-level cosmetic changes, yes. Florida requires licensed professionals for plumbing and electrical work, and permits must be pulled when those systems are modified. Even for cosmetic updates, tile installation, waterproofing, and fixture connections are trade skills that determine how long the room holds up. A poorly waterproofed shower develops leaks behind the walls — damage that may not become apparent for months or years, by which point repairs are expensive. A qualified contractor handles the work correctly, files permits when required, and stands behind the result.

### Can a bathroom be remodeled in stages?

Yes, and it is a practical strategy for homeowners managing a fixed budget. A common sequence: Stage 1, replace the vanity, toilet, and faucet. Stage 2, update the floor tile. Stage 3, remodel the shower. Each stage can be completed independently, months or years apart, as long as plumbing is not relocated between stages. The constraint is that any work requiring drain or supply line relocation should be consolidated into one project to avoid opening and closing walls multiple times. Plan the final vision even if you execute in phases.
