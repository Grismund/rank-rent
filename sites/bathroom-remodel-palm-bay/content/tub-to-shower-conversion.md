A **tub to shower conversion Palm Bay** homeowners request removes the bathtub entirely and replaces it with a walk-in shower. Palm Bay Bath Renovations connects you with independent local contractors who handle the plumbing modification, waterproofing, tiling, and glass installation required to transform an unused tub into a shower designed for daily use.

This is one of the most popular bathroom projects in Brevard County. Many Palm Bay homes — built in the 1980s and 1990s when alcove tub-shower combos were standard in every bathroom — have tubs that rarely get used for soaking. Homeowners step over the tub wall to take a shower, and the tub itself takes up space that a walk-in shower would use more effectively. A conversion addresses both function and accessibility, particularly for residents planning to age in place.

## What Is Tub-to-Shower Conversion?

A tub-to-shower conversion removes the bathtub, reroutes the plumbing (the tub drain and shower drain sit in different positions), waterproofs the floor and walls, tiles the enclosure, and installs a glass door or panel. The shower occupies the same footprint the tub held, so no additional space is required.

The plumbing modification is a fixed part of the project. Because the drain location changes, a permit from the City of Palm Bay Building Department is required for every conversion. The contractor handles the permit application, rough plumbing inspection, and final sign-off.

## The Tub-to-Shower Conversion Process

### Planning and Design

The contractor visits the bathroom and assesses the tub space, the existing plumbing behind the wall, and the subfloor condition. Key decisions include shower threshold (curbed or curbless), tile material, glass enclosure type, showerhead configuration, and whether to include a built-in niche or bench. Curbless designs require precise floor slope work and add to labor costs, but they provide barrier-free entry — a practical choice for accessibility.

### Demolition and Rough-In

The tub is disconnected and removed. The contractor inspects the wall framing and subfloor behind the tub for moisture damage. In Palm Bay, where bathrooms have been exposed to 75 percent average humidity and hard water for decades, finding deterioration behind a tub surround is a common discovery.

The plumber reroutes the drain from the tub position to the shower position, adjusts supply lines, and sets the new valve. If the home has original polybutylene supply pipes or galvanized steel drains — present in many Brevard County homes from the 1980s and early 1990s — the contractor may recommend replacing those lines during the project. A rough plumbing inspection must pass before the walls are sealed.

### Finishing and Inspection

The shower pan is waterproofed (mortar bed with membrane, or a prefabricated pan depending on the design). Walls receive the waterproofing membrane — required by Florida Building Code Section R702.4 — then backer board, then tile. The glass enclosure, showerhead, valve trim, and accessories are installed last. A shower pan flood test may be required as part of the inspection process.

Most conversions in Palm Bay take one to three weeks depending on tile complexity, glass lead time, and inspection scheduling.

## Choices for Your Tub-to-Shower Conversion

- **Threshold:** A standard curb is the most common and least expensive. A curbless zero-threshold design requires precise slope engineering but offers barrier-free access.
- **Tile:** Porcelain tile in large format minimizes grout lines — an advantage in Brevard County's hard water, which deposits minerals on every grout joint. Ceramic is the budget option. Natural stone requires sealing.
- **Glass:** Frameless glass panels provide an open look and are easier to maintain than framed enclosures. Semi-frameless is a mid-range choice. Protective coatings help resist mineral buildup.
- **Showerhead:** Rain heads, handheld options, and multi-head systems. Thermostatic valves hold temperature steady regardless of water use elsewhere in the house.
- **Bench and niche:** A built-in tile bench and recessed wall niche add function without taking floor space. Both are popular additions in Palm Bay conversions.

## Tub-to-Shower Conversion Cost in Palm Bay

Tub-to-shower conversions in the Palm Bay area typically range from **$3,000 to $8,000**. A basic conversion with stock tile, a prefabricated pan, and a framed glass door falls at the lower end. A custom-tiled conversion with frameless glass, a curbless threshold, and a built-in bench lands at the upper end.

The plumbing modification — rerouting the drain and adjusting supply lines — is a fixed cost component, typically $500 to $1,500 of the total. The rest of the variation comes from tile, glass, and fixture selections.
