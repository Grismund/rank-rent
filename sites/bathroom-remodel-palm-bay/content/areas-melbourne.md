## Melbourne Bathroom Remodel Permits

The City of Melbourne Building Section issues building, plumbing, electrical, and mechanical permits for all properties within city limits. The department uses the EnerGov online portal for permit submissions.

A building permit is required for any bathroom remodel that relocates plumbing, adds or moves electrical circuits, retiles a shower or tub surround, or removes a wall. Cosmetic work — painting, replacing cabinet hardware, swapping a faucet in the same location — does not trigger a permit. Tile replacement on existing surfaces and work valued under $7,500 on an owner-occupied single-family home may qualify for exemption.

Permit fees for a standard bathroom remodel run $200 to $700 depending on project scope. A certified Notice of Commencement must be filed for work valued at $2,500 or more. Plumbing permits must be filed by a Florida-licensed plumbing contractor.

Melbourne enforces the Florida Building Code, 8th Edition (2023), for all code categories. Bathroom exhaust must terminate outside the building envelope — not into an attic, crawl space, or soffit cavity. When shower or tub-surround tile is removed and replaced, Section R702.4 requires a code-listed waterproofing membrane behind the new tile, verified by inspection before tile installation.

Melbourne homeowners whose properties are in unincorporated Brevard County (outside city limits) use the Brevard County Building Department in Viera instead.

## What Melbourne Homeowners Are Choosing

Melbourne homeowners gravitate toward updates that address both aesthetics and the practical realities of this climate and water quality:

- **Walk-in showers** replacing tub-shower combos in master baths. The [tub-to-shower conversion](/tub-to-shower-conversion/) is a consistent top request.
- **Large-format porcelain tile** on floors and shower walls. Fewer grout lines means less surface for hard water mineral deposits (Brevard County water hardness is 182 mg/L and has been trending upward since 2022).
- **Ceramic-disc valve fixtures** with spot-resistant finishes. These resist the mineral buildup that destroys standard compression valves in Brevard County's hard water.
- **Upgraded ventilation** to address Brevard County's 75 percent average humidity. Florida Building Code requires 50 CFM minimum exhaust, terminating outdoors.
- **Mold-resistant materials** in wet areas — porcelain tile over natural stone, quartz countertops, and cement board or green board behind shower walls.
