**How long does a bathroom remodel take?** Between one and six weeks of active construction for most projects, depending on scope. A cosmetic update wraps up in about a week. A mid-range renovation takes two to three weeks. A complete reconstruction with plumbing and electrical work runs three to six weeks. That range exists because "bathroom remodel" covers everything from swapping a vanity to rebuilding the room from the studs out.

This guide breaks down the timeline by project scope, explains what extends or compresses it, and covers how conditions specific to the Palm Bay area — including permit scheduling, humidity, and common housing stock issues — affect the schedule.

*Last updated: November 2026. Based on typical Brevard County contractor timelines. Your specific project may differ.*

## Quick Summary

Three factors control the timeline: how much demolition the project requires, whether plumbing or electrical changes trigger permits and inspections, and how long the finishing work (tile, fixtures, glass) takes to complete.

Here is the overview:

| Scope | Typical timeline | Permit needed? |
|-------|-----------------|----------------|
| Cosmetic update | 1 week | Usually no |
| Mid-range renovation | 2-3 weeks | Often yes |
| Complete reconstruction | 3-6 weeks | Yes |
| Tub-to-shower conversion | 1-3 weeks | Yes |
| Bathtub replacement (same location) | 2-4 days | Usually no |
| Shower retile | 3-5 days | Usually no |

These timelines cover active construction only. They do not include the design and planning phase before work starts, which can take one to four weeks depending on decision-making speed and material ordering.

## Key Differences

**Cosmetic update (1 week).** New vanity, toilet, faucet, mirror, light fixtures, and paint. No demolition beyond removing the old fixtures. No plumbing relocation. No tile work on the shower walls (floor tile may be part of the scope). This is the fastest bathroom project because it requires no inspections, no waterproofing cure time, and no vertical tile setting. A capable crew completes it in three to five working days.

**Mid-range renovation (2 to 3 weeks).** New tile on the floor and in the shower, a new vanity with countertop, updated fixtures, and possibly a new shower valve. This scope involves demolition (removing old tile and backer board), waterproofing, tile setting, and grouting — each step requires drying or curing time before the next one can begin.

The approximate breakdown:
- Demolition and inspection for water damage: 1-2 days
- Plumbing and electrical rough-in: 1-2 days
- Rough inspection (if permit required): scheduling adds 1-2 days
- Waterproofing membrane application and cure: 1 day
- Shower pan flood test: 1 day
- Backer board and tile installation: 3-5 days
- Grout cure: 1 day
- Fixture installation, glass, and trim: 2-3 days
- Final inspection: 1 day

**Complete reconstruction (3 to 6 weeks).** Everything is removed to the framing. The room is rebuilt with any needed framing repairs, new plumbing rough-in, new electrical, waterproofing, tile, fixtures, and paint. The extended timeline results from more demolition and repair work at the front end, plus mandatory inspections at multiple stages.

A complete remodel in Palm Bay requires at minimum: a rough plumbing inspection (before walls close), a rough electrical inspection, a shower pan flood test, and a final inspection. Each inspection is scheduled through the City of Palm Bay Building Department, and the contractor cannot proceed to the next phase until each one passes.

**Tub-to-shower conversion (1 to 3 weeks).** The plumbing modification (rerouting the drain from tub position to shower position) requires a permit and inspections, adding scheduling time. A standard conversion with stock tile and a framed glass door finishes in about one week of active work plus inspection days. A custom-tiled conversion with frameless glass and a bench takes two to three weeks.

## How This Affects Your Decision

**What extends the timeline:**

- **Material lead times.** Custom glass enclosures can take two to four weeks from order to delivery. Specialty tile may need to be shipped. The contractor can begin demolition and rough-in while waiting, but finishing stalls if the glass or tile has not arrived.
- **Permit inspection scheduling.** In Palm Bay, inspections are typically available within 24 to 48 hours of request, but delays during peak construction periods can add days at each inspection point.
- **Concealed water damage.** Discovering rot behind shower walls or under the subfloor adds demolition and repair time. This is a frequent finding in Brevard County homes that have spent 25 to 30 years exposed to hard water and high humidity. The contractor cannot give a firm timeline until demo reveals the actual condition behind the walls.
- **Humidity and drying.** Brevard County's 75 percent average humidity — peaking at 80 percent in summer — slows cure times for waterproofing membranes, thin-set mortar, and grout. Contractors build this into their schedules, but exceptionally wet stretches can extend drying periods further.

**What compresses the timeline:**

- **Preserving the existing layout.** No plumbing relocation means no permit, no rough inspection wait, and significantly less labor. This is the most effective way to shorten the schedule.
- **Selecting stock materials.** Stock tile, stock vanities, and standard glass enclosures are available immediately. No waiting for custom fabrication or shipping.
- **Phasing the project.** A vanity and toilet swap now, a shower remodel later. Each individual phase is shorter and less disruptive than combining everything into one project.

**Scheduling considerations for Palm Bay:** Brevard County's summer months (June through September) bring the highest humidity and the most afternoon thunderstorms, which can extend drying times. Scheduling bathroom remodels during the drier fall-through-spring window, when humidity is lower and cure times are more predictable, can help keep the timeline on track. If you have only one bathroom, the contractor can typically keep the toilet operational throughout the project, but the shower will be unavailable during the tile and waterproofing phase — usually four to seven days for a mid-range renovation.
