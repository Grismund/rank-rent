Not every bathroom needs a full gut-and-rebuild. A guest bath with a dated vanity, worn faucet, and discolored light fixtures can be transformed with focused work that keeps the existing layout intact. Keeping plumbing in place is the single biggest factor in controlling costs — no relocation means less labor, no permit requirement in most cases, and a faster timeline.

## What Is Small Bathroom Remodel?

A small bathroom remodel refreshes the fixtures, finishes, and appearance of a bathroom without changing its layout or plumbing positions. The typical scope includes a new vanity, faucet, toilet, light fixtures, mirror, floor tile or flooring, and paint. The existing supply and drain lines stay where they are.

This is the right project for a secondary bathroom that is functional but visually outdated. In Palm Bay, where the median home is roughly 30 years old, guest baths and half baths installed in the early 1990s often have builder-grade components that look tired — brass fixtures, laminate vanity tops, and basic tile that has absorbed decades of Brevard County hard water deposits.

## The Small Bathroom Remodel Process

### Planning and Design

The contractor visits the bathroom and reviews what can be updated within your budget. Decisions include vanity style, faucet finish, toilet type, lighting, mirror, and whether to replace the floor tile or install new flooring over the existing surface. Because the layout does not change, design is straightforward and focused on material selections.

### Demolition and Rough-In

Demo is minimal. The old vanity, toilet, and fixtures are removed. Floor tile may be pulled up if replacement is included. No walls are opened, no plumbing is rerouted, and no waterproofing membrane work is needed unless a shower or tub surround is part of the scope. In a half bath or powder room, there is no shower or tub at all — the project focuses entirely on the vanity wall, toilet, floor, and finishes.

### Finishing and Inspection

New flooring goes down first, followed by the vanity installation, toilet, faucet, mirror, and lighting. Paint and trim complete the room. Because no plumbing or electrical was relocated, a permit inspection is usually not required. Work valued under $7,500 on an owner-occupied single-family home may be exempt under Florida HB 803.

A small bathroom remodel in Palm Bay typically takes three to five working days.

## Choices for Your Small Bathroom Remodel

- **Vanity:** Stock vanities from home improvement stores keep costs in the $150 to $500 range. A fresh vanity with a solid-surface or quartz top immediately changes how the room looks and feels.
- **Faucet:** Ceramic-disc valves are recommended for Brevard County's hard water — they resist mineral buildup better than compression or ball-type valves. Spot-resistant finishes (brushed nickel, matte black) reduce visible water spots.
- **Toilet:** A comfort-height model with a water-efficient flush. Standard toilets start around $150 to $300. This is one of the simplest upgrades with the most noticeable impact.
- **Floor tile:** Porcelain tile is durable and handles moisture. Luxury vinyl plank is a lower-cost alternative that installs quickly and resists water. Both are practical in Brevard County's humidity.
- **Lighting and mirror:** Updated fixtures and a larger or framed mirror make a small room feel noticeably different. LED fixtures reduce heat in a small space.

A small bathroom remodel in the Palm Bay area typically costs **$3,000 to $6,000**. That range covers a guest bath or half bath with a new vanity, faucet, toilet, light fixtures, mirror, floor tile or flooring, and paint — all with the existing layout preserved.

The budget stays low because there is no plumbing relocation, no waterproofing, and minimal demolition. Material choices are the main variable: stock fixtures and standard tile keep costs toward $3,000, while upgraded finishes and name-brand fixtures push toward $6,000.
